Important Formulas

1. Algebraic Fraction

P / Q, where Q ≠ 0
Examples of rational algebraic expressions: 1/x, x/10, x/(x−5), x/(y+6), 5x/(x+1)

2. Simplest Form

Simplest form = Divide numerator and denominator by their greatest common factor (GCF).
Common non-zero factors can be cancelled from the numerator and denominator.

3. Addition of Algebraic Fractions

a/b + c/d = (ad + bc) / bd,   b ≠ 0, d ≠ 0
For unlike denominators, first find the LCM and then add the fractions.

4. Subtraction of Algebraic Fractions

a/b − c/d = (ad − bc) / bd,   b ≠ 0, d ≠ 0
Subtraction: Change the signs of the second expression and then follow the addition procedure.

5. Multiplication of Algebraic Fractions

(a/b)(c/d) = ac/bd,   b ≠ 0, d ≠ 0
Factorize first and cancel common non-zero factors before multiplying.

6. Division of Algebraic Fractions

a/b ÷ c/d = a/b × d/c = ad/bc
Division of a fraction = Multiplication by the reciprocal of the divisor.

7. BODMAS Rule

BODMAS = Brackets → Orders → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction
In complex algebraic expressions, operations are performed according to the BODMAS rule.

8. Important Factorization Rules

a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)
a³ − b³ = (a − b)(a² + ab + b²)
a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² − ab + b²)
a⁴ − b⁴ = (a² − b²)(a² + b²)

9. Rational Equation

Multiply the whole equation by the LCD (Least Common Denominator) to remove fractions.
LCD = Least Common Denominator of all fractions in the equation.

10. Extraneous Solution

If a solution makes any original denominator equal to zero, it must be excluded.
Original denominator ≠ 0

11. Speed, Distance and Time

S = V × T
T = S / V
V = S / T
Equal time condition: S₁/V₁ = S₂/V₂

Definitions

Algebraic Expression: An expression in which variables, constants, and arithmetic operations are combined using addition, subtraction, multiplication, or division.

Polynomial: An algebraic expression in which the exponents of the variables are non-negative integers.

Monomial: A polynomial containing one term.

Binomial: A polynomial containing two terms.

Trinomial: A polynomial containing three terms.

Zero Polynomial: A polynomial whose all coefficients are zero. It has no defined degree.

Constant Polynomial: A non-zero polynomial of degree zero.

Linear Polynomial: A polynomial of degree one.

Quadratic Polynomial: A polynomial of degree two.

Cubic Polynomial: A polynomial of degree three.

Leading Coefficient: The coefficient of the first term when a polynomial is written in descending order of degree.

Degree of a Polynomial: The highest exponent of the variable in a polynomial. For a polynomial in several variables, its degree is the highest total degree of its terms.

Rational Expression: An expression that can be written in the form P(x)/Q(x), where P(x) and Q(x) are polynomials and Q(x) is not the zero polynomial.

Algebraic Fraction: If P and Q are algebraic expressions, then P/Q is called an algebraic fraction, where Q ≠ 0.

Irrational Expression: An algebraic expression that cannot be expressed as the ratio of two polynomials with a non-zero polynomial denominator.

Substitution: The process of replacing variables in an algebraic expression by given values.

Evaluation: The process of obtaining a numerical value of an algebraic expression after substitution.

Rational Equation: An equation containing one or more rational expressions.

LCD: Least Common Denominator, the smallest common denominator of the fractions in an equation or expression.

Extraneous Solution: A value obtained during the solving process that does not satisfy the original equation, usually because it makes an original denominator zero.

Reciprocal: The reciprocal of a non-zero number or expression is obtained by interchanging its numerator and denominator.

Key Points

  • An algebraic fraction has an algebraic expression in the numerator and denominator.
  • The denominator of an algebraic fraction can never be zero.
  • To simplify an algebraic fraction, factorize the numerator and denominator completely first.
  • Cancel only common non-zero factors, not separate terms.
  • For addition and subtraction, use a common denominator or LCM.
  • In subtraction, the signs of the second expression change when removing brackets.
  • For multiplication, multiply numerators together and denominators together.
  • Factorization before multiplication can make cancellation easier.
  • For division, multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.
  • BODMAS must be followed when simplifying complex algebraic expressions.
  • Important factorization identities should be used to simplify algebraic fractions.
  • To solve a rational equation, multiply every term by the LCD.
  • After solving a rational equation, check every solution in the original equation.
  • If a value makes an original denominator zero, it is an extraneous solution and must be rejected.
  • In word problems involving speed, distance and time, use S = V × T.
  • For equal travel time, set the corresponding time expressions equal to each other.
  • Restrictions from the original denominators must be kept even after simplification.

Most Important Things

1. Addition: Find a common denominator, combine the numerators, and simplify.
2. Subtraction: Find a common denominator, change the signs of the second expression, combine, and simplify.
3. Multiplication: Factorize, cancel common factors, and multiply.
4. Division: Change division into multiplication by the reciprocal.
5. Rational Equations: Find the LCD and multiply the complete equation by the LCD.
6. Final Check: Always check the obtained values in the original equation and reject values that make a denominator zero.
7. Word Problems: Define the unknown variable first, form the rational equation, solve it, and verify the answer.
