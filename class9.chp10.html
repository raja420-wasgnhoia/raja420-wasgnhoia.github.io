2. CONCEPTS

Construction of a Triangle

Two Sides and Included Angle
1. Draw one given side.
2. Construct the given angle at the appropriate endpoint.
3. Mark the second given side on the angle's ray using a compass.
4. Join the remaining vertices to complete the triangle.
One Side and Two Angles
1. Draw the given side.
2. Construct the given angles at the endpoints.
3. Extend their rays until they intersect.
4. The intersection gives the third vertex of the triangle.
Two Sides and an Angle Opposite One Side
1. Draw the given base side.
2. Construct the given angle at the appropriate endpoint.
3. Draw an arc with a radius equal to the other given side.
4. The arc may intersect the ray at zero, one, or two points, giving zero, one, or two possible triangles.

Important Constructions

Altitudes
Draw a perpendicular from each vertex to the opposite side or its extension. The three altitudes meet at the orthocentre.
Perpendicular Bisectors
Draw equal-radius arcs from both endpoints of each side. Join the arc intersections to construct its perpendicular bisector. The three bisectors meet at the circumcentre.
Medians
Find the midpoint of each side using equal-radius arcs. Join each vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. The three medians meet at the centroid.
Angle Bisectors
Construct a ray that divides an angle into two equal angles. The three angle bisectors of a triangle meet at the incentre.

3. DEFINITIONS

Practical Geometry:
The branch of geometry concerned with constructing geometrical figures using instruments such as a ruler and compass.
Altitude:
A perpendicular segment drawn from a vertex to the opposite side or its extension.
Perpendicular Bisector:
A line that divides a segment into two equal parts at a right angle.
Median:
A line segment joining a vertex of a triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side.
Angle Bisector:
A ray or line that divides an angle into two equal angles.
Concurrency:
The property of three or more lines or segments passing through one common point.

4. KEY POINTS

• The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is 180°.
• Three altitudes meet at the orthocentre.
• Three perpendicular bisectors meet at the circumcentre.
• Three medians meet at the centroid.
• Three angle bisectors meet at the incentre.
• In an acute triangle, the orthocentre lies inside the triangle.
• In a right triangle, the orthocentre is at the right-angle vertex.
• In an obtuse triangle, the orthocentre lies outside the triangle.
• The circumcentre lies inside an acute triangle, at the midpoint of the hypotenuse in a right triangle, and outside an obtuse triangle.
