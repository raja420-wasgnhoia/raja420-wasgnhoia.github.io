FORMULAS & RULES

A. Circumference of a Circle

1. Circumference ÷ Diameter = π
2. C = πd
3. Since d = 2r, circumference C = 2πr
4. π ≈ 22/7 ≈ 3.14
5. Diameter d = 2r
6. Radius r = d/2

B. Circle Examples

C = πd = (22/7) × 4.2 = 13.2 cm

2πr = 330
r = 330 × 7/44 = 52.5 m

F. Surface Area of Prisms

30. Rectangular Prism:
Surface Area = 2(lw + wh + lh)
31. Cube:
Surface Area = 6a²
32. General Prism:
Surface Area = 2 × Area of base + Perimeter of base × Length of prism
33. Right Triangular Prism:
Surface Area = 2(½bh) + (a + b + c)L
Here, a, b and c are the triangle's side lengths and L is the prism's length.
34. Equilateral Triangular Prism:
Surface Area = ah + 3aL
Here, a is the triangle's side, h is its perpendicular height and L is the prism's length.
35. Trapezoidal Prism:
Surface Area = 2 × Area of trapezoidal base + Perimeter of base × Length
36. Regular Pentagonal Prism:
Surface Area = 2 × Area of base + 5aL
Here, a is the side length of the pentagonal base.
37. Triangular Prism Example:
Base area = ½ × 16 × 10 = 80 cm²
Length = 14 cm; triangle sides = 10 cm, 16 cm and 20 cm
Surface Area = 2(80) + (10 + 16 + 20) × 14
= 160 + 644 = 804 cm²
38. Regular Pentagonal Prism Example:
Base area = 20 cm², side = 3 cm, length = 8 cm
Surface Area = 2(20) + 5 × 3 × 8
= 40 + 120 = 160 cm²
39. Trapezoidal Prism Example:
Using the supplied values: base area = 38.5 cm², base perimeter = 22 cm and length = 10 cm.
Surface Area = 2(38.5) + 22 × 10 = 297 cm²

G. Volume of Prisms

40. General Prism:
Volume = Area of base × Length/height between parallel ends
41. Rectangular Prism:

56. Curved Surface Area (CSA):

TSA = πr² + 2πrh

CONCEPTS

1. Buildings and Mensuration

Buildings may contain rectangular prisms and triangular prisms. Mensuration helps calculate their surface areas and volumes.

2. Circumference

The circumference is the distance around a circle. It is the circle's boundary length.

3. The Value of π

The ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter is constant and is called π. It has no unit because it is a ratio of two lengths.

4. Area of a Circle

A circle can be divided into sectors and rearranged into an approximate rectangle. Its length is πr and width is r, so its area is πr².

5. Area Units

Area measures a two-dimensional region. Its units are square units such as mm², cm² and m². Conversion factors must be squared when converting area units.

6. Prisms

A prism is a three-dimensional solid with two identical, parallel polygonal ends and flat side faces. Its cross-section stays the same along its length.
A rectangular prism has six rectangular faces. A cube is a special square prism. Prisms are named after the shape of their bases.
A triangular prism has two triangular ends and three parallelogram side faces.
A cylinder is not a prism because it has circular bases and a curved side surface, rather than polygonal bases and flat parallelogram side faces.

7. Surface Area of Prisms

The surface area of a solid is the total area of all its outer faces. For a prism, add the areas of its two bases and all its side faces.

8. Volume of Prisms

Volume measures the space occupied by a solid. The volume of any prism is the area of its base multiplied by the distance between its parallel bases.
For a triangular prism, use the triangular base area, ½ × base × perpendicular height, then multiply it by the prism's length.

9. Circular Cylinder

A circular cylinder is a solid with two parallel circular bases joined by a curved surface. Examples include oil drums, tins and pipes.
When the curved surface is cut and opened flat, it forms a rectangle. Its length equals the circle's circumference, 2πr, and its width equals cylinder height h.
A closed cylinder has two circular faces and one curved surface. Its total surface area includes all three surfaces.
The volume of a cylinder equals its circular base area multiplied by its height.

10. Composite Shapes

To find the area of a composite shape, divide it into familiar shapes, calculate each area, then add or subtract the relevant areas.

DEFINITIONS

Mensuration: The branch of mathematics concerned with measuring lengths, perimeters, areas, surface areas and volumes.
Circumference: The length of the boundary of a circle.
Diameter: A line segment passing through the centre of a circle and joining two points on its circumference.
Radius: The distance from the centre of a circle to its circumference.
Pi (π): The constant ratio of the circumference of a circle to its diameter, approximately 3.14.
Area: The amount of two-dimensional surface covered by a shape.
Composite Shape: A shape made by combining two or more simple geometric shapes.
Prism: A three-dimensional solid with two identical, parallel polygonal bases joined by flat side faces.
Cross-section: The two-dimensional shape formed when a solid is cut across.
Surface Area: The total area of all the outer surfaces of a three-dimensional object.
Volume: The amount of three-dimensional space occupied by an object.
Circular Cylinder: A solid with two parallel circular bases connected by a curved surface.
Curved Surface Area: The area of the curved side surface of a cylinder, excluding its circular bases.
Total Surface Area: The sum of the areas of all surfaces of a solid.

KEY POINTS

1. Use the same units before calculating area, surface area or volume.
2. Circumference is measured in units such as cm or m; area is measured in square units; volume is measured in cubic units.
3. The symbol ≈ means “approximately equal to.” The value of π is not exact or terminating.
4. For area conversions, square the length conversion factor. For example, 1 m² = 10,000 cm².
5. For volume conversions, cube the length conversion factor. For example, 1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³.
6. 1000 cm³ = 1 litre.
7. In prism formulas, use the area of the correct base shape. A triangular prism does not use l × w × h unless those dimensions actually describe a rectangular prism.
8. The length of a prism is the distance between its parallel bases.
9. For a closed cylinder, total surface area includes both circular bases and the curved surface.
10. For a cylinder open at one end, include one circular base and the curved surface only.
11. Do not confuse the cylinder's curved surface area, 2πrh, with its total surface area, 2πr(r + h).
12. In volume problems, rearrange V = area of base × height to find an unknown height or base area.
13. A cylinder is not a prism because its bases are circular and its side surface is curved.
14. For a wheel completing multiple rotations, distance travelled = circumference × number of rotations.
15. For painting or polishing, multiply the area being covered by the cost per unit area.
