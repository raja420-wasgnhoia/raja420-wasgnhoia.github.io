FORMULAS & RULES

1. Complementary Angles
Sum of complementary angles = 90°
If one angle is x, its complement = 90° − x
Example: 70° + x = 90°
x = 90° − 70° = 20°
2. Supplementary Angles
Sum of supplementary angles = 180°
Supplement of x = 180° − x
Example: 120° + x = 180°
x = 180° − 120° = 60°

If two supplementary angles are in the ratio 1:2:
x + 2x = 180°
3x = 180°
x = 60°; second angle = 120°.
3. Adjacent Angles
Adjacent angles have a common vertex, a common arm and non-overlapping interiors.
4. Angles on a Straight Line
Sum of adjacent angles on a straight line = 180°
If x + 110° = 180°:
x = 180° − 110° = 70°
5. Angles Around a Point
Sum of angles around a point = 360°
∠a + ∠b + ∠c + ∠d = 360°
6. Vertically Opposite Angles
Vertically opposite angles are equal.
If z and 110° are vertically opposite:
z = 110°
If x + 110° = 180°:
x = 70°; its vertically opposite angle y = 70°.
7. Corresponding Angles
If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, corresponding angles are equal.
∠1 = ∠2
8. Alternate Angles
If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, alternate interior angles are equal and alternate exterior angles are equal.
If one angle is 55°, its alternate angle = 55°.
An adjacent supplementary angle = 180° − 55° = 125°.
9. Parallel Lines
Symbol: ∥
AB ∥ CD means AB is parallel to CD.
10. Perpendicular Lines
Symbol: ⊥
PQ ⊥ RS means the lines meet at 90°.
11. Lines of Symmetry of Quadrilaterals
Square = 4
Rectangle = 2
Parallelogram = 0
Rhombus = 2
Kite = 1
General trapezium = 0
Isosceles trapezium = 1
Irregular quadrilateral = usually 0
12. Reflection Rules
• The reflected image has the same shape and size as the original.
• Each point and its image are equally distant from the mirror line.
• The segment joining a point to its image is perpendicular to the mirror line.
• The mirror line bisects the segment joining corresponding points.
13. Constructing a Line of Reflection
1. Join each original point to its corresponding reflected point.
2. Find the midpoint of each joining segment.
3. Draw a line through the midpoints.
This line is the required line of reflection.
14. Rotation
Quarter turn = 90°
Half turn = 180°
Three-quarter turn = 270°
Full turn = 360°
15. Order of Rotational Symmetry
Order = number of times a figure fits exactly onto its original position during one complete turn.

Order = 360° ÷ smallest angle of rotation

Square: 360° ÷ 90° = 4
Circle: Infinite order
16. Centre of Rotation of a Square
The centre is the intersection of the diagonals.
Smallest angle of rotation = 90°
Order of rotational symmetry = 4

CONCEPTS

1. Parallel Lines
Parallel lines lie in the same plane and never meet, however far they are extended. The perpendicular distance between them remains constant. Examples include railway tracks and lines on a notebook page.
2. Perpendicular Lines
Two lines are perpendicular when they intersect at a right angle of 90°. Roads crossing at a right angle and the adjacent sides of a square are examples.
3. Transversal
A transversal is a line that intersects two or more coplanar lines. It helps identify corresponding and alternate angles.
4. Adjacent Angles
Two angles are adjacent when they have a common vertex, a common arm and do not overlap. Having a common vertex alone is not enough.
5. Complementary and Supplementary Angles
Complementary angles add up to 90°. Supplementary angles add up to 180°. Either pair may be adjacent or non-adjacent.
6. Vertically Opposite Angles
When two lines intersect, opposite angles are equal. Any adjacent pair formed by the intersecting lines adds up to 180°.
7. Corresponding Angles
Corresponding angles occupy the same relative position when a transversal intersects two lines. They are equal when the two lines are parallel.
8. Alternate Angles
Alternate angles lie on opposite sides of a transversal and are non-adjacent. Alternate interior angles lie between the two lines; alternate exterior angles lie outside them. They are equal when the lines are parallel.
9. Reflection
Reflection flips a shape across a mirror line. The image retains the original shape and size but appears on the opposite side of the mirror line.
10. Line of Symmetry
A line of symmetry divides a figure into two matching halves. When folded along that line, one half fits exactly over the other.
11. Rotational Symmetry
A figure has rotational symmetry if it fits exactly onto its original position at least once during a turn smaller than 360°.

DEFINITIONS

1. Parallel Lines
Two coplanar lines that never meet, however far they are extended, are called parallel lines.
2. Perpendicular Lines
Two lines that intersect at a right angle of 90° are called perpendicular lines.
3. Transversal
A line that intersects two or more coplanar lines is called a transversal.
4. Adjacent Angles
Two angles having a common vertex, a common arm and non-overlapping interiors are called adjacent angles.
5. Complementary Angles
Two angles whose measures add up to 90° are called complementary angles.
6. Supplementary Angles
Two angles whose measures add up to 180° are called supplementary angles.
7. Vertically Opposite Angles
When two lines intersect, the non-adjacent angles opposite each other at the vertex are called vertically opposite angles.
8. Corresponding Angles
When a transversal intersects two coplanar lines, angles in the same relative position are called corresponding angles.
9. Alternate Angles
Non-adjacent angles lying on opposite sides of a transversal are called alternate angles. They may be interior or exterior.
10. Reflection
A transformation that flips a figure across a line to produce its mirror image is called reflection.
11. Line of Reflection
The mirror line across which a figure is reflected is called the line of reflection.
12. Line of Symmetry
A line that divides a figure into two halves that match exactly when folded is called a line of symmetry.
13. Rotation
The movement of a figure through a given angle about a fixed point is called rotation.
14. Centre of Rotation
The fixed point about which a figure turns is called the centre of rotation.
15. Rotational Symmetry
A figure has rotational symmetry if it fits exactly onto its original position during a turn of less than 360°.
16. Order of Rotational Symmetry
The number of times a figure fits exactly onto its original position during one complete turn is called the order of rotational symmetry.
17. Coplanar Lines
Lines that lie in the same plane are called coplanar lines.

KEY POINTS

1. Parallel lines never intersect; perpendicular lines meet at 90°.
2. Complementary angles sum to 90°, while supplementary angles sum to 180°.
3. Adjacent angles must have a common vertex and common arm, and their interiors must not overlap.
4. Vertically opposite angles are always equal when two lines intersect.
5. Angles around a point add up to 360°.
6. Corresponding and alternate angles are equal when the two lines cut by a transversal are parallel.
7. If adjacent angles form a straight line, their sum is 180°.
8. A square has 4 lines of symmetry; a rectangle has 2; a rhombus has 2.
9. A general parallelogram has no line of reflection symmetry, but it has rotational symmetry of order 2.
10. An isosceles trapezium has 1 line of symmetry. A general trapezium usually has none.
11. In a reflection, corresponding points lie at equal perpendicular distances from the mirror line.
12. A square's rotational centre is the intersection of its diagonals.
13. A square has rotational symmetry of order 4 and its smallest positive angle of rotation is 90°.
14. A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry and infinite order of rotational symmetry.
15. Always check whether the given lines are parallel before using the corresponding-angle or alternate-angle equality rules.
