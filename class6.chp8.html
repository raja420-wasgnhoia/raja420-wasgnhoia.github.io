FORMULAS & RULES

1. Bisecting a Line Segment
If AB = 6 cm, then AC = CB = 3 cm.
2. Perpendicular Lines
Perpendicular lines intersect at 90°.
3. Important Constructible Angles
30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, 90°, 105° and 120°.
4. Angle of 30°
30° = 60° ÷ 2
5. Angle of 45°
45° = 90° ÷ 2
6. Angle of 60°
An angle constructed using equal-radius compass arcs is 60°.
7. Angle of 75°
75° = (60° + 90°) ÷ 2
8. Angle of 105°
105° = (90° + 120°) ÷ 2
9. Angle of 120°
120° = 180° − 60°
10. Angles on a Straight Line
a + b = 180°
A straight angle = 180°.
11. Angles Around a Point
Sum of angles around a point = 360°.
12. Vertically Opposite Angles
Vertically opposite angles are equal.
13. Triangle Angle Sum
A + B + C = 180°
14. Isosceles Triangle
Two equal sides have equal opposite angles.
15. Equilateral Triangle
Each angle = 180° ÷ 3 = 60°.
16. Supplementary Angles
a + b = 180°
17. Angles in Ratio 1:3 on a Straight Line
Let angles be x and 3x.
x + 3x = 180°
4x = 180°
x = 45°
Angles = 45° and 135°.
18. Angles Around a Point: Figure (i)
z = 120° (vertically opposite angles)
x + z + y + 120° = 360°
x + 120° + y + 120° = 360°
x + y = 120°
Since x = y, 2x = 120°
x = y = 60°.
19. Angles Around a Point: Figure (ii)
a + 3a + 80° = 180°
4a = 100°
a = 25°
b = 80° (vertically opposite angles)
4a + 80° + b + c = 360°
100° + 80° + 80° + c = 360°
c = 100°.
20. Triangle Example (i)
x + 70° + 50° = 180°
x + 120° = 180°
x = 60°.
21. Triangle Example (ii)
x + x + 80° = 180°
2x = 100°
x = 50°.
22. Triangle with Exterior Angle
y + 122° = 180°
y = 58°
x + x + y = 180°
2x + 58° = 180°
x = 61°.
23. Triangle with Vertically Opposite Angles
a = 100°
c + 140° = 180°
c = 40°
a + b + c = 180°
100° + b + 40° = 180°
b = 40°.

CONCEPTS

1. Construction of a Perpendicular Bisector

Given: AB = 6 cm
  1. Draw AB = 6 cm.
  2. Open the compass to more than half of AB.
  3. With A as centre, draw arcs above and below AB.
  4. With B as centre and the same radius, draw arcs intersecting at X and Y.
  5. Join X and Y. Let XY meet AB at C.
  6. XY bisects AB perpendicularly, so AC = CB = 3 cm and XY ⟂ AB.

2. Perpendicular from a Point on a Line

  1. Draw line AB and mark point P on it.
  2. With P as centre, draw an arc cutting AB at X and Y.
  3. With X and Y as centres and equal radius greater than half XY, draw arcs intersecting at Q.
  4. Join P to Q.
  5. PQ is perpendicular to AB at P, making an angle of 90°.

3. Perpendicular from a Point Outside a Line

  1. Draw line PQ and mark point A outside it.
  2. With A as centre, draw an arc intersecting PQ at B and C.
  3. With B and C as centres, draw arcs of equal radius greater than half BC.
  4. Let the arcs intersect at D.
  5. Join A to D. AD is perpendicular to PQ.

4. Construction of a 60° Angle

  1. Draw ray AB.
  2. With A as centre, draw an arc cutting AB at P.
  3. With P as centre and the same radius, draw an arc intersecting the first arc at Q.
  4. Join AQ. Angle BAQ = 60°.

5. Construction of a 30° Angle

  1. Construct a 60° angle BAC.
  2. Bisect angle BAC using a compass.
  3. The two equal angles are each 30°.

6. Construction of a 90° Angle

  1. Draw ray AB.
  2. Construct a perpendicular to AB at A using a compass.
  3. The angle formed is 90°.

7. Construction of a 45° Angle

  1. Construct a 90° angle at A.
  2. Bisect the 90° angle using a compass.
  3. The resulting angle is 45°.

8. Construction of 75°, 105° and 120°

  • 75°: Construct 60° and 90° from the same ray, then bisect the angle between them.
  • 105°: Construct 90° and 120° from the same ray, then bisect the angle between them.
  • 120°: Construct 60° adjacent to a straight angle of 180°. The remaining angle is 120°.

9. Bisecting an Angle

  1. Draw an arc from the vertex that cuts both arms of the angle.
  2. From the two intersection points, draw equal-radius arcs that intersect.
  3. Join the vertex to the intersection of the arcs.
  4. The ray divides the angle into two equal angles.

DEFINITIONS

1. Line: A line extends endlessly in both directions and has no endpoints. It cannot be measured as a finite length.
2. Line Segment: A part of a line with two endpoints and a measurable length.
3. Ray: A part of a line that has one endpoint and extends endlessly in one direction.
4. Bisect: To divide something into two equal parts.
5. Perpendicular Lines: Two lines that intersect at an angle of 90°.
6. Perpendicular Bisector: A line that divides a line segment into two equal parts at 90°.
7. Angle: A figure formed by two rays meeting at a common endpoint called the vertex.
8. Angle Bisector: A ray that divides an angle into two equal angles.
9. Straight Angle: An angle measuring 180°.
10. Full Angle: An angle measuring 360°, representing one complete turn.
11. Vertically Opposite Angles: Opposite angles formed when two straight lines intersect. They are equal.
12. Triangle: A closed plane figure formed by three line segments.
13. Isosceles Triangle: A triangle with two equal sides and two equal opposite angles.
14. Equilateral Triangle: A triangle with three equal sides and three equal angles of 60° each.
15. Supplementary Angles: Two angles whose measures add up to 180°.

KEY POINTS

  • A line has no endpoints; a line segment has two endpoints.
  • A line segment has a measurable length.
  • Bisecting divides a segment or angle into two equal parts.
  • A perpendicular bisector meets a segment at 90° and cuts it into equal halves.
  • Use equal compass radii for paired arcs in constructions.
  • Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
  • Angles around a point add up to 360°.
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • The three interior angles of a triangle add up to 180°.
  • In an isosceles triangle, the base angles are equal.
  • Each angle of an equilateral triangle is 60°.
  • Supplementary angles add up to 180°.
  • Show construction arcs clearly and label points correctly.
