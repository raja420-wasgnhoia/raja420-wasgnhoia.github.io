FORMULAS & RULES

1. Ratio

Ratio of a to b = a : b = a / b, where b ≠ 0.
In a : b, a is the antecedent and b is the consequent.
Equivalent ratio: Multiply or divide both terms by the same non-zero number.
Simplest form of a ratio: HCF of its terms = 1.
To simplify a ratio, divide both terms by their HCF.
To compare ratios as fractions, express them with a common denominator.
Ratios compare like quantities, and units are omitted after quantities are expressed in the same units.

2. Continued Ratio

Continued ratio: x : y : z
For ratios x : y and y : z, make the common term y equal in both ratios.
Multiply or divide each ratio to obtain equivalent ratios with the same common term.
Combine the adjusted ratios as x : y : z.

3. Rate and Unit Rate

Rate = First quantity / Second quantity
Unit rate = Total quantity / Number of units
A unit rate expresses a rate for one unit.
Rates include units because they compare quantities measured in different units.

4. Percentage

Percentage means “out of 100”.
x% = x / 100
Percentage = (First quantity / Second quantity) × 100%
Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100%
Pass percentage = (Obtained marks / Total marks) × 100%
x% of y = (x / 100) × y
Remaining percentage = 100% − Percentage spent
Increase = New value − Original value
Decrease = Original value − New value
Increase % = (Increase / Original value) × 100%
Decrease % = (Decrease / Original value) × 100%
Original amount = Remaining amount / (Remaining percentage / 100)
Total percentage of a whole = 100%

5. Golden Ratio

Golden ratio: φ = (1 + √5) / 2 ≈ 1.618

CONCEPTS

1. Ratio

A ratio compares two like quantities. The symbol “:” is read as “to”.

Both quantities must be expressed in the same units before comparing them. Units are not written in the simplified ratio.

The two numbers in a ratio are called its terms. The first term is the antecedent, and the second is the consequent.

2. Equivalent Ratios

Equivalent ratios have the same value. They are obtained by multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number.

A ratio is in simplest form when the HCF of its terms is 1.

When a ratio is written as a fraction, simplify the fraction but do not convert an improper fraction into a mixed number.

3. Continued Ratio

A ratio comparing more than two quantities is called a continued ratio.

To combine two ratios, identify their common term and make its value equal in both ratios before combining them.

4. Rate

A rate compares two quantities measured in different units. Units are included when writing a rate.

A unit rate gives the amount for one unit, such as cost per item, distance per litre or words per minute.

5. Percentage

Percentage is a fraction with 100 as its denominator. The symbol % means “per cent”, or “out of a hundred”.

Percentages help compare quantities quickly and are commonly used for marks, increases, decreases and amounts remaining.

6. Golden Ratio

The golden ratio is a mathematical ratio often discussed in relation to patterns in nature, design and proportions.

DEFINITIONS

Ratio: A comparison of two like quantities by division.

Terms of a Ratio: The numbers being compared in a ratio.

Antecedent: The first term of a ratio.

Consequent: The second term of a ratio.

Equivalent Ratios: Ratios that have the same value.

Simplest Form of a Ratio: A ratio whose terms have an HCF of 1.

Continued Ratio: A ratio used to compare three or more quantities.

Rate: A comparison of two quantities measured in different units.

Unit Rate: A rate expressed for one unit of a quantity.

Percentage: A fraction or ratio expressed out of 100.

Golden Ratio: A special mathematical ratio approximately equal to 1.618.

KEY POINTS

  • Ratios compare like quantities.
  • Convert quantities into the same units before forming a ratio.
  • Ratio terms have no units once the quantities are expressed in the same units.
  • Multiply or divide both terms by the same non-zero number to obtain an equivalent ratio.
  • In simplest form, the HCF of the ratio terms is 1.
  • To form a continued ratio, make the common term equal in both ratios first.
  • Rates compare quantities with different units, so their units matter.
  • A unit rate is the rate for one unit.
  • Percentage means out of 100.
  • The total percentage of a whole is 100%.
  • Use the original value as the denominator when calculating percentage increase or decrease.
  • To find an original amount from the remaining amount and remaining percentage, divide the remaining amount by the remaining percentage expressed as a decimal.
  • The golden ratio is approximately 1.618.
