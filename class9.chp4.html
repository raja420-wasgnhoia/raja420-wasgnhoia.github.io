1. FORMULAS

Basic Factorization Identities

ka + kb + kc = k(a + b + c)
ac + ad + bc + bd = (a + b)(c + d)
a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)²
a² − 2ab + b² = (a − b)²
a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b)
a² + 2ab + b² − c² = (a + b + c)(a + b − c)
a² − 2ab + b² − c² = (a − b + c)(a − b − c)
a⁴ + a²b² + b⁴ = (a² + ab + b²)(a² − ab + b²)
a⁴ + 4b⁴ = (a² + 2b² + 2ab)(a² + 2b² − 2ab)
a⁴ + 64b⁴ = (a² + 8b² + 4ab)(a² + 8b² − 4ab)

Quadratic Factorization

x² + px + q = (x + m)(x + n), where m + n = p and mn = q
y² − 7y + 12 = (y − 3)(y − 4)
m² − 2m − 15 = (m − 5)(m + 3)
3x² + 22x − 16 = (3x − 2)(x + 8)
(x² + 3x − 4)(x² + 3x + 5) + 8 = (x² + 3x + 4)(x² + 3x − 3)

Perfect Cube Identities

a³ + 3a²b + 3ab² + b³ = (a + b)³
a³ − 3a²b + 3ab² − b³ = (a − b)³

Sum and Difference of Cubes

a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² − ab + b²)
a³ − b³ = (a − b)(a² + ab + b²)
x³ + 27y³ = (x + 3y)(x² − 3xy + 9y²)
x⁶ − y⁶ = (x + y)(x − y)(x² − xy + y²)(x² + xy + y²)
8a³ − 125b³ − 2a + 5b = (2a − 5b)(4a² + 10ab + 25b² − 1)

HCF (Highest Common Factor)

HCF(18ab³c, 30a²b²c³, 24b²c⁵) = 6b²c
HCF(2m² − 2mn, 4m⁴ − 4m²n², 2m³ − 4m²n + 2mn²) = 2m(m − n)
HCF(ax² + 7ax + 12a, ax² − 5ax − 24a, 2ax² + 5ax − 3a) = a(x + 3)

LCM (Least Common Multiple)

LCM(45x²y, 60x³y²z) = 180x³y²z
LCM(18a⁵b³c⁵, 60a⁶b⁶, 42a⁴b³) = 1260a⁶b⁶c⁵
LCM(x⁴y³ − x³y⁴, x³y − xy³) = x³y³(x − y)(x + y)
LCM(2x² − 12xy + 16y², x² − 6xy + 8y², 3x² − 12y²) = 6(x − 4y)(x − 2y)(x + 2y)
HCF × LCM = P × Q (for two expressions P and Q)
LCM = (P × Q) ÷ HCF (for two expressions P and Q)

Algebraic Fractions

(S/U) × (T/Q) = ST/UQ
(S/U) ÷ (T/Q) = SQ/UT
(r² − s²)/r ÷ (r − s)/s = s(r + s)/r

Square Roots and Perfect Squares

If P = Q², then Q and −Q are square roots of P; the principal square root √P is non-negative for P ≥ 0.
4a² + 12ab + 9b² = (2a + 3b)²
(2a + 1)(2a + 3)(2a + 5)(2a + 7) + 16 = (4a² + 16a + 11)²
x⁴ + 4x³ + 10x² + 10x + 5 + 2x + 4 = (x² + 2x + 3)²

Applications

x³ − y³ = (x − y)(x² + xy + y²)
x³ − 6x²y + 12xy² − 8y³ = (x − 2y)³
Surface area of a cube = 6(side)²

2. CONCEPTS

Factorization: Expressing an algebraic expression as a product of its factors.

Common factor: A factor shared by every term of an expression.

Quadratic factorization: For x² + px + q, find two numbers whose sum is p and product is q.

HCF of monomials: Take the HCF of numerical coefficients and each common variable with its lowest power.

HCF of compound expressions: Factorize each expression completely, then take the common factors with their lowest powers.

Division method for HCF: Divide repeatedly; the last non-zero divisor gives the HCF, up to a non-zero constant factor.

LCM of monomials: Take the LCM of numerical coefficients and every variable with its highest power.

LCM of compound expressions: Factorize each expression and take every factor with its highest power.

Algebraic fractions: Fractions whose numerators and/or denominators contain algebraic expressions.

Like fractions: Fractions with the same denominator.

Unlike fractions: Fractions with different denominators.

Combined operations: Apply DMAS; perform division and multiplication before addition and subtraction, following the order from left to right when operations have equal priority.

Square root: A number or expression which, when squared, gives the original number or expression.

Principal square root: The non-negative square root of a non-negative number.

Perfect square expression: An expression that can be written as the square of another expression.

3. DEFINITIONS

Factor: An expression that divides another expression exactly.

Factorization: The process of expressing an algebraic expression as a product of factors.

Highest Common Factor (HCF): The greatest factor common to all given expressions.

Least Common Multiple (LCM): The least expression that is a multiple of all given expressions, subject to the usual constant-factor convention for polynomials.

Algebraic fraction: A fraction in which the numerator or denominator, or both, are algebraic expressions.

Square root: If Q² = P, then Q is a square root of P.

4. KEY POINTS

  • Always look for a common factor before applying an identity.
  • For x² + px + q, the two numbers must add to p and multiply to q.
  • For HCF, use the lowest power of each common variable.
  • For LCM, use the highest power of every variable or factor that appears.
  • For algebraic fractions, factorize first and cancel only common factors, not separate terms.
  • Before dividing by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.
  • When adding or subtracting unlike fractions, use a common denominator, often the LCM.
  • The principal square root is non-negative; the equation x² = P may have both positive and negative roots.
  • To make a perfect square, compare the expression with the expansion of a square and identify the missing terms.
  • For a cube with side (x − 2y), surface area is 6(x − 2y)².
