Important Formulas

1. Basic Differential Equation

dy/dx = f(x)
y = ∫f(x)dx = F(x) + c

2. Order and Degree

Order = highest order derivative
Degree = power of highest order derivative

3. General Form of an nth Order ODE

F(x, y, y′, ..., y(n−1)) = y(n)

4. nth Order Linear Differential Equation

an(x)y(n) + an−1(x)y(n−1) + ... + a1(x)y′ + a0(x)y = f(x)

5. Formation of Differential Equation

Differentiate to eliminate arbitrary constants.
y = ex → y′ = ex → y′ − y = 0
y = a cos x + b sin x → y″ + y = 0
y = A sin(2x − B) → y″ + 4y = 0

6. Variable Separable Differential Equation

dy/dx = f(x)g(y)
dy/g(y) = f(x)dx
∫dy/g(y) = ∫f(x)dx

7. Initial Value Problem (IVP)

dy/dx = f(x,y),   y(x₀) = y₀
General solution → put x₀, y₀ → find c

8. Homogeneous Function

f(tx, ty) = tnf(x, y)

9. Homogeneous Differential Equation

M(x,y)dx + N(x,y)dy = 0
y = ux → dy/dx = u + x(du/dx)
dy = u dx + x du
x = vy

10. Falling Body

a = dv/dt = g
v = gt
S = gt²/2

11. Newton’s Law of Cooling

dT/dt = k(T − T₀)
T − T₀ = cekt

Definitions

  • Differential Equation: An equation containing one or more derivatives of an unknown function.
  • Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE): A differential equation involving derivatives with respect to only one independent variable.
  • Order: The order of a differential equation is the highest order derivative present in it.
  • Degree: The power of the highest order derivative after the equation is made polynomial in derivatives.
  • Linear Differential Equation: A differential equation in which the dependent variable and all its derivatives occur only to the first power and are not multiplied together.
  • Non-linear Differential Equation: A differential equation that does not satisfy the conditions of a linear differential equation.
  • Solution of DE: A function which satisfies the given differential equation on an interval.
  • General Solution: A solution containing arbitrary constants and representing a family of solutions.
  • Particular Solution: A solution obtained by assigning specific values to the arbitrary constants.
  • Explicit Solution: A solution written in the form y = f(x).
  • Implicit Solution: A solution written in the form F(x,y) = 0.
  • Variable Separable Equation: A differential equation in which variables can be separated so that x and y occur on opposite sides.
  • Initial Value Problem: A differential equation together with a specified initial condition such as y(x₀) = y₀.
  • Homogeneous Function: A function satisfying f(tx,ty) = tnf(x,y).
  • Homogeneous Differential Equation: A differential equation that can be transformed into a separable equation using y = ux or x = vy.
  • Newton’s Law of Cooling: The rate of change of temperature is proportional to the difference between the object's temperature and surrounding temperature.

Key Points

  • DE contains derivative(s) of an unknown function.
  • ODE has only one independent variable.
  • Order is determined by the highest derivative.
  • Degree is determined by the power of the highest order derivative, provided the equation is polynomial in derivatives.
  • Order and degree are positive integers when degree is defined.
  • If a derivative occurs inside a logarithm, exponential, trigonometric function, etc., degree may not be defined.
  • An nth-order differential equation generally has n arbitrary constants in its general solution.
  • A first-order DE generally has one arbitrary constant.
  • A second-order DE generally has two arbitrary constants.
  • General solution represents a family of curves.
  • Particular solution represents one member of that family.
  • To form a differential equation, differentiate the given equation enough times to eliminate arbitrary constants.
  • For a linear DE, y and its derivatives must occur to the first power and must not be multiplied together.
  • Terms such as y², yy′, or (y′)² make a differential equation non-linear.
  • In a separable equation, separate x and y variables before integrating.
  • For an IVP, first find the general solution, then use the given initial condition to find the constant.
  • For a homogeneous equation, the substitution y = ux gives dy/dx = u + x(du/dx).
  • The substitution x = vy can also be used for homogeneous equations.
  • For falling-body problems, acceleration is represented by g when air resistance is ignored.
  • In falling-body problems, choose the sign of g according to the selected direction as positive.
  • In Newton’s Law of Cooling, k is usually negative when an object cools toward the surrounding temperature.
  • For Newton’s Law of Cooling, T₀ represents the surrounding temperature.
