FORMULAS

1. Radius and Diameter

d = 2r
r = d/2

2. Tangent from an External Point

l² = d² − r²
l = √(d² − r²)

3. Angle Between Two Tangents

Angle between tangents = 180° − θ
θ is the central angle between the radii drawn to the points of contact.

4. Direct Common Tangent to Unequal Circles

L = √[d² − (R − r)²]

5. Transverse Common Tangent to Unequal Circles

L = √[d² − (R + r)²]

6. Distance Between Centres of Touching Circles

External contact: d = R + r
Internal contact: d = R − r
Symbols:
R = radius of the larger circle
r = radius of the smaller circle
d = distance between the centres
L or l = tangent length
The common tangent length formulas apply only when the required tangents exist.

CONCEPTS

1. Locating the Centre of a Given Circle

  1. Choose three points A, B and C on the circle.
  2. Join AB and BC to form chords.
  3. Construct the perpendicular bisectors of AB and BC.
  4. Their intersection O is the centre of the circle.

2. Drawing a Circle Through Three Non-Collinear Points

  1. Mark the three non-collinear points A, B and C.
  2. Join the points to form a triangle.
  3. Construct the perpendicular bisectors of two sides.
  4. Let them intersect at O.
  5. With O as centre and OA as radius, draw the circle.

3. Completing a Circle from a Given Arc

  1. Choose three points on the given arc.
  2. Join the points to form chords.
  3. Construct the perpendicular bisectors of two chords.
  4. Their intersection gives the centre O.
  5. Draw the complete circle using the distance from O to any point on the arc as the radius.

4. Completing a Circle Without Finding Its Centre

  • Polygon method: Use repeated equal-length segments to continue around the arc and approximate the circle.
  • Hexagon method: Use the equal-chord property of a regular hexagon, with central angles of 60°, to mark points around the circle and complete the curve.
These are approximate drawing methods. Finding the centre using perpendicular bisectors gives a more accurate construction.

5. Drawing a Tangent to a Circle at a Point on It

  1. Join the centre O to the point of contact P.
  2. Construct a perpendicular to OP at P.
  3. The perpendicular line is the tangent.

6. Drawing Tangents from an External Point

  1. Join the centre O to the external point P.
  2. Bisect OP at Q.
  3. Draw an auxiliary circle with centre Q and radius QO.
  4. Let it intersect the given circle at A and B.
  5. Join PA and PB. These are the two tangents.

7. Drawing Two Tangents at a Given Angle

  1. Draw the given circle and mark the required points of contact.
  2. Join the centre to each point of contact.
  3. Use the relationship between the central angle and the angle between the tangents.
  4. Draw a perpendicular to each radius at its point of contact.
  5. The two perpendicular lines are the required tangents.

8. Direct Common Tangents to Two Equal Circles

  1. Draw the line joining the centres of the two circles.
  2. Draw the two equal circles.
  3. Draw radii perpendicular to the line joining the centres, in the same direction.
  4. Join the corresponding endpoints of the radii.
  5. Repeat on the opposite side to obtain the second direct common tangent.

9. Transverse Common Tangents to Two Equal Circles

  1. Draw the two equal circles and join their centres.
  2. Find the midpoint of the line joining the centres.
  3. Use the midpoint construction to locate the tangent contact geometry.
  4. Join the appropriate contact points to obtain the transverse common tangents.

10. Direct Common Tangents to Two Unequal Circles

  1. Draw the two circles with their given radii and centre distance.
  2. Draw an auxiliary circle using the difference of the radii, R − r.
  3. Use the auxiliary construction to locate the required tangent direction.
  4. Draw parallel lines through the corresponding points on the second circle.
  5. Join the corresponding contact points to obtain the direct common tangents.

11. Transverse Common Tangents to Two Unequal Circles

  1. Draw the two circles with their given radii and centre distance.
  2. Draw an auxiliary circle using the sum of the radii, R + r.
  3. Use the auxiliary construction to locate the required tangent direction.
  4. Draw the corresponding parallel lines through the second circle.
  5. Join the appropriate contact points to obtain the transverse common tangents.

12. Tangent to Two Externally Touching Circles

  1. Draw the two circles so that they touch externally.
  2. Join their centres and mark the point of contact.
  3. Draw a perpendicular to the line joining the centres at the common point of contact.
  4. This line is the common tangent at the point where the circles touch.

DEFINITIONS

1. Circle

A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance from a fixed point called the centre.

2. Centre

The fixed point inside a circle from which every point on the circle is at an equal distance.

3. Radius

A line segment joining the centre of a circle to any point on the circle.

4. Diameter

A chord passing through the centre of a circle.

5. Chord

A line segment joining any two points on a circle.

6. Perpendicular Bisector

A line that divides a line segment into two equal parts at a right angle.

7. Tangent

A straight line that touches a circle at exactly one point.

8. Point of Contact

The point at which a tangent touches a circle.

9. External Point

A point lying outside a circle.

10. Common Tangent

A line that is tangent to two circles.

11. Direct Common Tangent

A common tangent that does not cross the segment joining the centres of the two circles.

12. Transverse Common Tangent

A common tangent that crosses the segment joining the centres of the two circles.

13. Concentric Circles

Circles having the same centre but different radii.

14. Non-Collinear Points

Three or more points that do not lie on the same straight line.

KEY POINTS

  • The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre of a circle.
  • The perpendicular bisectors of two chords intersect at the centre of the circle.
  • All radii of the same circle are equal.
  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
  • A tangent from an external point can be constructed using an auxiliary circle with the midpoint of the centre-to-point segment as its centre.
  • Two tangents can be drawn from an external point to a circle, provided the point lies outside the circle.
  • The angle between two tangents and the central angle between their radii add up to 180°.
  • Equal circles have direct common tangents parallel to the line joining their centres.
  • For direct common tangents, use the difference of the radii, R − r.
  • For transverse common tangents, use the sum of the radii, R + r.
  • Two externally touching circles have centre distance R + r.
  • Two internally touching circles have centre distance R − r, where R is the larger radius.
  • At the point where two circles touch externally, their common tangent is perpendicular to the line joining their centres.
  • The tangent-length formulas are based on the Pythagorean theorem.
  • A direct common tangent exists for separated circles; a transverse common tangent requires the centre distance to be greater than the sum of the radii.
Remember:
Direct tangent → Difference of radii
Transverse tangent → Sum of radii
Tangent at contact → Perpendicular to radius
