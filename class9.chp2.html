1. FORMULAS

A. Scientific Notation

N = a × 10n
Where 1 ≤ a < 10 and n ∈ Z.
Decimal moves left → Positive exponent.
Decimal moves right → Negative exponent.

B. Logarithmic and Exponential Forms

y = logbx ⇔ by = x
logb1 = 0
logbb = 1
logb(bx) = x
If logbx = logby, then x = y.

C. Common and Natural Logarithms

log x = log10x
ln x = logex, where e ≈ 2.71828.
log(10n) = n
ln(en) = n
Logarithm = Characteristic + Mantissa

D. Product Law

logb(mn) = logbm + logbn

E. Quotient Law

logb(m/n) = logbm − logbn

F. Power Law

logb(mn) = n logbm
logb√m = ½ logbm
logb∛m = ⅓ logbm

G. Change of Base Law

logam = logbm × logab
logbm = logam / logab
logbm = log m / log b
For common logarithms, base 10 is used.

H. Important Logarithmic Results

logba × logab = 1
logca = 1 / logac
logba × logcb = logca
logba × logcb × logac = 1

I. Richter Scale

M = log(I/I0)
I/I0 = 10M
Here, I is the intensity and I0 is a reference intensity.
I2/I1 = 10M2−M1
For comparing intensities using the Richter formula.

J. Number of Digits

Number of digits in N = ⌊log10N⌋ + 1
For a positive integer N.
Number of digits = Characteristic + 1
For a positive whole number using common logarithms.
log(Nk) = k log N

2. CONCEPTS

Scientific Notation

  • A number is written as a decimal factor multiplied by a power of 10.
  • The decimal factor must be at least 1 and less than 10.
  • Scientific notation makes very large and very small numbers easier to write.

Converting Logarithms

Example 1: log5625 = 4 means 54 = 625.
Example 2: log2x = 4 means x = 24 = 16.

Characteristic and Mantissa

  • Characteristic is the integral part of a common logarithm.
  • Mantissa is the non-negative fractional part of the common logarithm.
  • Logarithm tables are used to find mantissas and antilogarithms.
  • The characteristic helps determine the decimal point in the final number.
Example: log 156.3 ≈ 2.1939
Characteristic = 2; mantissa ≈ 0.1939.
Negative characteristic: −2 + 0.0899 = −1.9101
In logarithm tables, a negative characteristic is written using a bar: 2̅.0899, which means −2 + 0.0899.

Antilogarithm

  • If log x = y, then x = antilog y.
  • Use the mantissa to look up the antilogarithm in the table.
  • Use the characteristic to place the decimal point.
Example: antilog 2 = 102 = 100.

Expanding Logarithmic Expressions

Example: log(5p²q½ / (4√s t³))

= log 5 + 2 log p + ½ log q − log 4 − ½ log s − 3 log t

Combining Logarithmic Expressions

Example: 2 log x + 3 log y

= log x² + log y³
= log(x²y³)

Using Logarithms in Calculations

  • Convert products into sums of logarithms.
  • Convert quotients into differences of logarithms.
  • Convert powers into multiplication by the exponent.
  • Take the antilogarithm to obtain the final numerical result.

3. DEFINITIONS

1. Logarithm

If by = x, where b > 0, b ≠ 1 and x > 0, then y = logbx is called the logarithm of x to base b.

2. Common Logarithm

A logarithm with base 10 is called a common logarithm. It is written as log x.

3. Natural Logarithm

A logarithm with base e is called a natural logarithm. It is written as ln x.

4. Characteristic

The integral part of a common logarithm is called its characteristic.

5. Mantissa

The non-negative fractional part of a common logarithm is called its mantissa.

6. Antilogarithm

If log x = y, then x is called the antilogarithm of y.

7. Product Law

The logarithm of a product is equal to the sum of the logarithms of its factors, provided all logarithms are defined.

8. Quotient Law

The logarithm of a quotient is equal to the logarithm of the numerator minus the logarithm of the denominator.

9. Power Law

The logarithm of a power is equal to the exponent multiplied by the logarithm of the base of that power.

10. Change of Base Law

The change of base law allows a logarithm to be expressed using another valid base.

11. Richter Scale

The Richter scale is a logarithmic scale used to express the magnitude of an earthquake.

4. KEY POINTS

  • The base of a logarithm must be positive and not equal to 1.
  • The argument of a real logarithm must be positive.
  • logb0 and logb(−x) are undefined for real logarithms when x ≥ 0.
  • log12 is undefined because the base cannot be 1.
  • logb1 = 0 and logbb = 1.
  • Common logarithms have base 10; natural logarithms have base e.
  • For 1 ≤ x < 10, 0 ≤ log x < 1.
  • For a positive whole number, the number of digits equals the characteristic plus 1.
  • The mantissa of a common logarithm is non-negative and less than 1.
  • A barred characteristic is negative, while the mantissa remains non-negative.
  • Product law applies to multiplication, not addition or subtraction inside the logarithm.
  • log(m+n) ≠ log m + log n in general.
  • log(m−n) ≠ log m − log n in general.
  • For the power law, logb(mn) = n logbm.
  • For change of base, the denominator must be defined and non-zero.
  • When comparing earthquake magnitudes, a difference of 1 on the Richter scale corresponds to a factor of 10 in the intensity ratio used in the formula.
Remember: Logarithms are exponents. Keep the base and argument conditions in mind before applying any logarithmic law.
