✍️ Important Derivations

1. Derivation of cos(α − β)

Using the cosine difference identity:

cos(α − β) = cos α cos β + sin α sin β

This is the fundamental law used to derive the other sum and difference identities.

2. Derivation of sin(α + β)

sin(α + β) = cos[π/2 − (α + β)]

= cos[(π/2 − α) − β]

= cos(π/2 − α) cos β + sin(π/2 − α) sin β

= sin α cos β + cos α sin β

3. Derivation of sin(α − β)

sin(α − β) = sin[α + (−β)]

= sin α cos(−β) + cos α sin(−β)

= sin α cos β − cos α sin β

4. Derivation of tan(α + β)

tan(α + β) = sin(α + β)/cos(α + β)

= (sin α cos β + cos α sin β)/(cos α cos β − sin α sin β)

Dividing numerator and denominator by cos α cos β:

tan(α + β) = (tan α + tan β)/(1 − tan α tan β)

5. Derivation of sin 2α

sin(α + α) = sin α cos α + cos α sin α

sin 2α = 2 sin α cos α

6. Derivation of cos 2α

cos(α + α) = cos α cos α − sin α sin α

cos 2α = cos² α − sin² α

Using sin² α + cos² α = 1:

cos 2α = 2 cos² α − 1 = 1 − 2 sin² α

7. Derivation of half-angle formulas

cos θ = 1 − 2 sin²(θ/2)

2 sin²(θ/2) = 1 − cos θ

sin(θ/2) = ±√[(1 − cos θ)/2]

Similarly, from cos θ = 2 cos²(θ/2) − 1:

cos(θ/2) = ±√[(1 + cos θ)/2]

8. Derivation of sin 3α

sin 3α = sin(2α + α)

= sin 2α cos α + cos 2α sin α

= 2 sin α cos² α + (cos² α − sin² α) sin α

= 3 sin α cos² α − sin³ α

Using cos² α = 1 − sin² α:

sin 3α = 3 sin α − 4 sin³ α

9. Derivation of cos 3α

cos 3α = cos(2α + α)

= cos 2α cos α − sin 2α sin α

= cos³ α − 3 sin² α cos α

Using sin² α = 1 − cos² α:

cos 3α = 4 cos³ α − 3 cos α

10. Derivation of product-to-sum formulas

sin(P + Q) = sin P cos Q + cos P sin Q

sin(P − Q) = sin P cos Q − cos P sin Q

Adding both equations:

2 sin P cos Q = sin(P + Q) + sin(P − Q)

Subtracting the equations:

2 cos P sin Q = sin(P + Q) − sin(P − Q)

Similarly, using the cosine sum and difference identities:

2 cos P cos Q = cos(P + Q) + cos(P − Q)

2 sin P sin Q = cos(P − Q) − cos(P + Q)

11. Derivation of sum-to-product formulas

Let A = (P + Q)/2 and B = (P − Q)/2.

Then P = A + B and Q = A − B.

Using the sum and difference identities gives:

sin P + sin Q = 2 sin[(P + Q)/2] cos[(P − Q)/2]

sin P − sin Q = 2 cos[(P + Q)/2] sin[(P − Q)/2]

cos P + cos Q = 2 cos[(P + Q)/2] cos[(P − Q)/2]

cos P − cos Q = −2 sin[(P + Q)/2] sin[(P − Q)/2]

12. Power-reduction identity

sin² x = (1 − cos 2x)/2

sin⁴ x = [(1 − cos 2x)/2]²

Using cos² 2x = (1 + cos 4x)/2 and simplifying:

sin⁴ x = (3 − 4 cos 2x + cos 4x)/8

📖 Concepts

Fundamental Law of Trigonometry

The identity cos(α − β) = cos α cos β + sin α sin β is a basic law from which other trigonometric identities can be derived.

Allied Angles

Allied angles are angles related to an angle θ by adding or subtracting multiples of 90° (π/2), such as 90° ± θ, 180° ± θ, 270° ± θ and 360° ± θ.

Double Angle

A double angle is twice a given angle, such as 2θ. Double-angle identities express its trigonometric ratios in terms of θ.

Half Angle

A half angle is half a given angle, such as θ/2. Its formulas are obtained from the double-angle identities.

Triple Angle

A triple angle is three times a given angle, such as 3θ. Triple-angle identities express its trigonometric ratios in terms of θ.

Product-to-Sum

These identities convert products of two trigonometric ratios into sums or differences of trigonometric functions.

Sum-to-Product

These identities convert sums or differences of two sine or cosine functions into products.

Expressing a sin θ + b cos θ in a Single Function

The expression can be written as r sin(θ + φ), where r = √(a² + b²), cos φ = a/r and sin φ = b/r. Choose φ in the correct quadrant.

📝 Definitions

1. Trigonometric Identity

An equation involving trigonometric ratios that is true for every angle for which both sides are defined.

2. Allied Angles

Angles formed by adding or subtracting multiples of 90° to a given angle are called allied angles.

3. Double-Angle Formula

A formula that expresses a trigonometric ratio of 2θ in terms of trigonometric ratios of θ.

4. Half-Angle Formula

A formula that expresses a trigonometric ratio of θ/2 in terms of trigonometric ratios of θ.

5. Triple-Angle Formula

A formula that expresses a trigonometric ratio of 3θ in terms of trigonometric ratios of θ.

6. Product-to-Sum Formula

An identity that converts the product of trigonometric functions into a sum or difference.

7. Sum-to-Product Formula

An identity that converts a sum or difference of trigonometric functions into a product.

📌 Key Points

• The fundamental law is used to derive the sine and cosine sum and difference identities.

• In allied angles, the trigonometric ratio may change to its co-ratio when the angle contains an odd multiple of 90°.

• The sign of a trigonometric ratio depends on the quadrant of the angle.

• For half-angle formulas, the ± sign is chosen according to the quadrant of θ/2.

• For double-angle formulas, remember all three forms of cos 2θ.

• The triple-angle identities for sine and cosine are frequently used to simplify expressions.

• Product-to-sum and sum-to-product identities are useful for simplifying trigonometric expressions.

• When converting a sin θ + b cos θ into a single function, choose φ in the correct quadrant.

• tan(α + β) and tan(α − β) have different denominators. Check the signs carefully.

• Exact values: sin 15° = (√6 − √2)/4, cos 15° = (√6 + √2)/4 and tan 15° = 2 − √3.

• sin 22.5° cos 22.5° = √2/4 ≈ 0.3536.

• For a triangle with α + β + γ = π, cot α cot β + cot β cot γ + cot γ cot α = 1.
