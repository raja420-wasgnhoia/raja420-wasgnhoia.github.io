FORMULAS & RULES

1. Sum of Angles of a Triangle:
∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°
2. Third Angle of a Triangle:
Third angle = 180° − (First angle + Second angle)
Example: Acute Triangle LMN
∠L = 45°, ∠M = 60°
∠N = 180° − 45° − 60°
∠N = 75°
3. Triangle Side Rule:
The sum of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the third side.
a + b > c
a + c > b
b + c > a
4. Equilateral Triangle:
All three sides are equal.
Each interior angle = 60°
5. Isosceles Triangle:
Two sides are equal in length, and the angles opposite those equal sides are equal.
6. Scalene Triangle:
All three sides have different lengths.
7. Acute-Angled Triangle:
All three angles are less than 90°.
8. Right-Angled Triangle:
One angle = 90°
9. Obtuse-Angled Triangle:
One angle is greater than 90°.
10. Perpendicular Lines:
Perpendicular lines intersect at 90°.
11. Perpendicular Distance:
The perpendicular distance from a point to a line is the shortest distance between them.
12. Check Whether a Triangle Can Be Constructed:
Add every pair of side lengths. Each sum must be greater than the remaining side.
Exercise 10.1 Q1 (a):
Sides = 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm
3 + 4 > 5, 3 + 5 > 4, 4 + 5 > 3
Triangle can be constructed.
Exercise 10.1 Q1 (b):
Sides = 5 cm, 7 cm, 2 cm
5 + 2 = 7, which is not greater than 7.
Triangle cannot be constructed.
Exercise 10.1 Q1 (c):

2 + 4.2 = 6.2, which is less than 7.3.
Triangle cannot be constructed.
Exercise 10.1 Q1 (d):

CONCEPTS

1. Introduction to Practical Geometry

The pyramids of ancient Egypt are among the largest structures built by humans. They demonstrate mastery of art, architecture, engineering and social organization.
Geometrical shapes used in pyramids show how geometry is applied in real-life construction and design.

2. Learning Outcomes

- Construct equilateral, isosceles and scalene triangles.
- Construct acute-angled, obtuse-angled and right-angled triangles.
- Understand that the perpendicular distance from a point to a line is the shortest distance.

3. Triangle Basics

A triangle is a closed plane figure with three sides, three angles and three vertices.
Triangles can be classified according to their side lengths or their angles.

4. Types of Triangles According to Sides

Equilateral Triangle: All three sides are equal.
Isosceles Triangle: Two sides are equal.
Scalene Triangle: All three sides are different.

5. Construction of an Equilateral Triangle of Side 5 cm

Step 1: Draw line segment AB = 5 cm.
Step 2: With A as centre and radius 5 cm, draw an arc.
Step 3: With B as centre and radius 5 cm, draw another arc intersecting the first arc at C.
Step 4: Join AC and BC.
ABC is the required equilateral triangle because AB = AC = BC = 5 cm.

6. Construction of Isosceles Triangle PQR

Given: PQ = 6 cm, QR = 4.5 cm and PR = 4.5 cm.
Step 1: Draw PQ = 6 cm.
Step 2: With P as centre and radius 4.5 cm, draw an arc.
Step 3: With Q as centre and radius 4.5 cm, draw another arc intersecting the first at R.
Step 4: Join PR and QR. Triangle PQR is the required isosceles triangle.

7. Construction of Scalene Triangle XYZ

Given: XY = 4 cm, YZ = 5 cm and ZX = 4.5 cm.
Step 1: Draw XY = 4 cm.
Step 2: With X as centre and radius 4.5 cm, draw an arc.
Step 3: With Y as centre and radius 5 cm, draw another arc intersecting the first at Z.
Step 4: Join XZ and YZ. Triangle XYZ is the required scalene triangle.

8. Types of Triangles According to Angles

Acute-Angled Triangle: All angles are less than 90°.
Right-Angled Triangle: One angle is exactly 90°.
Obtuse-Angled Triangle: One angle is greater than 90°.
Right Isosceles Triangle: A right-angled triangle with two equal sides. Its two acute angles are each 45°.

9. Construction of Acute Triangle LMN

Given: LM = 6.2 cm, ∠L = 45° and ∠M = 60°.
Step 1: Draw LM = 6.2 cm.
Step 2: At L, construct a 45° angle and draw a ray.
Step 3: At M, construct a 60° angle on the same side of LM and draw another ray.
Step 4: Let the two rays intersect at N. Triangle LMN is the required acute-angled triangle.
Check: ∠N = 180° − 45° − 60° = 75°.

10. Construction of Right-Angled Triangle DEF

Given: DE = 6 cm, ∠DEP = 90° and DF = 8 cm.
Step 1: Draw DE = 6 cm.
Step 2: At E, construct a 90° angle and draw ray EP perpendicular to DE.
Step 3: With D as centre and radius 8 cm, draw an arc cutting ray EP at F.
Step 4: Join D to F. Triangle DEF is the required right-angled triangle.

11. Construction of Obtuse Triangle ABC

Given: AB = 5 cm, ∠A = 105° and AC = 4.6 cm.
Step 1: Draw AB = 5 cm.
Step 2: At A, construct an angle of 105°.
Step 3: On the second ray, mark AC = 4.6 cm.
Step 4: Join B to C. Triangle ABC is the required obtuse-angled triangle.

12. Perpendicular Distance from a Point to a Line

The perpendicular distance is measured along a line segment that meets the given line at 90°. It is the shortest distance from the point to that line.
Construction Steps:
1. Draw line PQ and mark a point A outside it.
2. With A as centre, draw an arc cutting PQ at B and C.
3. With B and C as centres and equal radii greater than half of BC, draw two arcs intersecting at D on the opposite side of PQ.
4. Join A to D. Let AD intersect PQ at R.
5. AR is perpendicular to PQ.
6. Draw other segments from A to points on PQ and compare their lengths.
7. AR is the shortest distance.

13. Exercise 10.1 Q1: Triangle Construction

(a) 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm: Possible. The triangle inequality is satisfied.
(b) 5 cm, 7 cm, 2 cm: Not possible because 5 + 2 = 7, not greater than 7.
(c) 2 cm, 4.2 cm, 7.3 cm: Not possible because 2 + 4.2 = 6.2, which is less than 7.3.
(d) 4.6 cm, 4.6 cm, 4.6 cm: Possible. It forms an equilateral triangle.

DEFINITIONS

Practical Geometry: The branch of geometry concerned with constructing geometrical figures accurately using instruments such as a ruler and compass.
Triangle: A closed plane figure with three sides, three angles and three vertices.
Equilateral Triangle: A triangle with all three sides equal.
Isosceles Triangle: A triangle with two equal sides.
Scalene Triangle: A triangle with all three sides of different lengths.
Acute-Angled Triangle: A triangle in which all three angles are less than 90°.
Right-Angled Triangle: A triangle with one angle equal to 90°.
Obtuse-Angled Triangle: A triangle with one angle greater than 90°.
Right Isosceles Triangle: A right-angled triangle with two equal sides.
Perpendicular Lines: Two lines that intersect at an angle of 90°.
Perpendicular Distance: The shortest distance from a point to a line, measured along a perpendicular segment.
Compass: A geometrical instrument used to draw circles and arcs and to transfer distances.
Line Segment: A part of a line bounded by two endpoints.
Arc: A part of a circle's circumference.

KEY POINTS

1. A triangle has three sides, three angles and three vertices.
2. The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is 180°.
3. The sum of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the third side.
4. An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three angles of 60° each.
5. An isosceles triangle has two equal sides and equal angles opposite those sides.
6. A scalene triangle has three unequal sides.
7. An acute-angled triangle has all angles less than 90°.
8. A right-angled triangle has one angle equal to 90°.
9. An obtuse-angled triangle has one angle greater than 90°.
10. A right isosceles triangle has one 90° angle and two 45° angles.
11. A triangle cannot have more than one right angle or more than one obtuse angle.
12. The perpendicular distance from a point to a line is the shortest distance.
13. A perpendicular line meets another line at 90°.
14. An equilateral triangle of side 5 cm is constructed by drawing two arcs of radius 5 cm from the two endpoints of the base.
15. In triangle LMN, if ∠L = 45° and ∠M = 60°, then ∠N = 75°.
16. For sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm, a triangle can be constructed.
17. For sides 5 cm, 7 cm and 2 cm, a triangle cannot be constructed because 5 + 2 = 7.
18. For sides 2 cm, 4.2 cm and 7.3 cm, a triangle cannot be constructed because 2 + 4.2 is less than 7.3.
19. Three equal sides of 4.6 cm form an equilateral triangle.
20. The construction of triangles can use a ruler, compass and protractor, depending on the given measurements.
