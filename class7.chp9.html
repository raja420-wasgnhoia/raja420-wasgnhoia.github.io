FORMULAS & RULES

Polygons

1. Sum of Interior Angles:
Sum = (n − 2) × 180°
Here, n = number of sides.
2. Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon:
Interior angle = ((n − 2) × 180°) ÷ n
3. Exterior Angle and Interior Angle:
Interior angle + Exterior angle = 180°
Interior angle = 180° − Exterior angle
Exterior angle = 180° − Interior angle
4. Sum of Exterior Angles:
Sum of exterior angles of any polygon = 360°
5. Exterior Angle of a Regular Polygon:
Exterior angle = 360° ÷ Number of sides
6. Number of Sides of a Regular Polygon:
Number of sides = 360° ÷ Exterior angle
7. Total Number of Diagonals:
Diagonals = n(n − 3) ÷ 2
8. Diagonals from One Vertex:
Number of diagonals = n − 3
9. Triangles Formed from One Vertex:
Number of triangles = n − 2
10. Triangle Angle Sum:
A + B + C = 180°
11. Quadrilateral Angle Sum:
A + B + C + D = 360°

Important Examples

12. Interior Angle of a Regular Hexagon:
n = 6
Interior angle = ((6 − 2) × 180°) ÷ 6
= 720° ÷ 6 = 120°
13. Interior Angle of a Regular 15-Sided Polygon:
Interior angle = ((15 − 2) × 180°) ÷ 15
= (13 × 180°) ÷ 15 = 156°
14. Find an Unknown Quadrilateral Angle:
x + 110° + 90° + 90° = 360°
x + 290° = 360°
x = 70°
15. Find an Unknown Triangle Angle:
∠1 + 19° + 41° = 180°
∠1 = 120°
Vertically opposite angles are equal, so ∠2 = 120°.
∠2 + 38° + y = 180°
120° + 38° + y = 180°
y = 22°

Circle

16. Diameter and Radius:
Diameter = 2 × Radius
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
17. Equal Radii:
All radii of the same circle are equal in length.
18. Diameter:
The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.
19. Circle Centre:
A circle has one and only one centre.
20. Important Circle Relationship:
Every diameter is a chord, but every chord is not a diameter.

CONCEPTS

1. Introduction to Geometry

Designs and buildings created by humans, as well as structures in nature, inspire two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) geometry.
Architects use geometrical shapes to design buildings and make them beautiful.

2. Learning Outcomes

- Calculate interior and exterior angles of a polygon.
- Calculate the sum of interior angles of a polygon.
- Differentiate between convex and concave polygons.
- Calculate unknown angles in triangles and quadrilaterals.
- Recognize quadrilaterals and their characteristics.
- Describe the elements and properties of a circle.

3. Polygon

A polygon is a simple closed plane figure made up of straight line segments. The word "poly" means many, and "gon" refers to angles.
Vertex: The point where two sides of a polygon meet.
Adjacent Sides: Two sides that share a common vertex.
Diagonal: A line segment joining two non-consecutive vertices of a polygon.
Types of Polygons by Number of Sides:
3 sides = Triangle
4 sides = Quadrilateral
5 sides = Pentagon
6 sides = Hexagon
7 sides = Heptagon
8 sides = Octagon
10 sides = Decagon

4. Interior and Exterior Angles

Interior Angle: An angle inside a polygon at one of its vertices.
Exterior Angle: An angle outside a polygon formed by one side and the extension of an adjacent side.
For a regular polygon, all interior angles are equal and all exterior angles are equal.

5. Sum of Interior Angles

Triangle: (3 − 2) × 180° = 180°
Quadrilateral: (4 − 2) × 180° = 360°
Pentagon: (5 − 2) × 180° = 540°
Hexagon: (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°
Heptagon: (7 − 2) × 180° = 900°
Octagon: (8 − 2) × 180° = 1080°
Decagon: (10 − 2) × 180° = 1440°

6. Regular and Irregular Polygons

Regular Polygon: A polygon with all sides equal and all interior angles equal.
Examples: Equilateral triangle and square.
Irregular Polygon: A polygon whose sides or interior angles are not all equal.
Examples: Scalene triangle and a general trapezium.

7. Convex Polygon

A convex polygon has every interior angle less than 180°. It has no inward dents.
Properties of a Convex Polygon:
- All interior angles are less than 180°.
- All diagonals lie inside the polygon.
- The line segment joining any two points of the polygon lies completely inside it.
Regular Convex Polygon: All sides and all interior angles are equal, and all vertices are equally distant from the centre.
Example: Regular pentagon.
Irregular Convex Polygon: A convex polygon whose sides or angles are not all equal.
Example: A non-rectangular parallelogram.

8. Concave Polygon

A concave polygon has at least one interior angle greater than 180°.
Some part of at least one diagonal lies outside a concave polygon.
Convex vs Concave:
Convex: No inward dents; every interior angle is less than 180°; diagonals lie inside.

Concave: Has an inward dent; at least one interior angle is greater than 180°; a diagonal may lie partly outside.
Arrowhead: An arrowhead is a concave quadrilateral. In a symmetrical arrowhead, one pair of angles may be congruent, and its diagonals can intersect at right angles outside part of the shape.

9. Exterior Angles of Polygons

For a rectangle, each exterior angle is 90°.
Sum = 90° + 90° + 90° + 90° = 360°.
For a regular hexagon, each interior angle is 120°.
Each exterior angle = 180° − 120° = 60°.
Sum = 6 × 60° = 360°.
The sum of one exterior angle at each vertex of any polygon is 360°, whether the polygon is regular or irregular.

10. Characteristics of Quadrilaterals

Square: A quadrilateral with four equal sides, four right angles and opposite sides parallel.
Rectangle: A quadrilateral with opposite sides equal and parallel, and all interior angles equal to 90°.
Parallelogram: A quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
Rhombus: A quadrilateral with all sides equal and opposite sides parallel. Its angles are not necessarily all equal.
Trapezium: A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.
Kite: A quadrilateral whose sides form two pairs of equal adjacent sides.

11. Circle

Circle: A closed curve consisting of all points in a plane that are at the same distance from a fixed point.
Centre: The fixed point inside a circle from which all points on the circle are equally distant.
Radius: A line segment joining the centre to any point on the circle. Its length is called the radius. The plural of radius is radii.
Chord: A line segment joining two points on a circle.
Diameter: A chord passing through the centre of a circle.
Arc: A part of the circumference of a circle. Arc AB is written as ⌢AB.
Minor Arc: The shorter arc between two points on a circle.
Major Arc: The longer arc between two points on a circle. It is generally named using three letters.
Semicircle: Either of the two equal arcs formed when a diameter divides a circle. A semicircle is named using three letters, with the first and third letters marking the diameter's endpoints.
Circular Region: The union of a circle and its interior.
Segment of a Circle: A chord divides a circular region into two segments.
Minor segment: The region between the chord and the minor arc.
Major segment: The region between the chord and the major arc.

DEFINITIONS

Geometry: The branch of mathematics concerned with shapes, sizes, positions and properties of figures.
Polygon: A simple closed plane figure made up of straight line segments.
Vertex: The point where two sides of a polygon meet.
Adjacent Sides: Two sides of a polygon that have a common vertex.
Diagonal: A line segment joining two non-consecutive vertices of a polygon.
Interior Angle: An angle formed inside a polygon at one of its vertices.
Exterior Angle: An angle formed outside a polygon by one side and the extension of an adjacent side.
Regular Polygon: A polygon with all sides equal and all interior angles equal.
Irregular Polygon: A polygon whose sides or interior angles are not all equal.
Convex Polygon: A polygon in which every interior angle is less than 180°.
Concave Polygon: A polygon with at least one interior angle greater than 180°.
Square: A quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles.
Rectangle: A quadrilateral with opposite sides equal and all angles right angles.
Parallelogram: A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel.
Rhombus: A quadrilateral with all four sides equal.
Trapezium: A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.
Kite: A quadrilateral with two pairs of equal adjacent sides.
Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equally distant from a fixed point.
Centre: The fixed point from which all points on a circle are equally distant.
Radius: A line segment joining the centre of a circle to a point on the circle.
Chord: A line segment joining two points on a circle.
Diameter: A chord passing through the centre of a circle.
Arc: A part of the circumference of a circle.
Semicircle: Half of a circle's circumference, formed by a diameter.
Minor Segment: The smaller region between a chord and its minor arc.
Major Segment: The larger region between a chord and its major arc.

KEY POINTS

1. A polygon is a simple closed figure made from straight line segments.
2. The number of diagonals from one vertex of an n-sided polygon is n − 3.
3. The total number of diagonals in a polygon is n(n − 3) ÷ 2.
4. The sum of the interior angles of an n-sided polygon is (n − 2) × 180°.
5. A regular polygon has equal sides and equal interior angles.
6. Every interior angle of a convex polygon is less than 180°.
7. A concave polygon has at least one interior angle greater than 180°.
8. The sum of exterior angles of any polygon is 360°.
9. Interior angle + Exterior angle = 180° at a vertex.
10. A square has 4 lines of symmetry.
11. A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry.
12. A rhombus has 2 lines of symmetry.
13. A general parallelogram has no lines of symmetry.
14. A general trapezium has no lines of symmetry.
15. A kite has 1 line of symmetry when it has the usual symmetrical kite shape.
16. The diagonals of a square are equal, bisect each other perpendicularly and divide the square into four congruent right isosceles triangles.
17. The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other, but are not generally perpendicular.
18. Each diagonal of a rectangle divides it into two congruent triangles.
19. Opposite sides of a parallelogram are parallel and equal. Opposite angles are equal, and adjacent angles add up to 180°.
20. The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
21. The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles. They are not generally equal.
22. Joining the midpoints of the four sides of a quadrilateral in order forms a parallelogram.
23. The diagonals of a kite intersect at right angles.
24. A circle has one centre, and all its radii are equal.
25. A diameter is the longest chord of a circle.
26. Every diameter is a chord, but not every chord is a diameter.
27. A diameter divides a circle into two equal semicircles.
28. A major arc and a semicircle are generally named using three letters.
29. A semicircle is an arc. A circular region includes both the circle and its interior.
30. A chord divides a circular region into a minor segment and a major segment.
31. Examples of circles in everyday life include bangles, wall clocks, circular water waves and the rings in the Olympic logo.
32. A rainbow is an example of a curved arc-like shape. The supporting bricks of Sukkur Barrage include semicircular forms.
