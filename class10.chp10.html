FORMULAS

Tangent and Radius

OT ⟂ PT
The radius OT is perpendicular to the tangent PT at the point of contact T.
l² = d² − r²
l = √(d² − r²)
d² = l² + r²
Here, l = tangent length, d = distance from the centre to the external point, and r = radius.

Equal Tangents

PA = PB
∠BAO = ∠CAO
Tangents drawn from the same external point are equal. The line joining that point to the centre bisects the angle between the tangents.

Touching Circles

d = R + r
Distance between centres of externally touching circles.
d = R − r
Distance between centres of internally touching circles, where R is the larger radius.
d = 2r
Distance between centres of congruent circles touching externally.
d = 0
Distance between centres of congruent circles touching internally.

Angles in a Circle

Central Angle = 2 × Inscribed Angle
Inscribed Angle = ½ × Central Angle
∠AOB = 2∠ACB
These formulas apply when the central angle and inscribed angle subtend the same minor arc, with the inscribed angle's vertex on the remaining circumference.
Angle in a Semicircle = 90°
Angles in the Same Segment are Equal
Angle between Tangent and Chord = Angle in Alternate Segment
Angle in Major Segment < 90°
Angle in Minor Segment > 90°

Cyclic Quadrilateral

∠A + ∠C = 180°
∠B + ∠D = 180°
Sum of Angles of a Triangle = 180°

CONCEPTS

Tangent Theorems

Theorem 10.1: If a line is drawn perpendicular to a radius at its outer endpoint, the line is tangent to the circle.
Theorem 10.2: The tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact.
Theorem 10.3: Two tangents drawn to a circle from the same external point are equal in length.
Theorem 10.4: If two circles touch externally, the distance between their centres equals the sum of their radii.
Theorem 10.5: If two circles touch internally, the distance between their centres equals the difference of their radii.
Theorem 10.6 — Alternate Segment Theorem: The angle between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact equals the angle subtended by that chord in the alternate segment.

Angles in Segments

Theorem 10.7: The angle subtended by a minor arc at the centre is double the angle subtended by the same arc at a point on the remaining circumference.
Theorem 10.8: Any two angles in the same segment of a circle are equal.
Theorem 10.9: The angle in a semicircle is a right angle.
Corollary: If an arc subtends a right angle at a point on the remaining circumference, that arc is a semicircle.
Theorem 10.10: The angle in a segment greater than a semicircle is less than a right angle.
Theorem 10.11: The angle in a segment less than a semicircle is greater than a right angle.

Cyclic Quadrilateral

Theorem 10.12: The opposite angles of a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle are supplementary.
A cyclic trapezium is isosceles.
A perpendicular drawn from the centre to a tangent at its point of contact passes through the centre and the point of contact.
A line perpendicular to a diameter at either endpoint is a tangent to the circle at that endpoint.
Tangents at the endpoints of a diameter are parallel.

DEFINITIONS

Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point.
Point of Contact: The point at which a tangent touches a circle.
Radius: A line segment joining the centre of a circle to a point on its circumference.
External Point: A point lying outside a circle.
Segment of a Circle: Either of the two regions into which a chord divides the circular region.
Minor Segment: The smaller region bounded by a chord and its minor arc.
Major Segment: The larger region bounded by a chord and its major arc.
Angle in a Segment: An angle whose vertex lies on the circumference and whose arms pass through the endpoints of a chord.
Inscribed Angle: An angle formed by two chords with its vertex on the circumference of a circle.
Cyclic Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose four vertices lie on the circumference of the same circle.
Concyclic Points: Points that lie on the circumference of the same circle.
Supplementary Angles: Two angles whose sum is 180°.
Alternate Segment: The segment opposite the angle formed between a tangent and a chord at the point of contact.
Externally Touching Circles: Two circles that touch at one point externally.
Internally Touching Circles: Two circles that touch at one point, with one circle inside the other.

KEY POINTS

  • A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point.
  • The radius drawn to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.
  • A line perpendicular to the radius at its outer endpoint is a tangent.
  • Two tangents from the same external point are equal in length.
  • The line joining the centre to an external point bisects the angle between the two tangents.
  • For externally touching circles, d = R + r.
  • For internally touching circles, d = R − r, where R is the larger radius.
  • Congruent circles touching externally have centre distance equal to their diameter.
  • Congruent circles touching internally have coincident centres.
  • The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • The angle subtended by a minor arc at the centre is twice the corresponding inscribed angle.
  • Any two angles in the same segment are equal.
  • The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
  • An arc that subtends a right angle at the circumference is a semicircle.
  • The angle in the major segment is acute, that is, less than 90°.
  • The angle in the minor segment is obtuse, that is, greater than 90°.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
  • A cyclic trapezium is isosceles.
  • For tangent length, use l = √(d² − r²), where d is greater than r.
  • In a cyclic quadrilateral, ∠A + ∠C = 180° and ∠B + ∠D = 180°.

Symbols

r = radius of a circle
R = larger radius when comparing two circles
d = distance between centres or distance from centre to external point, as specified
l = tangent length
