Important Formulas

1. Imaginary Unit

x² + 1 = 0 ⇒ x² = −1 ⇒ x = ±√(−1) = ±i
i = √(−1),   i² = −1
√(−x) = i√x,   x > 0
Pure imaginary number: bi, where b ≠ 0

2. Complex Number

z = a + bi, where a,b ∈ R
Real part = a,   Imaginary part = b

3. Addition and Subtraction

(a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i
(a + bi) − (c + di) = (a − c) + (b − d)i

4. Multiplication

(a + bi)(c + di) = (ac − bd) + (ad + bc)i

5. Powers of i

i¹ = i
i² = −1
i³ = −i
i⁴ = 1
i, −1, −i, 1 → repeat in cycle

6. Conjugate

If z = a + bi, then z̄ = a − bi

7. Division of Complex Numbers

z₁/z₂ = (x₁ + iy₁)/(x₂ + iy₂)
z₁/z₂ = [(x₁ + iy₁)(x₂ − iy₂)] / [(x₂ + iy₂)(x₂ − iy₂)]

8. Additive Inverse

If z = a + bi, then −z = −a − bi

9. Multiplicative Inverse

Multiplicative inverse of a + bi = 1/(a + bi)
1/(a + bi) = (a − bi)/(a² + b²)

10. Properties of Conjugate

z z̄ = |z|²
overline(z₁z₂) = z̄₁ z̄₂
overline(z₁/z₂) = z̄₁/z̄₂

11. Modulus / Absolute Value

If z = a + bi, then |z| = √(a² + b²)
|z| = |−z| = |z̄|
z z̄ = |z|²

12. Geometrical Addition

z₁ + z₂ is represented by the diagonal of the parallelogram formed by z₁ and z₂.

13. Complex Equations

pz + qw = r
p₁z + q₁w = r₁
p₂z + q₂w = r₂

14. Important Factorization

z² + 9 = (z − 3i)(z + 3i)
3z² + 5 = (√3z − i√5)(√3z + i√5)

15. Electrical Impedance

E = IZ
Z = E/I

Definitions

Imaginary Unit: The imaginary unit i is defined by i = √(−1), so i² = −1.

Imaginary Number: A number having a non-zero imaginary part, written in the form a + bi where b ≠ 0.

Pure Imaginary Number: A complex number of the form bi where b ≠ 0.

Complex Number: A number of the form a + bi where a and b are real numbers.

Conjugate: The conjugate of a + bi is a − bi. The real part remains the same and the sign of the imaginary part changes.

Modulus: The modulus of z = a + bi is |z| = √(a² + b²), representing the distance of z from the origin in the complex plane.

Complex Plane: The plane in which the real part is represented on the horizontal axis and the imaginary part on the vertical axis.

Argand Diagram: A graphical representation of complex numbers on the complex plane.

Additive Inverse: For z = a + bi, the additive inverse is −z = −a − bi, and z + (−z) = 0.

Multiplicative Inverse: The multiplicative inverse of a non-zero complex number z is 1/z, such that z(1/z) = 1.

Impedance: In an AC electrical circuit, impedance is represented by the complex number Z and satisfies E = IZ.

Key Points

  • i² = −1 and the powers of i repeat after every four powers.
  • Every real number is also a complex number because a real number can be written as a + 0i.
  • A complex number has the form a + bi.
  • If a = 0 and b ≠ 0, the number is pure imaginary.
  • If b = 0, the complex number is real.
  • For addition and subtraction, combine real parts with real parts and imaginary parts with imaginary parts.
  • For multiplication, use i² = −1.
  • To divide complex numbers, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.
  • The conjugate changes only the sign of the imaginary part.
  • z z̄ is always a real number and equals |z|².
  • The modulus |z| represents the distance of z from the origin.
  • Real numbers lie on the horizontal axis of the complex plane.
  • Pure imaginary numbers lie on the vertical axis of the complex plane.
  • Complex numbers are used in electronics, electromagnetism, engineering and control systems.
