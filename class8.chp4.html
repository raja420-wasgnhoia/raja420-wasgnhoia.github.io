FORMULAS / RULES / CONCEPTS

1. Direct Proportion

y ∝ x
y = kx
y / x = k
a / b = c / d
ad = bc

In direct proportion, both quantities increase or decrease together in the same ratio.

2. Inverse Proportion

y ∝ 1 / x
y = k / x
xy = k

In inverse proportion, when one quantity increases, the other decreases so their product remains constant.

3. Compound Proportion

Combined ratio = Direct ratios × Inverse ratios

Identify which quantities vary directly and which vary inversely, then combine their ratios accordingly.

4. Simple Partnership

Profit or Loss Share = Total Profit or Loss × Capital / Total Capital

When partners invest for the same period, profit or loss is divided in the ratio of their capitals.

5. Compound Partnership

Weighted Investment = Capital × Time
Partner's Share = Total Profit × Partner's Weighted Investment / Total Weighted Investment

When investment amounts or durations differ, use capital multiplied by time to determine the sharing ratio.

6. Inheritance

Net Estate = Total Estate − Debts − Applicable Expenses
Beneficiary's Share = Distributable Estate × Applicable Share Fraction

Inheritance shares depend on the applicable legal and religious rules and the circumstances of the estate.

7. Currency Exchange

PKR Amount = Foreign Currency Amount × Exchange Rate
Foreign Currency Amount = PKR Amount / Exchange Rate

Use the relevant buying or selling rate provided for the transaction.

Exchange rates change over time. Always use the rate given in the question.

8. Simple Markup

I = (P × R × T) / 100
A = P + I
P = (I × 100) / (R × T)
R = (I × 100) / (P × T)
T = (I × 100) / (P × R)

P = Principal, R = Rate percent per year, T = Time in years, I = Markup, A = Total Amount.

9. Profit and Loss

Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price
Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price
Profit % = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100
Loss % = (Loss / Cost Price) × 100
Selling Price = Cost Price + Profit
Selling Price = Cost Price − Loss

10. Successive Profit and Loss

New Price = Previous Price × (1 + Rate / 100) for a profit
New Price = Previous Price × (1 − Rate / 100) for a loss

For each successive change, calculate the next change using the updated price.

11. Discount

Discount = Marked Price − Selling Price
Discount % = (Discount / Marked Price) × 100
Selling Price = Marked Price × (1 − Discount Rate / 100)
Marked Price = Selling Price / (1 − Discount Rate / 100)

12. Insurance Premium

Premium = Insured Value × Premium Rate / 100
Total Premium = Sum of Premiums for All Periods

Use the premium rate and insured value specified in the question or policy.

13. Depreciation and Vehicle Insurance

Depreciation = Previous Value × Depreciation Rate / 100
New Value = Previous Value − Depreciation
Next Premium = New Insured Value × Premium Rate / 100

When the insured value decreases due to depreciation, the next premium may also decrease if the rate remains unchanged.

CONCEPTS

Direct Proportion

Two quantities are directly proportional when their ratio remains constant.

Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are inversely proportional when their product remains constant.

Compound Proportion

Compound proportion involves three or more quantities related through direct or inverse proportion.

Partnership

Partnership is a business arrangement in which two or more partners invest capital and share profit or loss.

Shares depend on capital and, when investment periods differ, the time for which capital is invested.

Inheritance

Inheritance is the distribution of a deceased person's estate among eligible heirs according to applicable rules.

Currency Exchange

Currency exchange is the conversion of money from one currency into another using an exchange rate.

Simple Markup

Simple markup is calculated on the original principal for the specified rate and time.

Profit and Loss

Profit occurs when the selling price is greater than the cost price. Loss occurs when the selling price is less than the cost price.

Discount

A discount is a reduction from the marked price of an item.

Insurance

Insurance is an arrangement that provides financial protection against specified risks under a policy.

Depreciation

Depreciation is the decrease in the value of an asset over time.

DEFINITIONS

1. Proportion

A proportion is an equality between two ratios.

2. Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in direct proportion if their ratio is constant.

3. Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are in inverse proportion if their product is constant.

4. Partnership

A partnership is a business owned by two or more persons who contribute capital and share its results.

5. Inheritance

Inheritance is property or assets received by eligible heirs after a person's death.

6. Exchange Rate

An exchange rate is the value of one currency expressed in terms of another currency.

7. Principal

The principal is the original amount of money on which markup is calculated.

8. Markup

Markup is an amount charged or earned on the principal according to a specified rate and time.

9. Cost Price

The cost price is the amount paid to purchase an item.

10. Selling Price

The selling price is the amount for which an item is sold.

11. Marked Price

The marked price is the price displayed or listed by the seller before discount.

12. Discount

Discount is the amount deducted from the marked price.

13. Insurance

Insurance is a contract that provides specified financial protection in return for a premium.

14. Premium

A premium is the amount paid to an insurance company for insurance coverage.

15. Policy

An insurance policy is the contract describing the coverage, conditions and obligations.

16. Insurer

The insurer is the company or organization that provides insurance coverage.

17. Insured

The insured is the person or entity covered by an insurance policy.

18. Depreciation

Depreciation is the reduction in an asset's value over a period of time.

19. Maturity Period

The maturity period is the time at which a policy or financial arrangement reaches its specified end date.

KEY POINTS

  • In direct proportion, the ratio of the quantities remains constant.
  • In inverse proportion, the product of the quantities remains constant.
  • Compound proportion combines direct and inverse relationships.
  • In a simple partnership, capital determines the sharing ratio when all partners invest for the same time.
  • In a compound partnership, compare capital multiplied by investment time.
  • Inheritance must be distributed according to the applicable rules after relevant liabilities and expenses.
  • Currency exchange calculations must use the given exchange rate and correct conversion direction.
  • Simple markup is calculated on the original principal.
  • Profit and loss percentages are generally calculated using cost price.
  • Discount percentage is calculated using marked price.
  • For successive price changes, each new percentage applies to the updated price.
  • Insurance premiums depend on the policy terms, insured value and applicable rate.
  • Depreciation reduces an asset's value over time.
  • Always use the rates and conditions provided in the question.
