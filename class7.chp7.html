FORMULAS & RULES

Cartesian Plane

Ordered pair = (x, y)
Quadrant I = (+, +)
Quadrant II = (−, +)
Quadrant III = (−, −)
Quadrant IV = (+, −)
Right = +x; Left = −x; Up = +y; Down = −y.

Translation Rules

(x, y) → (x ± a, y ± b)
Right by a units: (x, y) → (x + a, y)
Left by a units: (x, y) → (x − a, y)
Up by b units: (x, y) → (x, y + b)
Down by b units: (x, y) → (x, y − b)
5 units right and 4 units up: (x, y) → (x + 5, y + 4)
A(−3, 3) → A′(2, 7)
B(−5, −1) → B′(0, 3)
C(−2, 0) → C′(3, 4)

Lines of Symmetry

Isosceles triangle = 1 line of symmetry
Rectangle = 2 lines of symmetry
Letter E = 1 line of symmetry
Butterfly = 1 vertical line of symmetry
Square diamond shape = 2 lines of symmetry
Regular hexagon = 6 lines of symmetry

Rotational Symmetry

Angle of rotation = 360° ÷ Order of rotation
Equilateral triangle: 360° ÷ 3 = 120°
Parallelogram: order 2; angle = 180°
Four-petal flower = order 4
Three-blade fan = order 3
Square = order 4

Regular Polygons

Regular n-gon: n lines of symmetry and order n
Angle of rotation = 360° ÷ n
Triangle: 3 sides, 3 lines, order 3, angle 120°
Square: 4 sides, 4 lines, order 4, angle 90°
Pentagon: 5 sides, 5 lines, order 5, angle 72°
Hexagon: 6 sides, 6 lines, order 6, angle 60°
Heptagon: 7 sides, 7 lines, order 7, angle 51 3/7°

CONCEPTS

1. Cartesian Plane

The Cartesian plane helps locate points, like a map. It consists of two mutually perpendicular number lines.

  • The horizontal line is the x-axis.
  • The vertical line is the y-axis.
  • Their intersection is called the origin O.
  • A point is written as an ordered pair (x, y).
  • Always read x first, then y.

Example: A(−2, 3) means 2 units left of the y-axis and 3 units above the x-axis.

2. Quadrants and Points

  • A(4, 3) lies in Quadrant I.
  • B(−3, 4) lies in Quadrant II.
  • C(−3, −4) lies in Quadrant III.
  • D(3, −4) lies in Quadrant IV.
  • E(0, −3) and G(0, 3) lie on the y-axis.
  • F(−2, 0) lies on the x-axis.

Points on either axis do not belong to any quadrant.

3. Joining Points

Joining (1, 2), (0, −1), and (5, 3) forms a triangle.

Joining (1, 1), (2, 2), and (−3, −3) forms a straight line.

4. Translation

Translation slides an object along a straight line without turning it or changing its shape or size. It can be horizontal, vertical, or diagonal.

If triangle ABC is translated 3 squares right and 4 squares down, its image A′B′C′ has the same shape and size. Only its position changes.

5. Reflection and Line Symmetry

Reflection produces the mirror image of a figure. The mirror line is called the line of reflection or line of symmetry.

A figure has reflective symmetry if folding it along a line makes both halves match exactly.

Reflection can be used to create figures with line symmetry.

6. Rotation

Rotation is a turning motion about a fixed point called the centre of rotation. A figure can rotate clockwise or anticlockwise.

Examples include a pinwheel, a windmill, and Earth rotating about its axis. Earth's axis is tilted about 23.5°.

7. Rotational Symmetry

A figure has rotational symmetry if it fits exactly over its original position during a turn of less than one full revolution.

Order of rotation is the number of times a figure fits over its original position during a full 360° turn.

  • Four-petal flower: order 4.
  • Three-blade fan: order 3.
  • Square: order 4.
  • Parallelogram: order 2, because it matches after a 180° turn.
  • Equilateral triangle: order 3; it matches after 120°, 240°, and 360°.

DEFINITIONS

Transformation: A change in the position or orientation of a geometric figure, such as translation, reflection, rotation, or dilation.

Cartesian plane: A plane formed by two mutually perpendicular number lines, the x-axis and y-axis.

Origin: The point where the x-axis and y-axis intersect, written as O or (0, 0).

Ordered pair: A pair of numbers (x, y) that identifies the location of a point on the Cartesian plane.

Quadrant: One of the four regions into which the axes divide the Cartesian plane.

Translation: Sliding an object without turning it or changing its shape or size.

Reflection: A transformation that creates the mirror image of a figure across a line.

Line of symmetry: A line along which a figure can be folded so that its two halves match exactly.

Rotation: Turning a figure about a fixed point.

Centre of rotation: The fixed point about which a figure turns.

Rotational symmetry: When a figure matches its original position after a rotation.

Order of rotation: The number of times a figure matches its original position during a complete 360° turn.

Angle of rotation: The smallest angle that maps a regular figure onto itself; 360° divided by its order of rotation.

KEY POINTS

  • The four common transformations are translation, reflection, rotation, and dilation.
  • The x-coordinate is always written first in (x, y).
  • Positive x is right; negative x is left.
  • Positive y is up; negative y is down.
  • Points on the axes are not in any quadrant.
  • Translation changes position but preserves shape and size.
  • Reflection produces a mirror image across a line.
  • A shape can have line symmetry, rotational symmetry, both, or neither.
  • A parallelogram generally has no line symmetry, but has rotational symmetry of order 2.
  • For a regular n-sided polygon, the number of lines of symmetry and order of rotation are both n.
  • Angle of rotation = 360° ÷ n.
