Important Formulas

1. Point of Intersection

l₁: a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
l₂: a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0
x/(b₁c₂ − b₂c₁) = y/(c₁a₂ − c₂a₁) = 1/(a₁b₂ − a₂b₁)

2. Condition of Concurrency

a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0
a₃x + b₃y + c₃ = 0
| a₁ b₁ c₁ |
| a₂ b₂ c₂ | = 0
| a₃ b₃ c₃ |

3. Triangle Centers

Altitude: (x₃−x₂)x + (y₃−y₂)y − x₁(x₃−x₂) − y₁(y₃−y₂) = 0
Perpendicular Bisector: (x₃−x₂)x + (y₃−y₂)y − ½(x₃²−x₂²) − ½(y₃²−y₂²) = 0
Midpoint of BC: D = ((x₂+x₃)/2, (y₂+y₃)/2)
Median: (y−y₁)/((y₂+y₃)/2−y₁) = (x−x₁)/((x₂+x₃)/2−x₁)
m = (y₂−y₁)/(x₂−x₁)
For perpendicular lines: m₁m₂ = −1

4. Area of Triangle

Area of Trapezium = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × distance
A = ½ |x₁(y₂−y₃) + x₂(y₃−y₁) + x₃(y₁−y₂)|
Area = ½ | x₁ y₁ 1
x₂ y₂ 1
x₃ y₃ 1 |

5. Joint Equation of Two Lines

(a₁x+b₁y+c₁)(a₂x+b₂y+c₂) = 0
ax² + 2hxy + by² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0
Homogeneous pair: (a₁x+b₁y)(a₂x+b₂y) = 0
ax² + 2hxy + by² = 0
(y−m₁x)(y−m₂x) = 0
m₁m₂ = a/b
m₁ + m₂ = −2h/b

6. Angle Between Pair of Lines

tan θ = |(m₁−m₂)/(1+m₁m₂)|
tan θ = 2√(h²−ab) / |a+b|
Coincident lines: h² − ab = 0
Perpendicular lines: a + b = 0

7. Nature of Pair of Lines

h² > ab → Real and distinct
h² = ab → Real and coincident
h² < ab → Imaginary

8. Useful Example Results

3k + 7 = 0 → k = −7/3
x² − 7xy + 12y² = (x−3y)(x−4y)
x² − 4xy − 12y² = (x+2y)(x−6y)
For the rail-track example: tan θ = 8/13
θ ≈ 36.03°

Definitions

  • Point of Intersection: The common point of two non-parallel lines.
  • Concurrent Lines: Three or more lines passing through the same point.
  • Point of Concurrency: The point at which three or more lines meet.
  • Incenter: The point of intersection of the three angle bisectors of a triangle.
  • Circumcenter: The point of intersection of the three perpendicular bisectors of a triangle.
  • Centroid: The point of intersection of the three medians of a triangle.
  • Orthocenter: The point of intersection of the three altitudes of a triangle.
  • Homogeneous Linear Equation: A linear equation of the form ax + by = 0.
  • Non-Homogeneous Linear Equation: A linear equation containing a non-zero constant term, such as ax + by + c = 0.
  • Joint Equation: A single equation representing two lines together.
  • Homogeneous Quadratic Equation: A second-degree equation of the form ax² + 2hxy + by² = 0.
  • Pair of Lines: Two straight lines represented together by one joint equation.

Key Points

  • If the determinant of three lines is zero, the lines are concurrent.
  • Alternatively, find the intersection of two lines and substitute it into the third line.
  • Three diameters of a circle are always concurrent at the center.
  • The three altitudes of a triangle are concurrent at the orthocenter.
  • The three perpendicular bisectors of a triangle are concurrent at the circumcenter.
  • The three medians of a triangle are concurrent at the centroid.
  • The centroid divides each median in the ratio 2:1.
  • A homogeneous linear equation has no constant term and passes through the origin.
  • A homogeneous quadratic equation also represents a pair of lines passing through the origin.
  • A joint equation is obtained by combining two line equations into one equation.
  • The general second-degree equation can represent a pair of straight lines under a specific condition.
  • The condition for the pair of lines to be perpendicular is a + b = 0.
  • If h² > ab, the pair of lines is real and distinct.
  • If h² = ab, the pair of lines is real and coincident.
  • If h² < ab, the pair of lines is imaginary.
  • For perpendicular lines, the product of their slopes is −1.
  • The angle between two lines can be found from their slopes or from the coefficients of their joint equation.
  • The area of a triangle is always taken as a positive value.
  • If the area calculated from the determinant is zero, the three points are collinear.
  • The area of a triangle can also be found by dividing it into trapeziums.
  • Heron's formula can also be used to find the area of a triangle after finding its three side lengths.
  • Real-life roads or tracks can be checked for concurrency using the determinant or direct substitution method.
  • For a joint equation representing two tracks, factorization gives the individual equations of the tracks.
