FORMULAS & RULES

1. Four Basic Algebraic Identities

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab
(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²

2. Identity Examples

(x + 2y)² = x² + 4xy + 4y²
(2m + 5n)² = 4m² + 20mn + 25n²
(x − 4)² = x² − 8x + 16
(x + 1)(x + 2) = x² + 3x + 2
(2x + 5)(2x − 6) = 4x² − 2x − 30

3. Mental Multiplication Using Identities

103 × 102 = (100 + 3)(100 + 2)
= 10000 + 500 + 6
= 10506
102 × 97 = (100 + 2)(100 − 3)
= 10000 − 100 − 6
= 9894

4. Algebraic Expressions

4 cups of tea, 1 cup = z ml → Total = 4z ml
Add 3x to 7y = 3x + 7y
Subtract 7x from 12 = 12 − 7x
Multiply y by 3z = 3yz
Divide 4a by 9b = 4a/(9b)
Subtract the product of 2x and 5y from the sum of a and b = a + b − 10xy

5. Degree of Monomials and Polynomials

12 → Degree 0, monomial
4x → Degree 1
7y² → Degree 2
−15z⁶ → Degree 6
Degree of a monomial = sum of the exponents of its variables.
Degree of a polynomial = greatest degree among its terms.

6. Like-Term Rules

3x + 8x = (3 + 8)x = 11x
(−5x) + (−8x) = −13x
−5x + 8x = 3x
5x − 8x = −3x
3x² + 7x² = 10x²

7. Addition of Polynomials

(2x² + y² + 3z²) + (6x² − 3y² + 9z + 6)
= 8x² − 2y² + 3z² + 9z + 6
(4x² − 5xy − 6y²) + (11xy − 7x² + 10y²) + (6y² − 5x² + 10xy)
= −8x² + 16xy + 10y²

8. Perimeter and Area

Perimeter of a rectangle = sum of all sides.
Sides: xy, x² + y, xy, x² + y
P = 2xy + 2(x² + y)
= 2x² + 2xy + 2y
Rectangle width = x, length = 2x + 3
Area = x(2x + 3) = 2x² + 3x
Area = 24a²b − 18ab²
= 6ab(4a − 3b)
The factors are 6ab and (4a − 3b).

9. Subtraction of Polynomials

(8x − 12) − (4x − 10)
= 8x − 12 − 4x + 10
= 4x − 2
(3x² − 9x − 6) − (4x² − 7x)
= −x² − 2x − 6
(5a⁵ + 3a⁴ − 2a³ + 3a + 1) − (2a⁵ − 2a⁴ + 5a³ − 8)
= 3a⁵ + 5a⁴ − 7a³ + 3a + 9

10. Multiplication of Polynomials

(+x)(+y) = +xy
(−x)(−y) = +xy
(+x)(−y) = −xy
(−x)(+y) = −xy
aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
2x³(x³ + 3x² − 2x + 4)
= 2x⁶ + 6x⁵ − 4x⁴ + 8x³
(8x²y²z)(2xyz) = 16x³y³z²
a(b + c + d) = ab + ac + ad
6xy(3x − 2y) = 18x²y − 12xy²
6x(8x³ − 3x² + x) = 48x⁴ − 18x³ + 6x²
(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd
(a + b)(c + d + e) = ac + ad + ae + bc + bd + be
(x − 4)(3x + 2)
= 3x² + 2x − 12x − 8
= 3x² − 10x − 8

11. Factorization

Factorization of a trinomial ax² + bx + c:
Write in descending powers, then split the middle term using two numbers whose product is a × c and whose sum is b.
Type 1: Middle +, Last +
6x² + 19x + 10
Product = 6 × 10 = 60; numbers = 4 and 15
= 6x² + 4x + 15x + 10
= 2x(3x + 2) + 5(3x + 2)
= (3x + 2)(2x + 5)
Type 2: Middle +, Last −
3x² + 22x − 16
Product = 3 × (−16) = −48; numbers = 24 and −2
= 3x² + 24x − 2x − 16
= 3x(x + 8) − 2(x + 8)
= (x + 8)(3x − 2)
Type 3: Middle −, Last +
3k² − 16k + 16
Product = 3 × 16 = 48; numbers = −4 and −12
= 3k² − 4k − 12k + 16
= k(3k − 4) − 4(3k − 4)
= (3k − 4)(k − 4)
Type 4: Middle −, Last −
x² − x − 12
Product = 1 × (−12) = −12; numbers = −4 and +3
= x² − 4x + 3x − 12
= x(x − 4) + 3(x − 4)
= (x − 4)(x + 3)

12. Sequences and Patterns

Arithmetic sequence: add or subtract the same constant each time.
1, 3, 5, 7, ... → add 2 → Next: 9, 11, 13
12, 17, 22, ... → add 5 → Next: 27, 32, 37
50, 47, 44, 41, 38, ... → subtract 3 → Next: 35, 32, 29
Geometric sequence: multiply the previous term by the same fixed number each time.
1, 2, 4, ... → multiply by 2 → Next: 8, 16, 32
Pentagon pattern:
1 pentagon = 5 lines
2 pentagons = 10 lines
3 pentagons = 15 lines
4 pentagons = 20 lines
Rule: L = 5P
For 63 pentagons: L = 5 × 63 = 315 lines

CONCEPTS

1. Algebra

Algebra studies relationships among symbols and uses mathematical expressions to model relationships and changes. It helps solve everyday problems.

2. Variables, Constants and Coefficients

Qasim = y years; Furqan = (y + 3) years.
Furqan = x years; Qasim = (x − 3) years.
In 9y, 9 is the coefficient and y is the variable.
x + y + 12: variables x, y; constant 12; implied coefficients are 1.
x² + y + 5z: variables x, y, z; coefficients 1, 1, 5.
7 + 3y = 15: variable y; constants 7 and 15; coefficient 3.

3. Terms and Expressions

In x + y, the terms are x and y.
In x² − 3xz + yz, the terms are x², −3xz and yz.

4. Monomials and Polynomials

Monomial examples: 12, 4x, 7y², −15z⁶.
Not monomials: 2 + y, y/x, 3/x³, x⁻².
Polynomial examples:
8 is a constant polynomial of degree 0.
5x² + x − 6 is a degree 2 trinomial.
6y⁴ − 7^? is unclear as written in the source notes. A variable raised to a non-whole-number or negative exponent makes an expression non-polynomial.
7x²y³ + 4x²z is a degree 5 binomial because the first term has degree 2 + 3 = 5.
p² + pq + 8 is a degree 2 trinomial.
x² − x³ + 6x + 1/4 is a degree 3 polynomial with four terms.
x² + y^(1/2) + 6 is not a polynomial because of the fractional exponent.
9x²y + 6x²y³ · xy + x² + 4 is a polynomial of degree 5 with four terms as written.

5. Leading Coefficient

6x³ + x² − 7x + 13
Degree = 3; leading coefficient = 6; constant term = 13.
18x − x³ + 3 = −x³ + 18x + 3
Degree = 3; leading coefficient = −1.

6. Addition and Subtraction

Horizontal method: Group like terms in one line and simplify.

Vertical method: Arrange like terms in columns and add or subtract each column.

Subtraction rule: Change every sign in the polynomial being subtracted, then add like terms.

7. Multiplication

Multiply each term of one expression by every term of the other expression. Apply the sign rules and add exponents when multiplying powers with the same base.

8. Algebraic Identities

An algebraic identity is a relationship that remains true for all allowed values of its variables. It simplifies calculations without expanding every step from scratch.

Identity I proof: A square with side (a + b) has area (a + b)². Splitting it into parts gives a² + ab + ab + b², which equals a² + 2ab + b².

Identity II proof: Expanding (a − b)(a − b) gives a² − ab − ab + b² = a² − 2ab + b².

Identity III proof: Expand (x + a)(x + b) to get x² + xb + ax + ab = x² + (a + b)x + ab.

Identity IV proof: Expanding (a + b)(a − b) gives a² − ab + ab − b² = a² − b².

9. Factorization

Factorization writes an expression as a product of simpler factors. For a trinomial ax² + bx + c, split the middle term using factors of ac that add to b, then group and factor.

10. Number Sequences

A sequence is an ordered arrangement of numbers called terms. A rule determines how the terms are formed and helps predict the next term.

DEFINITIONS

1. Algebra: A branch of mathematics that uses symbols and variables to represent numbers and relationships.

2. Variable: A letter or symbol such as x, y or z that represents a number whose value may vary.

3. Constant: A fixed numerical value, such as 2, 6, 0 or −3.

4. Coefficient: The numerical factor multiplying a variable. In 9y, the coefficient is 9.

5. Algebraic Expression: An expression made using numbers, variables and operation signs, such as 3x − 3 or 7y + 9.

6. Term: A part of an algebraic expression separated by addition or subtraction signs.

7. Monomial: A constant, a variable, or a product of constants and variables with non-negative whole-number exponents.

8. Degree of a Monomial: The sum of the exponents of its variables.

9. Polynomial: A monomial or a sum of monomials with non-negative whole-number exponents.

10. Degree of a Polynomial: The greatest degree of its terms after simplification.

11. Binomial: A polynomial with two terms, such as 2x − 13.

12. Trinomial: A polynomial with three terms, such as x + 4y − 8.

13. Leading Coefficient: The coefficient of the highest-degree term when a polynomial is written in descending powers.

14. Like Terms: Terms containing the same variables raised to the same exponents.

15. Unlike Terms: Terms that do not have exactly the same variables and exponents.

16. Algebraic Identity: An equality that is true for all permitted values of its variables.

17. Factorization: Expressing a mathematical expression as a product of factors.

18. Sequence: An ordered arrangement of numbers called terms, formed according to a rule.

19. Arithmetic Sequence: A sequence in which a constant is added to or subtracted from each term to obtain the next term.

20. Geometric Sequence: A sequence in which each term is multiplied by the same fixed number to obtain the next term.

21. Perimeter: The total length of the boundary of a shape.

22. Area: The amount of surface enclosed by a two-dimensional shape.

23. Father of Algebra: Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi, a ninth-century Muslim mathematician associated with the development of algebra. The word algebra comes from the title of his work, Kitab al-Jabr wal-Muqabala.

KEY POINTS

1. Algebra Basics

Algebra is generalized arithmetic and uses letters to represent numbers.
In 4z, the coefficient of z is 4.
In x + y + 12, the implied coefficient of x and y is 1.

2. Polynomial Rules

A polynomial cannot have variables with negative or fractional exponents, or variables in denominators.
A constant polynomial other than zero has degree 0.
A polynomial's degree is the highest term degree after simplification.
Like terms can be combined; unlike terms cannot be combined into a single term.

3. Addition and Subtraction

Combine like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
When subtracting brackets, change the sign of every term inside the second bracket.

4. Multiplication

Positive × positive = positive; negative × negative = positive.
Positive × negative = negative; negative × positive = negative.
When multiplying powers with the same base, add their exponents.

5. Identities to Memorize

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
(a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²
(x + a)(x + b) = x² + (a + b)x + ab
(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²

6. Factorization Checklist

1. Arrange terms in descending powers.
2. Multiply the leading coefficient by the last term's coefficient.
3. Find two numbers whose product is ac and sum is b.
4. Split the middle term.
5. Group terms and factor out common factors.

7. Sequences

Arithmetic sequence: add or subtract the same number each time.
Geometric sequence: multiply by the same number each time.
Pentagon pattern: L = 5P. For P = 63, L = 315 lines.

8. Quick Revision

Monomial: one term.
Binomial: two terms.
Trinomial: three terms.
Like terms have the same variable part and exponents.
A sequence's rule helps predict its next term.
Perimeter = sum of boundary sides.
Rectangle area = length × width.
