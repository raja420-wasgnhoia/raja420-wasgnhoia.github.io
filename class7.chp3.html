FORMULAS & RULES

1. Ratio

Forms of a ratio:
\(a\text{ to }b = a:b = \frac{a}{b}\), where \(b\neq0\).
Ratio in simplest form:
Divide all terms by their highest common factor (HCF).
Ratio of three quantities:
\(a:b:c\)
Divide a quantity in a given ratio:
Sum of ratio parts \(=a+b+c\)
First share \(=\frac{a}{a+b+c}\times\text{Total}\)
Second share \(=\frac{b}{a+b+c}\times\text{Total}\)
Third share \(=\frac{c}{a+b+c}\times\text{Total}\)

2. Increase and Decrease in Ratio

Comparison of new and old quantities:
\(\text{Ratio}=\frac{\text{New quantity}}{\text{Old quantity}}\)
Increase:
\(\frac{\text{New}}{\text{Old}}>1\)
Decrease:
\(\frac{\text{New}}{\text{Old}}<1\)
When a quantity decreases by a known amount:
New quantity \(=\text{Old quantity}-\text{Decrease}\)

3. Rate and Unit Rate

Rate:
\(\text{Rate}=\frac{\text{First quantity}}{\text{Second quantity}}\)
Unit rate:
\(\text{Unit rate}=\frac{\text{Total quantity}}{\text{Number of units}}\)
Total cost or earnings at a constant rate:
\(\text{Total}=\text{Rate per unit}\times\text{Number of units}\)

4. Proportion

General form:
\(\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\), where \(b\neq0,\ d\neq0\).
Equivalent ratios:
\(a:b::c:d\)
Cross-product rule:
\(\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}\Rightarrow ad=bc\)

5. Direct Proportion

Direct proportion:
\(\frac{x_1}{x_2}=\frac{y_1}{y_2}\)
Constant ratio:
\(\frac{y}{x}=k\), or \(y=kx\)
Find an unknown value:
If \(\frac{x_1}{y_1}=\frac{x_2}{y_2}\), then use cross multiplication to find the unknown.
Unitary method:
Value of 1 unit \(=\frac{\text{Total value}}{\text{Number of units}}\)
Value of \(n\) units \(=\text{Value of 1 unit}\times n\)

6. Inverse Proportion

Inverse proportion:
\(x_1y_1=x_2y_2\)
Constant product:
\(xy=k\), or \(y=\frac{k}{x}\), where \(x\neq0\).
Workers and time:
\(W_1T_1=W_2T_2\)
Assuming the work and each worker's rate remain the same.
Taps and filling time:
\(N_1T_1=N_2T_2\)
Assuming identical taps and the same tank.
Speed and time for a fixed distance:
\(v_1t_1=v_2t_2=d\)

7. Distance, Speed and Time

Speed:
\(\text{Speed}=\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}\)
Distance:
\(\text{Distance}=\text{Speed}\times\text{Time}\)
Time:
\(\text{Time}=\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}\)
Average speed:
\(\text{Average speed}=\frac{\text{Total distance}}{\text{Total time}}\)
Fuel efficiency:
\(\text{Distance per litre}=\frac{\text{Distance travelled}}{\text{Fuel used}}\)

8. Unit Conversions

\(1\text{ km}=1000\text{ m}\)
\(1\text{ hour}=60\text{ minutes}=3600\text{ seconds}\)
\(1\text{ minute}=60\text{ seconds}\)
Convert km/h to m/s:
\(\text{m/s}=\text{km/h}\times\frac{5}{18}\)
Convert m/s to km/h:
\(\text{km/h}=\text{m/s}\times\frac{18}{5}\)
Convert decimal hours to minutes:
\(\text{Minutes}=\text{Decimal part of hours}\times60\)

9. 12-hour and 24-hour Clock

12-hour to 24-hour clock:
For p.m. times other than 12 p.m., add 12 to the hour.
12 a.m. \(=00{:}00\)
12 p.m. \(=12{:}00\)
24-hour to 12-hour clock:
For hours greater than 12, subtract 12 and use p.m.
00:00 \(=12{:}00\) a.m. (midnight)
12:00 \(=12{:}00\) p.m. (noon)

10. Journey Time Calculations

Arrival time:
Arrival time \(=\) Departure time \(+\) Journey time
Journey time:
Journey time \(=\) Arrival time \(-\) Departure time
Departure time:
Departure time \(=\) Arrival time \(-\) Journey time
Time across midnight:
If a journey crosses midnight, continue counting from 00:00 on the next day.

CONCEPTS

1. Ratio
A ratio compares two or more quantities of the same kind. Convert quantities into the same units before comparing them. A ratio has no unit.
2. Ratio in Simplest Form
A ratio is in simplest form when its integer terms have no common factor other than 1.
3. Dividing a Quantity in a Ratio
Add the ratio terms. Divide the total quantity by their sum to find the value of one part, then multiply by each ratio term.
4. Rate
A rate compares quantities with different units, such as rupees per hour, words per minute, or kilometres per hour. A rate includes units.
5. Proportion
A proportion states that two ratios are equivalent. Unknown values can be found using cross multiplication.
6. Direct Proportion
Two quantities are directly proportional when their ratio remains constant. If one quantity doubles, the other doubles; if one halves, the other halves.
7. Unitary Method
Find the value of one unit first, then multiply or divide to find the required quantity.
8. Inverse Proportion
Two quantities are inversely proportional when their product remains constant. As one increases, the other decreases in the corresponding ratio.
9. Average Speed
Average speed is calculated using total distance divided by total time, not by simply averaging the speeds of different parts of a journey.
10. Speed, Distance and Time
For a constant speed, distance increases with time. For a fixed distance, speed and journey time are inversely proportional.
11. 12-hour Clock
The 12-hour clock uses a.m. for times before noon and p.m. for times after noon. Midnight is 12:00 a.m.; noon is 12:00 p.m.
12. 24-hour Clock
The 24-hour clock uses hours from 00 to 23 and minutes from 00 to 59. For example, 21:15 represents 9:15 p.m.
13. Journey Calculations
Use the departure time and journey duration to find the arrival time. When subtracting times, borrow 1 hour as 60 minutes when needed.

DEFINITIONS

1. Ratio:
A ratio is a comparison of two or more quantities of the same kind, measured in the same units.
2. Equivalent Ratios:
Equivalent ratios are ratios that represent the same relationship between quantities.
3. Rate:
A rate is a comparison of two quantities with different kinds of units.
4. Unit Rate:
A unit rate is a rate expressed for one unit of a quantity.
5. Proportion:
A proportion is an equation stating that two ratios or rates are equivalent.
6. Cross Products:
The products obtained by multiplying the numerator of each ratio by the denominator of the other ratio are called cross products.
7. Direct Proportion:
Direct proportion is a relationship between two quantities in which their ratio remains constant.
8. Inverse Proportion:
Inverse proportion is a relationship between two quantities in which their product remains constant.
9. Speed:
Speed is the distance travelled per unit of time.
10. Constant or Uniform Speed:
An object moves at constant speed when it covers equal distances in equal intervals of time.
11. Average Speed:
Average speed is the total distance travelled divided by the total time taken.
12. 12-hour Clock:
A time system that divides the day into two periods of 12 hours, identified by a.m. and p.m.
13. 24-hour Clock:
A time system that represents the hours of a day from 00:00 to 23:59.

KEY POINTS

1. Always convert quantities into the same units before writing a ratio.
2. Ratios have no units, but rates include units.
3. To divide a quantity in a ratio, add all ratio terms before calculating each share.
4. In a proportion, the cross products are equal: \(ad=bc\).
5. Direct proportion has a constant ratio: \(\frac{y}{x}=k\).
6. Inverse proportion has a constant product: \(xy=k\).
7. More identical workers generally need less time to complete the same fixed job, assuming equal work rates.
8. For a fixed distance, increasing speed decreases travel time.
9. Speed = Distance ÷ Time.
10. Distance = Speed × Time.
11. Time = Distance ÷ Speed.
12. Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time.
13. To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by \(\frac{5}{18}\). To convert m/s to km/h, multiply by \(\frac{18}{5}\).
14. One hour has 60 minutes and 3,600 seconds.
15. In the 24-hour clock, 00:00 is midnight and 12:00 is noon.
16. When a journey passes midnight, remember to include the next day in the calculation.
