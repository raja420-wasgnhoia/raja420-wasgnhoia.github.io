FORMULAS & RULES

1. Divisibility Rules

Divisible by 2: Last digit is 0, 2, 4, 6 or 8.
Divisible by 3: Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
Divisible by 4: Last two digits form a number divisible by 4.
Divisible by 5: Last digit is 0 or 5.
Divisible by 6: Number is divisible by both 2 and 3.
Divisible by 8: Last three digits form a number divisible by 8.
Divisible by 9: Sum of digits is divisible by 9.
Divisible by 10: Last digit is 0.
Divisible by 11: Difference between the sums of alternate digits is 0 or a multiple of 11.

2. Prime Factorization

Prime factorization: Expressing a composite number as a product of prime factors.
Index notation: 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2² × 3²
Methods: Factor Tree and Division Method.

3. HCF (Highest Common Factor)

HCF = Highest Common Factor
HCF is also called GCD (Greatest Common Divisor).
Prime Factorization Method: HCF = Product of common prime factors, using the smallest powers.
Division Method: Repeatedly divide; the last non-zero divisor is the HCF.
Use HCF to find the greatest number that exactly divides given numbers.

4. LCM (Least Common Multiple)

LCM = Least Common Multiple
Prime Factorization Method: LCM = Product of all prime factors, using the greatest power of each.
Division Method: LCM = Product of all divisors × product of remaining numbers.
Use LCM to find the smallest common multiple, shortest equal length, or when events happen together again.

5. Relation Between HCF and LCM

First Number × Second Number = HCF × LCM
LCM = (Product of the two numbers) ÷ HCF
HCF = (Product of the two numbers) ÷ LCM
Second Number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ First Number

CONCEPTS

1. Factors

A factor divides a given number exactly without leaving a remainder.

Every number is a factor of itself, and 1 is a factor of every whole number.

A positive factor is never greater than the number itself.

When listing factor pairs in order, stop when the pairs begin to repeat.

2. Multiples

A multiple is the product of a number and a counting number.

A number is a factor of each of its multiples.

Positive multiples of a number are equal to or greater than that number and continue infinitely.

3. Relationship Between Factors and Multiples

If a divides b exactly, then a is a factor of b, and b is a multiple of a.

4. Prime Factorization

A composite number can be expressed as a product of prime factors.

Repeated prime factors can be written using powers (index notation).

5. HCF and LCM

Common factors are factors shared by two or more numbers.

HCF is the greatest factor common to the given numbers.

LCM is the smallest positive multiple common to the given numbers.

DEFINITIONS

Factor: A number that divides another number exactly without a remainder.

Multiple: A number obtained by multiplying a given number by a counting number.

Prime Number: A number greater than 1 that has exactly two positive factors: 1 and itself.

Composite Number: A number greater than 1 that has more than two positive factors.

Prime Factors: Prime numbers that divide a given number exactly.

Prime Factorization: Expressing a composite number as a product of prime numbers.

Co-prime Numbers: Two numbers whose HCF is 1.

Common Factors: Factors shared by two or more numbers.

HCF: The greatest factor common to two or more numbers.

GCD: Greatest Common Divisor; another name for HCF.

LCM: The smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or more given numbers.

KEY POINTS

  • Factors of a positive whole number are finite, while its positive multiples are infinite.
  • Every number is a factor of itself, and 1 is a factor of every whole number.
  • A number is divisible by 6 only when it is divisible by both 2 and 3.
  • Test for divisibility by 7 is not included in these notes.
  • The product of two prime numbers greater than 1 is composite.
  • The sum of two prime numbers may be prime or composite.
  • Example of co-primes: 10 and 17, because their HCF is 1.
  • For HCF by prime factorization, use the common prime factors with the smallest powers.
  • For LCM by prime factorization, use every prime factor with the greatest power.
  • HCF is useful for greatest equal grouping or exact division.
  • LCM is useful for finding when repeating events happen together again.
  • For two positive integers: First Number × Second Number = HCF × LCM.
