FORMULAS & RULES

1. Similar Figures

Similarity symbol: ∼
If figures A and B are similar: A ∼ B
For similar figures:
Corresponding angles are equal.
Corresponding sides are proportional.
For similar triangles:
∠A = ∠D, ∠B = ∠E, ∠C = ∠F
AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF

2. Congruent Figures

Congruence symbols: ≅ or ≡
If figures A and B are congruent: A ≅ B
For congruent figures:
Corresponding sides are equal and corresponding angles are equal.
All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures are not necessarily congruent.

3. Triangle Congruency Tests

SSS (Side-Side-Side)
If all three corresponding sides are equal, the triangles are congruent.
SAS (Side-Angle-Side)
If two corresponding sides and their included angle are equal, the triangles are congruent.
ASA (Angle-Side-Angle)
If two corresponding angles and their included side are equal, the triangles are congruent.
HS (Hypotenuse-Side)
For right-angled triangles, equal hypotenuses and one corresponding side establish congruence.
Corresponding sides:
AB ↔ DE, BC ↔ EF, AC ↔ DF
Corresponding angles:
∠A ↔ ∠D, ∠B ↔ ∠E, ∠C ↔ ∠F

4. Circle: Arc Length

Circumference of a circle:
C = 2πr
Arc length:
L = (x/360°) × 2πr
r = radius, x = central angle in degrees.

5. Sector of a Circle

Area of a circle:
A = πr²
Area of a sector:
A = (x/360°) × πr²
r = radius, x = central angle in degrees.
Minor sector: central angle < 180°
Major sector: central angle > 180°
Quadrant: central angle = 90°

6. Chords and Intersecting Chords

If two chords intersect inside a circle:
PA × PB = PC × PD

7. Angles in a Semicircle

Angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference:
∠APB = 90°
The angle in a semicircle is half the central angle standing on the same arc:
Angle at circumference = ½ × central angle

8. Angles in the Same Segment

Angles standing on the same chord in the same segment are equal:
∠P = ∠Q = ∠R

CONCEPTS

1. Similar and Congruent Figures

Similar figures have the same shape, but their sizes may differ. Congruent figures have the same shape and size.

Congruent figures may have different positions or orientations.

2. Corresponding Parts

Corresponding sides and angles are the matching parts of two figures. The order of vertices shows which parts correspond.

3. Triangle Congruency

SSS, SAS, ASA and HS are tests used to establish whether two triangles are congruent.

In SAS, the angle must be included between the two sides. In HS, both triangles must be right-angled.

4. Parts of a Circle

An arc is part of a circle's circumference. A diameter divides a circle into two semicircles.

A central angle has its vertex at the centre of the circle.

5. Arc Length and Sector Area

Arc length is the corresponding fraction of the circle's circumference. Sector area is the corresponding fraction of the circle's total area.

The fraction is determined by the central angle divided by 360°.

6. Chords, Secants and Tangents

A chord is a line segment with both endpoints on a circle. A secant is a line that intersects a circle at two points. A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point.

7. Concentric Circles and Concyclic Points

Concentric circles have the same centre but different radii. Concyclic points lie on the same circle.

8. Circle Angle Properties

An angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference is a right angle.

Angles standing on the same chord in the same segment are equal.

DEFINITIONS

1. Similar Figures

Figures having the same shape but not necessarily the same size are called similar figures.

2. Congruent Figures

Figures having the same shape and size are called congruent figures.

3. Corresponding Sides

Matching sides of two figures are called corresponding sides.

4. Corresponding Angles

Matching angles of two figures are called corresponding angles.

5. Minor Arc

The shorter arc joining two points on a circle is called the minor arc.

6. Major Arc

The longer arc joining two points on a circle is called the major arc.

7. Semicircle

Each half of a circle divided by a diameter is called a semicircle.

8. Central Angle

An angle subtended by an arc at the centre of a circle is called a central angle.

9. Arc Length

The distance measured along an arc of a circle is called arc length.

10. Sector

A region enclosed by two radii and an arc is called a sector.

11. Minor Sector

A sector smaller than half of the circular region is called a minor sector.

12. Major Sector

A sector larger than half of the circular region is called a major sector.

13. Quadrant

A sector with a central angle of 90° is called a quadrant.

14. Segment of a Circle

A region bounded by a chord and its corresponding arc is called a segment of a circle.

15. Secant

A line that intersects a circle at two points is called a secant.

16. Chord

A line segment whose endpoints lie on a circle is called a chord.

17. Tangent

A line that touches a circle at exactly one point is called a tangent.

18. Point of Tangency

The point where a tangent touches a circle is called the point of tangency.

19. Concyclic Points

Points that lie on the same circle are called concyclic points.

20. Concentric Circles

Two or more circles having the same centre are called concentric circles.

KEY POINTS

  • Similar figures have the same shape; their sizes may differ.
  • Congruent figures have the same shape and size.
  • All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures are not necessarily congruent.
  • Corresponding angles of similar figures are equal.
  • Corresponding sides of similar figures are proportional.
  • SSS, SAS, ASA and HS are triangle congruency tests.
  • HS applies only to right-angled triangles.
  • Congruent figures have equal corresponding sides, equal corresponding angles and equal areas.
  • A diameter divides a circle into two semicircles.
  • A minor arc is shorter than a major arc between the same endpoints.
  • Arc length uses the fraction x/360° of the circumference.
  • Sector area uses the fraction x/360° of the circle's area.
  • A quadrant is one-fourth of a circle.
  • A chord joins two points on a circle; a secant intersects the circle at two points.
  • A tangent touches a circle at exactly one point.
  • Intersecting chords satisfy PA × PB = PC × PD.
  • The angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference is 90°.
  • Angles in the same segment standing on the same chord are equal.
  • Concentric circles have the same centre; concyclic points lie on the same circle.
