FORMULAS & RULES

1. Squares and Square Roots

Square of a number:
\(a^2=a\times a\)
Square root:
\(\sqrt{a^2}=a\), for \(a\geq0\)
Square root of a perfect square:
\(\sqrt{81}=9\)
\(\sqrt{121}=11\)
\(\sqrt{400}=20\)
Positive and negative square roots:
If \(x^2=a\), then \(x=\pm\sqrt{a}\), for \(a\geq0\).
Principal square root: \(\sqrt{16}=4\)
Solutions of \(x^2=16\): \(x=\pm4\)

2. Even and Odd Numbers

Even number: \(2n\)
Odd number: \(2n+1\)
Here, \(n\) is an integer.
Addition rules:
Even + Even = Even
Odd + Odd = Even
Even + Odd = Odd
Multiplication rules:
Even × Even = Even
Odd × Odd = Odd
Even × Odd = Even
Squares of even and odd numbers:
Even² = Even
Odd² = Odd

3. Prime Factorization and Perfect Squares

Prime factorization:
Express a number as a product of prime factors.
Square root by factorization:
1. Find the prime factors.
2. Group equal factors into pairs.
3. Take one factor from each pair and multiply.
Perfect square test:
A number is a perfect square if every prime factor occurs in pairs, or equivalently, every prime factor has an even exponent.
Square root by prime factorization:
\(\sqrt{a^2b^2c^2}=abc\), for non-negative \(a,b,c\).
Product of perfect squares:
The product of two perfect squares is a perfect square.

4. Square Roots of Fractions

Square of a fraction:
\(\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2=\frac{a^2}{b^2}\), \(b\neq0\)
Square root of a common fraction:
\(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}\)
For real principal square roots, \(a\geq0\) and \(b>0\).
Steps for a mixed fraction:
Convert the mixed fraction into an improper fraction before finding its square root.

5. Square Roots of Decimal Fractions

Method:
Convert the decimal fraction into a common fraction, find its square root, and convert the result into decimal form.
Square root of a decimal:
\(\sqrt{1.69}=1.3\)
\(\sqrt{0.0144}=0.12\)

6. Square and Area Rules

Area of a square:
\(A=s^2\)
Side of a square:
\(s=\sqrt A\)
Perimeter of a square:
\(P=4s\)
Rectangle area:
\(A=l\times w\)
Rectangle perimeter:
\(P=2(l+w)\)

7. Important Square Root Properties

Product property:
\(\sqrt a\times\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}\)
For \(a,b\geq0\).
Quotient property:
\(\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}=\sqrt{\frac ab}\)
For \(a\geq0\) and \(b>0\).
Important warning:
\(\sqrt{a+b}\neq\sqrt a+\sqrt b\) in general.

CONCEPTS

1. Square and Square Root
Squaring means multiplying a number by itself. A square root is a number that, when squared, gives the original number.
2. Even and Odd Numbers
Even numbers are divisible by 2 without a remainder. Odd numbers are not divisible by 2 without a remainder.
3. Prime Numbers
A prime number has exactly two distinct positive factors: 1 and itself. The number 2 is the smallest prime number and the only even prime number.
4. Sieve Method
The Sieve of Eratosthenes is a method for finding prime numbers by crossing out multiples of prime numbers, starting with 2.
5. Composite Numbers
A composite number has more than two positive factors. Every natural number greater than 1 is either prime or composite.
6. Prime Factorization
Prime factorization expresses a number as a product of prime numbers.
7. Perfect Squares
A perfect square is a number whose principal square root is a whole number. In prime factorization, all prime exponents of a perfect square are even.
8. Square Root by Factorization
Pair identical prime factors. Multiply one factor from each pair to find the positive square root.
9. Square Roots of Fractions
Find the square roots of the numerator and denominator separately when both are suitable perfect squares. Convert mixed fractions into improper fractions first.
10. Square Roots of Decimals
Convert a decimal fraction into a common fraction, simplify its square root, and express the result in decimal form.
11. Square Root and Area
The side of a square can be found by taking the square root of its area. Its perimeter is four times the side length.
12. Square Root Properties
Square roots can be combined using product and quotient properties under the appropriate conditions. They cannot generally be distributed over addition or subtraction.

DEFINITIONS

1. Square:
The square of a number is the product of the number multiplied by itself.
2. Square Root:
A square root of a number is a number which, when squared, gives that number.
3. Even Number:
An even number is an integer divisible by 2 without a remainder.
4. Odd Number:
An odd number is an integer that is not divisible by 2 without a remainder.
5. Prime Number:
A prime number is a natural number greater than 1 that has exactly two distinct positive factors, 1 and itself.
6. Composite Number:
A composite number is a natural number greater than 1 that has more than two positive factors.
7. Prime Factorization:
Prime factorization is the expression of a number as a product of prime factors.
8. Perfect Square:
A perfect square is a non-negative integer whose square root is an integer.
9. Square Root by Factorization:
It is a method in which prime factors are paired, and one factor from each pair is multiplied to obtain the positive square root.
10. Square Root of a Common Fraction:
The square root of a suitable non-negative fraction is found by taking the square roots of its numerator and denominator separately.
11. Square Root of a Decimal Fraction:
It is found by converting the decimal fraction into a common fraction, calculating the square root, and converting the result back into decimal form.

KEY POINTS

1. The square of an even integer is even, and the square of an odd integer is odd.
2. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.
3. The number 2 is the only even prime number.
4. A perfect square has an even exponent for every prime factor in its prime factorization.
5. When finding a square root by factorization, pair equal prime factors and take one factor from each pair.
6. The symbol \(\sqrt a\) represents the non-negative, or principal, square root of \(a\).
7. The equation \(x^2=a\), for \(a>0\), has two real solutions: \(x=\sqrt a\) and \(x=-\sqrt a\).
8. A mixed fraction must be converted into an improper fraction before finding its square root.
9. For a square with area \(A\), its side is \(\sqrt A\), and its perimeter is \(4\sqrt A\).
10. For non-negative numbers, \(\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}\).
11. Square roots do not generally distribute over addition or subtraction.
12. The square of a positive proper fraction is smaller than the fraction itself.
13. The square of a decimal number between 0 and 1 is smaller than the original number.
