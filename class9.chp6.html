1. FORMULAS

Angle Measurement

1° = 60′; 1′ = 60″; 1° = 3600″
1′ = 1/60°; 1″ = 1/60′ = 1/3600°
360° = 2π radians
180° = π radians
Radians to degrees: θ° = θ × 180/π
Degrees to radians: θ radians = θ° × π/180
1 radian ≈ 57.2958°

Arc Length and Sector Area

Arc length: l = rθ, where θ is in radians
Area of sector: A = ½r²θ, where θ is in radians
l/(2πr) = θ/(2π) = A/(πr²)

Co-terminal Angles

θ + 360°n, where n ∈ ℤ
In radians: θ + 2πn, where n ∈ ℤ

Basic Trigonometric Ratios

sin θ = Perpendicular / Hypotenuse = a/c
cos θ = Base / Hypotenuse = b/c
tan θ = Perpendicular / Base = a/b
cosec θ = 1/sin θ = c/a
sec θ = 1/cos θ = c/b
cot θ = 1/tan θ = cos θ/sin θ = b/a

Unit Circle

x² + y² = 1
Coordinates: P = (cos θ, sin θ)
sin θ = y; cos θ = x; tan θ = y/x

Standard Angles

30°: sin = 1/2; cos = √3/2; tan = 1/√3; cosec = 2; sec = 2/√3; cot = √3
45°: sin = √2/2; cos = √2/2; tan = 1; cosec = √2; sec = √2; cot = 1
60°: sin = √3/2; cos = 1/2; tan = √3; cosec = 2/√3; sec = 2; cot = 1/√3

Signs of Ratios: ASTC Rule

Quadrant I: All six ratios are positive.
Quadrant II: sin θ and cosec θ are positive.
Quadrant III: tan θ and cot θ are positive.
Quadrant IV: cos θ and sec θ are positive.

Quadrantal Angles

0°: sin = 0; cos = 1; tan = 0; sec = 1; cosec = Undefined; cot = Undefined
90°: sin = 1; cos = 0; tan = Undefined; sec = Undefined; cosec = 1; cot = 0
180°: sin = 0; cos = −1; tan = 0; sec = −1; cosec = Undefined; cot = Undefined
270°: sin = −1; cos = 0; tan = Undefined; sec = Undefined; cosec = −1; cot = 0
360°: sin = 0; cos = 1; tan = 0; sec = 1; cosec = Undefined; cot = Undefined

Finding Other Ratios

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
tan θ = sin θ/cos θ
cot θ = cos θ/sin θ
sec θ = 1/cos θ
cosec θ = 1/sin θ
cot θ = 1/tan θ
Hypotenuse² = Perpendicular² + Base²

Quotient Identities

tan θ = sin θ/cos θ
cot θ = cos θ/sin θ

Pythagorean Identities

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
sec²θ − tan²θ = 1
cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ
1 − sin²θ = cos²θ
1 − cos²θ = sin²θ
1/(sec θ − tan θ) = sec θ + tan θ

Solving Right Triangles

∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°
If ∠C = 90°, then ∠A + ∠B = 90°
c² = a² + b²
sin θ = Perpendicular/Hypotenuse
cos θ = Base/Hypotenuse
tan θ = Perpendicular/Base

Angle of Elevation and Depression

tan θ = Vertical height / Horizontal distance
sin θ = Opposite side / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent side / Hypotenuse

Bearings

Bearings are measured clockwise from north.
Bearings are written using three digits, e.g. 070°, 128°, 230°.
Reverse bearing = Forward bearing ± 180°, adjusted to 000°–359°.
For a right triangle: AC = √(AB² + BC²)

2. CONCEPTS

Angle Measurement

Positive angles are measured anticlockwise and negative angles clockwise. An angle in standard position has its vertex at the origin and its initial arm along the positive x-axis.

Arc Length and Sector

Arc length is the length of a part of a circle's circumference. A sector is the region bounded by an arc and two radii.

Co-terminal Angles

Co-terminal angles have the same initial and terminal arms. They can be found by adding or subtracting multiples of 360°.

Unit Circle

The unit circle has radius 1 and centre at the origin. A point at angle θ has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ).

Quadrantal Angles

A quadrantal angle has its terminal arm on a coordinate axis. Its measure is a multiple of 90° or π/2 radians.

Trigonometric Identities

Identities are equations involving trigonometric ratios that hold wherever both sides are defined. Quotient identities relate tangent and cotangent to sine and cosine. Pythagorean identities follow from the Pythagorean theorem.

Solving Right Triangles

Solving a right triangle means finding its unknown sides and angles using the angle sum, Pythagorean theorem and trigonometric ratios.

Angle of Depression

The angle of depression is measured from the observer's horizontal line down to the line of sight toward an object below it.

Bearings

A bearing describes direction as a clockwise angle from north and is normally written as a three-digit number.

3. DEFINITIONS

Angle: Two rays with a common endpoint.

Standard position: An angle whose vertex is at the origin and whose initial arm lies along the positive x-axis.

Radian: The central angle subtended by an arc whose length equals the radius.

Sector: A region of a circle bounded by an arc and two radii.

Co-terminal angles: Angles in standard position with the same terminal ray.

Quadrantal angle: An angle whose terminal ray lies on a coordinate axis.

Unit circle: A circle of radius 1 centred at the origin, with equation x² + y² = 1.

Trigonometric ratios: Ratios relating the sides of a right triangle to an angle.

Trigonometric identity: An equality involving trigonometric expressions that is true wherever both sides are defined.

Angle of depression: The angle between the observer's horizontal line and the line of sight to an object below it.

Bearing: The clockwise angle measured from due north to a direction, usually written as a three-digit number.

4. KEY POINTS

  • 360° = 2π radians and 180° = π radians.
  • Use radians in l = rθ and A = ½r²θ.
  • Co-terminal angles differ by multiples of 360°.
  • On the unit circle, x = cos θ and y = sin θ.
  • ASTC: All, Sine, Tangent, Cosine show which ratios are positive in quadrants I–IV.
  • A ratio with zero in its denominator is undefined, not infinity.
  • The two acute angles of a right triangle add up to 90°.
  • When proving identities, simplify one side until it matches the other side.
  • Angles of elevation and depression are measured from a horizontal line.
  • Bearings are measured clockwise from north and written with three digits.
  • The reverse bearing differs by 180°, adjusted to 000°–359°.

5. APPLICATION

  • Angle measurement: Converting between degrees and radians.
  • Arc length and sector area: Finding curved lengths and sector areas.
  • Trigonometric ratios: Finding unknown sides and angles of right triangles.
  • Elevation and depression: Estimating heights and distances involving buildings, ladders, aircraft and objects below an observer.
  • Bearings: Describing directions and planning routes for ships, aircraft and journeys.
