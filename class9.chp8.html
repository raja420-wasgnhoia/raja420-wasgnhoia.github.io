1. FORMULAS

Gradient (Slope)

Gradient Using Inclination:
m = tan θ
Gradient as Rise over Run:
m = vertical distance / horizontal distance
m = rise / run
Gradient Through Two Points:
For (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂):
m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)
m = (y₁ - y₂) / (x₁ - x₂)

Horizontal and Vertical Lines

Horizontal Line:
θ = 0°
m = tan 0° = 0
Vertical Line:
θ = 90°
m = tan 90° = undefined

Sign of Gradient

Positive Gradient:
0° < α < 90°
m = tan α > 0
Negative Gradient:
90° < β < 180°
m = tan β < 0

Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

Parallel Lines:
m₁ = m₂
Perpendicular Lines:
m₁ = -1/m₂
m₂ = -1/m₁
m₁ × m₂ = -1
Collinear Points Using Gradients:
Gradient of AB = Gradient of BC = Gradient of AC

Equations of a Straight Line

Slope-Intercept Form:
y = mx + c
Line Through Origin:
y = mx, when c = 0
Point-Slope Form:
y - y₁ = m(x - x₁)
Two-Point Form:
y - y₁ = [(y₂ - y₁)/(x₂ - x₁)](x - x₁)
Two-Point Form (Alternate):
(y - y₁)/(y₂ - y₁) = (x - x₁)/(x₂ - x₁)
Two-Intercept Form:
x/a + y/b = 1

Two-Intercept Form: Related Formulas

m = -b/a
bx + ay = ab

Symmetric Form

m = tan α
tan α = (y - y₁)/(x - x₁)
(x - x₁)/cos α = (y - y₁)/sin α

Normal Form

Equation of a Line in Normal Form:
x cos α + y sin α = p
Intercept Relations:
OA = p/cos α
OB = p/sin α
Normal Distance from General Form:
For ax + by + c = 0:
p = -c / (±√(a² + b²))
Choose the sign so that p is positive.

General Form of a Straight Line

General Equation:
ax + by + c = 0
a, b, c are real numbers and a, b cannot both be zero.
Slope from General Form:
m = -a/b, when b ≠ 0
Y-Intercept:
y-intercept = -c/b, when b ≠ 0
X-Intercept:
x-intercept = -c/a, when a ≠ 0
Horizontal Line Case:
If a = 0 and b ≠ 0:
by + c = 0
y = -c/b
Vertical Line Case:
If b = 0 and a ≠ 0:
ax + c = 0
x = -c/a
Slope-Intercept Conversion:
y = -(a/b)x - c/b, when b ≠ 0
Point-Slope Conversion:
Using point (0, -c/b) and slope -a/b:
y + c/b = -(a/b)x
Two-Intercept Conversion:
x/(-c/a) + y/(-c/b) = 1
Applicable when both intercepts are defined and non-zero.
Symmetric Conversion:
m = tan θ = -a/b
cos θ = b/√(a² + b²)
sin θ = a/√(a² + b²)
Normal Form Conversion:
ax/√(a² + b²) + by/√(a² + b²) = -c/√(a² + b²)
Choose an overall sign that makes the right-hand side positive.

Angle Between Two Intersecting Lines

m₁ = tan α
m₂ = tan β
Angle Difference:
θ = α - β
Angle Formula:
tan θ = tan(α - β)
tan θ = (m₁ - m₂)/(1 + m₁m₂)
Parallel Lines Condition:
m₁ = m₂
Perpendicular Lines Condition:
1 + m₁m₂ = 0
m₁m₂ = -1

Point of Intersection of Two Lines

Two General Equations:
a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0
a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0
Determinant Condition:
D = a₁b₂ - a₂b₁
For a unique intersection, D ≠ 0.
X-Coordinate of Intersection:
x = (b₁c₂ - b₂c₁)/(a₁b₂ - a₂b₁)
Y-Coordinate of Intersection:
y = (a₂c₁ - a₁c₂)/(a₁b₂ - a₂b₁)
Point of Intersection:
P = ((b₁c₂ - b₂c₁)/D, (a₂c₁ - a₁c₂)/D)

Family of Lines

Family Through the Intersection of Two Lines:
L₁ + kL₂ = 0
a₁x + b₁y + c₁ + k(a₂x + b₂y + c₂) = 0
Gradient of the Family:
m = -(a₁ + ka₂)/(b₁ + kb₂), when b₁ + kb₂ ≠ 0

Real-World Linear Models

General Linear Model:
y = mx + c
Bus Fare Model from the Notes:
y = 1.12x + 8
Fruit Cost Model from the Notes:
150x + 80y = 620
Two-Point Form for a Changing Quantity:
(p - p₁)/(p₂ - p₁) = (t - t₁)/(t₂ - t₁)

2. CONCEPTS

Inclination of a Straight Line:
The inclination θ is the angle measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis to the line.
Types of Lines:
Horizontal line: parallel to the x-axis.
Vertical line: parallel to the y-axis.
Oblique line: neither horizontal nor vertical.
Gradient:
Gradient describes the steepness and direction of a straight line. It is the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.
Positive Gradient:
The line rises from left to right.
Negative Gradient:
The line falls from left to right.
Parallel Lines:
Two non-vertical parallel lines have equal gradients. Equal gradients also indicate parallel lines when the lines are distinct.
Perpendicular Lines:
Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular when their gradients are negative reciprocals of each other.
Two-Intercept Form:
If a line has x-intercept a and y-intercept b, it can be represented by x/a + y/b = 1, provided the intercepts are non-zero.
Symmetric Form:
This form describes a line using a point on the line and its direction, represented through the inclination angle.
Normal Form:
The normal form uses p, the perpendicular distance from the origin to the line, and α, the inclination of the perpendicular.
General Form:
The equation ax + by + c = 0 represents a straight line, where a and b are not both zero.
Reduction of General Form:
The general equation can be converted into other standard forms when their required denominators and intercepts are defined.
Angle Between Lines:
The angle between two lines can be determined from their gradients and the tangent-difference formula.
Point of Intersection:
Two non-parallel, distinct straight lines intersect at exactly one point.
Family of Lines:
Infinitely many lines can pass through the intersection of two lines. A parameter k selects a line from this family.
Linear Models:
A linear equation can represent relationships between quantities such as distance and fare, amounts and cost, or time and pollution.

3. DEFINITIONS

Inclination:
The angle measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis to a straight line.
Gradient (Slope):
The ratio of the vertical change to the horizontal change between two points on a straight line.
Horizontal Line:
A straight line parallel to the x-axis with gradient zero.
Vertical Line:
A straight line parallel to the y-axis whose gradient is undefined.
Oblique Line:
A straight line that is neither horizontal nor vertical.
Slope-Intercept Form:
The equation y = mx + c, where m is the slope and c is the y-intercept.
Point-Slope Form:
The equation of a line expressed using its slope and one known point.
Two-Point Form:
The equation of a line expressed using two known points on it.
Two-Intercept Form:
The equation of a line expressed in terms of its x-intercept and y-intercept.
Symmetric Form:
A form of a straight-line equation using a point on the line and its direction ratios or direction angles.
Normal Form:
The equation x cos α + y sin α = p, where p is the perpendicular distance from the origin to the line.
General Form:
The equation ax + by + c = 0, where a, b and c are real constants and a and b cannot both be zero.
X-Intercept:
The x-coordinate where a line crosses the x-axis.
Y-Intercept:
The y-coordinate where a line crosses the y-axis.
Point of Intersection:
The point shared by two intersecting lines.
Family of Lines:
A collection of lines represented by an equation containing a parameter.
Linear Model:
A mathematical equation representing a relationship between quantities using a straight line.

4. KEY POINTS

  • The inclination is measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis.
  • The inclination of a horizontal line is 0°.
  • The inclination of a vertical line is 90°.
  • The gradient of a horizontal line is zero.
  • The gradient of a vertical line is undefined.
  • A positive gradient means the line rises from left to right.
  • A negative gradient means the line falls from left to right.
  • Gradient is calculated as rise divided by run.
  • Parallel non-vertical lines have equal gradients.
  • Perpendicular non-vertical lines have gradients whose product is -1.
  • Collinear points have equal gradients between pairs, provided the relevant gradients are defined.
  • The slope-intercept form is y = mx + c.
  • The line passes through the origin when c = 0 in y = mx + c.
  • The general form is ax + by + c = 0.
  • In the general form, a and b cannot both be zero.
  • For a line with b ≠ 0, its slope is -a/b and its y-intercept is -c/b.
  • For a line with a ≠ 0, its x-intercept is -c/a.
  • Two non-parallel, distinct lines intersect at one point.
  • Parallel distinct lines do not intersect.
  • Infinitely many lines can pass through a single point.
  • A family-of-lines equation uses a parameter to represent different lines through a common point.
  • The normal form uses a positive perpendicular distance p from the origin.
  • Gradient in a pollution model represents the rate of change of pollution over time.
  • The y-intercept in a linear model represents the value of y when x = 0.

5. APPLICATION

Transport Fare:
A linear equation can represent the relationship between travel distance and the total fare, including a fixed starting charge.
Shopping Costs:
A linear equation can relate the quantities of different products to a total budget.
Pollution Monitoring:
A straight-line model can estimate how pollution changes over time. Its gradient represents the rate of change, while its y-intercept gives the model's starting value.
Roads and Ramps:
Gradient helps describe the steepness of roads, ramps and other sloping surfaces.
Intersecting Routes:
Equations of straight lines can be used to calculate the intersection point of two routes represented on a coordinate plane.
