FORMULAS & RULES

1. Pythagoras Theorem

Hypotenuse² = Base² + Perpendicular²
c² = a² + b²
Unknown hypotenuse:
c = √(a² + b²)
Unknown base:
a = √(c² − b²)
Unknown perpendicular:
b = √(c² − a²)
Right triangle condition:
a² + b² = c²
Acute triangle condition (c is the longest side):
a² + b² > c²
Obtuse triangle condition (c is the longest side):
a² + b² < c²
Triangle inequality:
a + b > c

2. Pyramid

Total Surface Area of a Pyramid:
TSA = Base Area + Lateral Surface Area
Regular Pyramid:
TSA = B + ½Pl
Lateral Surface Area of a Regular Pyramid:
LSA = ½Pl
Triangular Base Area:
B = ½bh
Volume of a Pyramid:
V = ⅓Bh

3. Sphere and Hemisphere

Diameter and Radius:
d = 2r
r = d ÷ 2
Surface Area of a Sphere:
SA = 4πr²
Curved Surface Area of an Open Hemisphere:
CSA = 2πr²
Total Surface Area of a Closed Hemisphere:
TSA = 3πr²
Volume of a Sphere:
V = ⁴⁄₃πr³
Volume of a Hemisphere:
V = ⅔πr³
Ratio of Surface Areas of Two Spheres:
SA₁ / SA₂ = (r₁ / r₂)²
Radius Ratio from Surface Area Ratio:
r₁ / r₂ = √(SA₁ / SA₂)
When n Equal Small Spheres Form One Large Sphere:
n × ⁴⁄₃πr³ = ⁴⁄₃πR³
R³ = nr³

4. Right Circular Cone

Pythagoras Relation:
l² = r² + h²
Slant Height:
l = √(r² + h²)
Perpendicular Height:
h = √(l² − r²)
Radius:
r = √(l² − h²)
Diameter of the Base:
d = 2r
Circumference of the Circular Base:
C = 2πr
Area of the Circular Base:
B = πr²
Curved Surface Area of a Cone:
CSA = πrl
Total Surface Area of a Cone:
TSA = πrl + πr²
TSA = πr(l + r)
Volume of a Right Circular Cone:
V = ⅓πr²h
V = ⅓ × Base Area × Height

5. Volume and Unit Conversions

Volume of a Cylinder:
V = πr²h
Relationship Between Cone and Cylinder:
Volume of Cone = ⅓ × Volume of Cylinder
V(cone) = ⅓πr²h
Volume Conservation When a Sphere Is Melted:
Total volume before melting = Total volume after recasting
Volume Conversion:
1 dm³ = 1 litre
1000 cm³ = 1 litre
1000 litres = 1 m³
Important Value:
π ≈ 3.1416 or 22/7 (when suitable)

CONCEPTS

1. Pythagoras Theorem

  • It applies to right-angled triangles.
  • The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is the longest side.
  • The square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • Pythagorean triplets are three positive integers satisfying a² + b² = c².
  • The theorem can be used to find unknown sides, diagonals and heights.

2. Pyramid

  • A pyramid has one polygonal base and triangular side faces meeting at an apex.
  • The number of triangular side faces equals the number of sides of the base.
  • The perpendicular height is measured from the apex to the base at a right angle.
  • The slant height is measured along a triangular side face of a regular pyramid.
  • The lateral surface area includes the triangular side faces, not the base.
  • The volume of a pyramid is one-third of a prism with the same base area and perpendicular height.

3. Sphere and Hemisphere

  • A sphere is a perfectly round three-dimensional solid.
  • A hemisphere is half of a sphere.
  • The curved surface area of a hemisphere excludes its circular base.
  • The total surface area of a closed hemisphere includes the circular base.
  • A hollow sphere has an empty interior, while a solid sphere contains material throughout.
  • When a solid is melted and recast without loss of material, its volume remains the same.

4. Cone

  • A cone has a circular base and one vertex called the apex.
  • A right circular cone has its apex directly above the centre of its circular base.
  • The perpendicular height and radius form a right triangle with the slant height.
  • The curved surface area covers only the curved side.
  • The total surface area includes the curved surface and circular base.
  • A cone has one-third the volume of a cylinder with the same base radius and height.

5. Units and Measurements

  • Use square units for surface area and cubic units for volume.
  • Convert all measurements to compatible units before calculating.
  • Use the given dimensions and the appropriate formula for each solid.

DEFINITIONS

1. Pythagoras Theorem

Pythagoras Theorem: In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

Hypotenuse: The side opposite the right angle in a right-angled triangle.

Pythagorean Triplet: Three positive integers that satisfy a² + b² = c².

2. Pyramid

Pyramid: A three-dimensional solid with a polygonal base and triangular side faces that meet at one apex.

Base: The polygonal face on which a pyramid stands.

Apex: The point where the triangular side faces of a pyramid meet.

Perpendicular Height: The shortest perpendicular distance from the apex to the plane of the base.

Slant Height: The distance measured along a sloping triangular face of a regular pyramid.

Lateral Surface Area: The total area of all the side faces, excluding the base.

Regular Pyramid: A pyramid with a regular polygonal base and its apex vertically above the centre of the base.

3. Sphere and Hemisphere

Sphere: A three-dimensional solid whose surface points are all at the same distance from its centre.

Hemisphere: One half of a sphere formed by cutting it through its centre.

Radius: The distance from the centre of a sphere or circular base to its boundary.

Diameter: A straight line through the centre joining two points on a circle or sphere; it is twice the radius.

Surface Area: The total area covering the outside surface of a solid.

Volume: The amount of three-dimensional space occupied by a solid.

4. Cone

Cone: A three-dimensional solid with a circular base and a single vertex called the apex.

Right Circular Cone: A cone whose axis is perpendicular to the circular base and passes through its centre.

Circular Base: The flat circular face of a cone.

Axis: The line joining the apex to the centre of the circular base of a right circular cone.

Perpendicular Height: The perpendicular distance from the apex to the plane of the base.

Slant Height: The distance along the sloping surface from the apex to the edge of the circular base.

Curved Surface Area: The area of the curved outer surface of a cone, excluding its base.

Total Surface Area: The sum of the curved surface area and the area of the circular base.

5. General Terms

Mensuration: The branch of mathematics concerned with measuring lengths, areas, surface areas and volumes.

Recasting: Melting a solid and shaping the same material into a new solid.

KEY POINTS

1. Pythagoras Theorem

  • Use Pythagoras theorem only when the triangle is right-angled.
  • Identify the hypotenuse first; it is always opposite the right angle.
  • For an unknown hypotenuse, add the squares of the other sides.
  • For an unknown shorter side, subtract the square of the known shorter side from the square of the hypotenuse.
  • Common Pythagorean triplets include (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13) and (6, 8, 10).
  • The theorem is associated with Pythagoras, a Greek mathematician of the sixth century BC.

2. Pyramid

  • Total surface area = base area + lateral surface area.
  • For a regular pyramid, LSA = ½Pl, where P is the base perimeter and l is the slant height.
  • Volume = ⅓Bh, where B is the base area and h is the perpendicular height.
  • Do not confuse slant height with perpendicular height.

3. Sphere and Hemisphere

  • Sphere surface area = 4πr².
  • Open hemisphere curved surface area = 2πr².
  • Closed hemisphere total surface area = 3πr².
  • Sphere volume = ⁴⁄₃πr³; hemisphere volume = ⅔πr³.
  • For surface area ratios, square the ratio of radii.
  • For melting and recasting questions, equate the total volumes before and after recasting.

4. Cone

  • Curved surface area = πrl.
  • Total surface area = πr(l + r).
  • Volume = ⅓πr²h.
  • Use l² = r² + h² to relate the radius, perpendicular height and slant height.
  • The circumference of the base is 2πr, and the base area is πr².
  • A cone's volume is one-third the volume of a cylinder with the same base radius and height.

5. Units and Accuracy

  • 1 dm³ = 1 litre.
  • 1000 cm³ = 1 litre.
  • 1000 litres = 1 m³.
  • Convert measurements to the same unit before using a formula.
  • Surface area is written in square units, such as cm² or m².
  • Volume is written in cubic units, such as cm³, dm³ or m³.
  • Keep π in the answer until the final step when possible, then round as required.
