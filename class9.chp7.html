1. FORMULAS

Distance on a Coordinate Line

Distance Between Two Points:
d(A, B) = AB = |b - a| = |a - b|
Distance is Symmetric:
d(A, B) = d(B, A)
AB = BA

Distance Formula in a Plane

Main Distance Formula:
For P₁(x₁, y₁) and P₂(x₂, y₂):
d(P₁, P₂) = P₁P₂ = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]
Horizontal Distance:
|x₂ - x₁|
Vertical Distance:
|y₂ - y₁|
Distance Formula from Pythagoras Theorem:
P₁P₂² = P₁P₃² + P₂P₃²
P₁P₂² = (x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²

Collinear Points

Distance Relationship for Three Collinear Points:
AC = AB + BC
d(A, C) = d(A, B) + d(B, C)
(When B lies between A and C)

Right Triangle

Pythagoras Theorem:
Hypotenuse² = Perpendicular² + Base²
Right Triangle Test Using Side Lengths:
c² = a² + b²
where c is the longest side.

Mid-Point Formula

Main Mid-Point Formula:
For endpoints A(x₁, y₁) and B(x₂, y₂):
P = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
Mid-Point Coordinates:
x-coordinate = (x₁ + x₂)/2
y-coordinate = (y₁ + y₂)/2

Diagonals of a Parallelogram

Mid-Points of Diagonals:
Mid-point of AC = ((x₁ + x₃)/2, (y₁ + y₃)/2)
Mid-point of BD = ((x₂ + x₄)/2, (y₂ + y₄)/2)
Condition for Diagonals to Bisect Each Other:
Mid-point of AC = Mid-point of BD

2. CONCEPTS

Cartesian Plane

Coordinate geometry uses ordered pairs (x, y) to represent points in a plane. The x-coordinate represents the horizontal position, while the y-coordinate represents the vertical position.

Distance Between Points on a Line

The distance between two points on a coordinate line is the absolute difference between their coordinates. Absolute value ensures that distance is never negative.

Derivation of the Distance Formula

A right triangle can be formed using two points in the Cartesian plane. Its horizontal side has length |x₂ - x₁| and its vertical side has length |y₂ - y₁|. Applying Pythagoras Theorem gives the distance formula.

Ordered Pairs

An ordered pair is written as (x, y). The order of its coordinates matters. In general, (x, y) is not equal to (y, x), unless x = y.

Collinear Points

Points are collinear when they lie on the same straight line. If one point lies between two other collinear points, the whole distance equals the sum of the two smaller distances.

Non-Collinear Points

Three or more points are non-collinear when they do not all lie on the same straight line. Three distinct non-collinear points form a triangle.

Mid-Point

The mid-point is the point exactly halfway between the endpoints of a line segment. Its x-coordinate is the average of the two x-coordinates, and its y-coordinate is the average of the two y-coordinates.

Congruent Triangles in Mid-Point Derivation

In the geometric construction, congruent triangles help establish the relationships needed to derive the mid-point formula.

Diagonals of a Parallelogram

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. This means they intersect at the mid-point of both diagonals.

Coordinate Geometry in Real Life

Coordinate geometry helps determine positions and distances. For example, a lamp post can be placed at a point that is equally distant from two homes.

3. DEFINITIONS

Coordinate Geometry:
A branch of mathematics that uses coordinates and algebra to study geometrical shapes and relationships.
Cartesian Plane:
A plane formed by two perpendicular number lines called the x-axis and y-axis.
Ordered Pair:
A pair of numbers written as (x, y) that represents the coordinates of a point in a plane.
Distance Between Two Points:
The length of the line segment joining two points. It is always non-negative.
Line Segment:
A part of a line bounded by two endpoints.
Length of a Line Segment:
The distance between the two endpoints of a line segment. The length of AB can be written as AB, |AB| or mAB.
Collinear Points:
A set of points that lie on the same straight line.
Non-Collinear Points:
A set of points that do not all lie on the same straight line.
Mid-Point:
The point that divides a line segment into two equal parts.
Hypotenuse:
The longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle.
Right Triangle:
A triangle that contains one angle of 90°.
Bisect:
To divide a line segment into two equal parts.
Diagonal:
A line segment joining two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon.

4. KEY POINTS

  • Distance between two points can never be negative.
  • Absolute value makes the distance formula independent of the order of subtraction.
  • d(A, B) = d(B, A).
  • The distance formula is derived using Pythagoras Theorem.
  • In an ordered pair, the order of coordinates is important.
  • The coordinates of a point are written as (x, y).
  • Collinear points lie on the same straight line.
  • If B lies between A and C on the same straight line, AC = AB + BC.
  • Non-collinear points do not all lie on one straight line.
  • The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is the longest side of a right triangle.
  • The square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.
  • The mid-point formula gives the coordinates halfway between two endpoints.
  • The mid-point has the average of the x-coordinates and the average of the y-coordinates.
  • The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
  • If both diagonals have the same mid-point, they bisect each other.
  • Coordinate geometry can be used to study shapes, distances and positions in real-life situations.

5. APPLICATION

Finding Distance:
The distance formula is used to calculate the straight-line distance between two locations represented by coordinates.
Finding a Mid-Point:
The mid-point formula can locate a point halfway between two places, such as positioning a lamp post equally far from two homes.
Checking Collinearity:
Distances between points can be compared to determine whether points lie on the same straight line.
Verifying Geometric Shapes:
Distances and diagonals can be used to examine the properties of triangles, squares, rectangles and parallelograms on the coordinate plane.
