FORMULAS & RULES

Basic Algebraic Expressions:
Sum of two numbers = x + y
10 minus a number = 10 − x
Sum of 5 and 4 times a number = 5 + 4x
A number multiplied by 7 = 7x
4 more than the product of 5 and a number = 4 + 5x
Evaluating an Expression:
Substitute the given value of the variable into the expression, then simplify.
Like Terms:
Terms with the same variable part and the same powers are like terms.
Examples: ⅓x, −4x, x
Also: xy² and −¾xy²
Adding Like Terms:
3x + 5x = (3 + 5)x = 8x
Subtracting Like Terms:
9x − 4x = (9 − 4)x = 5x
Simplifying Different Terms:
4xy + 3x + 9xy − 2x
= (4xy + 9xy) + (3x − 2x)
= 13xy + x
Combining Powers:
x³ − y³ − x³ + 2y³
= (x³ − x³) + (−y³ + 2y³)
= 0x³ + y³
= y³
Combining Multiple Like Terms:
5p + 9pq − 2p + 8pq − 6r
= (5p − 2p) + (9pq + 8pq) − 6r
= 3p + 17pq − 6r
Adding Algebraic Expressions:
Group like terms, then add their coefficients.

(2x + 3y − 4z) + (3x − y − 4z)
= (2x + 3x) + (3y − y) + (−4z − 4z)
= 5x + 2y − 8z
Subtracting Algebraic Expressions:
Change the sign of every term in the expression being subtracted, then combine like terms.

(2x + 5y − 3z) − (3x + 4y − 2z)
= 2x + 5y − 3z − 3x − 4y + 2z
= (2x − 3x) + (5y − 4y) + (−3z + 2z)
= −x + y − z
Rules for Like Terms:
ax + bx = (a + b)x
ax − bx = (a − b)x
Vertical Method:
When adding or subtracting expressions vertically, place like terms in the same column.

CONCEPTS

1. Introduction to Algebra: Mathematics is often called the gate and key of sciences. Algebra is an important part of mathematics and helps express problems in a simpler mathematical form.

2. History of Algebra: Muhammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī was a 9th-century Muslim mathematician and astronomer, widely known as the father of algebra. The word algebra is associated with the title of his book, Kitab al-Jabr wa-l-Muqabala.

3. Patterns: A pattern is an arrangement of shapes, numbers or objects formed according to a rule. Patterns occur in nature, art and music.

4. Number Sequence: An ordered list of numbers formed according to a rule. Each number in the sequence is called a term.

5. Rules of Number Patterns: A sequence may be formed by adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing by a number. Its rule helps predict the next terms.

6. Algebra: Algebra uses letters and symbols to represent numbers and perform mathematical operations.

7. Variable: A letter or symbol used to represent an unknown or changeable number. Common variables include a, b, c, x, y and z.

8. Algebraic Expression: A combination of numbers, variables and operation signs, such as 3 + 4x.

9. Coefficient: The numerical factor multiplying a variable. In 3 + 4x, the coefficient of x is 4.

10. Constant: A fixed numerical value. In 3 + 4x, the constant term is 3.

11. Like Terms: Terms that have identical variable parts with the same powers. Their coefficients can be added or subtracted.

12. Unlike Terms: Terms with different variable parts or different powers. They cannot be combined into one term by simple addition or subtraction.

13. Simplification: Combining like terms to write an expression in a simpler form.

14. Evaluating an Expression: Replacing variables with given numerical values and calculating the result.

DEFINITIONS

Algebra: A branch of mathematics that uses letters and symbols to represent numbers and express mathematical relationships.

Pattern: An arrangement of numbers, shapes or objects formed according to a particular rule.

Number Sequence: An ordered list of numbers formed according to a specific rule.

Term: Each individual number or expression in a sequence.

Variable: A letter or symbol used to represent an unknown or changeable number.

Algebraic Expression: A combination of variables, numbers and mathematical operation signs.

Coefficient: The numerical factor of a variable in an algebraic term.

Constant: A fixed numerical value that does not contain a variable.

Like Terms: Terms that have the same variables raised to the same powers.

Unlike Terms: Terms that have different variable parts or different powers.

Simplification: The process of combining like terms and rewriting an expression in a simpler form.

Evaluation: Finding the numerical value of an expression by substituting values for its variables.

KEY POINTS

  • Algebra helps represent mathematical problems using symbols and letters.
  • Muhammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī is widely known as the father of algebra.
  • A pattern follows a rule, while a sequence is an ordered list of numbers.
  • The numbers in a sequence are called terms.
  • Number patterns may use addition, subtraction, multiplication or division.
  • Variables are commonly represented by letters such as x, y and z.
  • In 3 + 4x, 4 is the coefficient of x, x is the variable and 3 is the constant term.
  • Like terms must have the same variable parts and powers.
  • Only like terms can be combined by adding or subtracting their coefficients.
  • Unlike terms remain separate when simplifying an expression.
  • To add expressions, group like terms and combine their coefficients.
  • To subtract an expression, change the sign of every term inside the expression being subtracted.
  • When working vertically, align like terms in the same column.
  • To evaluate an expression, substitute the given values for its variables.
  • Always check negative signs carefully when subtracting algebraic expressions.
