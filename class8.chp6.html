FORMULAS & RULES

Polynomial Addition: Add like terms by combining their coefficients.
Polynomial Subtraction: Change the signs of all terms in the subtracted polynomial, then combine like terms.
Polynomial Multiplication: a(b + c) = ab + ac
Product Law of Exponents: xm × xn = xm+n
Power of a Product: (xy)n = xnyn
Quotient Law of Exponents: xm ÷ xn = xm−n, where x ≠ 0 and m > n.
Quotient of Equal Powers: xn ÷ yn = (x/y)n, where y ≠ 0.
Power Law: (xn)m = xnm
Zero Exponent Law: x0 = 1, where x ≠ 0.
Negative Exponent Law: x−n = 1/xn, where x ≠ 0.
Negative Base with Odd Exponent: (−x)n is negative when n is odd.
Negative Base with Even Exponent: (−x)n is positive when n is even.
Dividend Formula: Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder
Polynomial Division: Dividend ÷ Divisor gives a Quotient and a Remainder.
Square of a Sum: (a + b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2
Square of a Difference: (a − b)2 = a2 − 2ab + b2
Sum and Difference: (a + b)(a − b) = a2 − b2
Cube of a Sum: (a + b)3 = a3 + 3ab(a + b) + b3
Cube of a Difference: (a − b)3 = a3 − 3ab(a − b) − b3
Perfect Square Trinomial: a2 + 2ab + b2 = (a + b)2
Perfect Square Trinomial: a2 − 2ab + b2 = (a − b)2
Difference of Squares: a2 − b2 = (a + b)(a − b)
Factorization Identity: a2 + 2ab + b2 − c2 = (a + b + c)(a + b − c)
Factorization Identity: a2 − 2ab + b2 − c2 = (a − b + c)(a − b − c)
Difference of Fourth Powers: a4 − b4 = (a2 + b2)(a + b)(a − b)

CONCEPTS

Algebraic Expressions: Expressions combine numbers, variables and mathematical operations.
Polynomials: Polynomials are formed from one or more monomial terms.
Types by Number of Terms: Monomial has one term, binomial has two terms, trinomial has three terms, and a polynomial may have several terms.
Types by Degree: Constant polynomials have degree 0, linear degree 1, quadratic degree 2 and cubic degree 3.
Degree of a Monomial: Add the exponents of all its variables.
Degree of a Polynomial: The greatest degree among its non-zero terms.
FOIL Method: A method for multiplying two binomials: First, Outer, Inner and Last.
Laws of Exponents: These laws simplify multiplication, division and powers of expressions.
Zero Exponent: Any non-zero number raised to the power zero equals one.
Negative Exponent: A negative exponent indicates the reciprocal of the corresponding positive power.
Polynomial Division: A polynomial can be divided by a monomial or another polynomial using suitable division methods.
Long Division: Repeatedly divide the leading terms, multiply, subtract and continue until the remainder has lower degree than the divisor.
Perfect Square Trinomial: A trinomial that can be written as the square of a binomial.
Sum and Difference Pattern: The product of two binomials with the same terms but opposite signs gives a difference of squares.
Factorization: Expressing a polynomial as a product of its factors.
Arithmetic Sequence: A sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant.
Geometric Sequence: A sequence in which the ratio between consecutive terms is constant.

DEFINITIONS

Algebraic Expression: A combination of variables, numbers and operation signs.
Monomial: A number, a variable or a product of numbers and variables with non-negative integer exponents.
Polynomial: A monomial or a sum of monomials.
Term: Each part of an algebraic expression separated by addition or subtraction signs.
Binomial: A polynomial consisting of two terms.
Trinomial: A polynomial consisting of three terms.
Degree of a Monomial: The sum of the exponents of its variables.
Degree of a Polynomial: The highest degree of its non-zero terms.
Constant Polynomial: A non-zero polynomial of degree zero.
Linear Polynomial: A polynomial of degree one.
Quadratic Polynomial: A polynomial of degree two.
Cubic Polynomial: A polynomial of degree three.
Dividend: The expression being divided.
Divisor: The expression by which the dividend is divided.
Quotient: The result obtained by dividing the dividend by the divisor.
Remainder: The amount left after division.
Factor: An expression that divides another expression exactly, leaving zero remainder.
Exponent: The number that indicates how many times a base is used as a factor.
Factorization: The process of expressing an algebraic expression as a product of factors.
Identity: An equality that remains true for every permitted value of its variables.

KEY POINTS

Like terms have the same variables raised to the same powers. Only their coefficients are combined.
When subtracting a polynomial, change the sign of every term in the polynomial being subtracted.
In polynomial multiplication, use the distributive property and combine like terms.
A polynomial's degree is determined by its highest-degree non-zero term.
When multiplying powers with the same base, add the exponents.
When dividing powers with the same non-zero base, subtract the exponents.
When raising a power to another power, multiply the exponents.
A power of a product applies to every factor inside the parentheses.
A zero exponent gives one only when the base is non-zero.
A negative exponent can be changed to a positive exponent by taking the reciprocal.
A negative base raised to an odd integer power gives a negative result; an even integer power gives a positive result.
In polynomial division, Dividend = Divisor × Quotient + Remainder.
When the remainder is zero, the divisor is a factor of the dividend.
In long division, arrange polynomial terms in descending order of degree and include missing powers with zero coefficients when necessary.
During subtraction in long division, change the signs of all terms in the product being subtracted.
The remainder must have a lower degree than the divisor, unless the remainder is zero.
Perfect-square identities are useful for expanding and factorizing algebraic expressions.
The difference of two squares can be factorized into the product of a sum and a difference.
For expressions of the form a² ± 2ab + b² − c², first identify the perfect-square trinomial, then use the difference-of-squares identity.
The FOIL method stands for First, Outer, Inner and Last.
An arithmetic sequence has a constant difference; a geometric sequence has a constant ratio.
