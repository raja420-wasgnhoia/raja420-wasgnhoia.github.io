1. FORMULAS

A. Algebraic Identities and Axioms

Square of a Sum:
(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²
Reflexive Property:
a = a
Transitive Property:
If a = b and b = c, then a = c
Addition Property of Inequalities:
If a > b and c > d, then a + c > b + d

B. Similarity of Figures

Similarity Symbol:
A ∼ B means figure A is similar to figure B.
Corresponding Angles:
If △ABC ∼ △DEF, then:
∠A = ∠D
∠B = ∠E
∠C = ∠F
Sum of Angles of a Triangle:
∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180°
Corresponding Sides of Similar Triangles:
AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF
SAS Similarity Condition:
If ∠A = ∠D and AB/DE = AC/DF, then:
△ABC ∼ △DEF

C. Basic Proportionality Theorem

Parallel Line in a Triangle:
In △ABC, if DE ∥ BC, where D lies on AB and E lies on AC, then:
∠ADE = ∠ABC
∠AED = ∠ACB
Basic Proportionality Theorem:
AD/DB = AE/EC
Corresponding Side Ratios:
AD/AB = AE/AC = DE/BC
Related Side Ratio:
AB/BE = AC/CD

D. Areas of Similar Figures

Area of a Triangle:
A = ½ × base × height
Area of a Rectangle:
A = length × width
Area of a Circle:
A = πr²
Area Ratio of Similar Figures:
A₁/A₂ = (a/b)² = k²
Here, a and b are corresponding lengths and k is the scale factor.
Area Ratio of Similar Triangles:
A₁/A₂ = (AB/DE)²
= (BC/EF)²
= (AC/DF)²
Area Ratio of Similar Quadrilaterals:
A₁/A₂ = (AB/EF)²
= (BD/FH)²
The corresponding lengths must be matched correctly.
Triangles with the Same Height:
A₁/A₂ = b₁/b₂

E. Similar Solids

Scale Factor:
k = l₁/l₂
Corresponding Lengths of Similar Cuboids:
a/d = b/e = c/f = k
Volume of a Cuboid:
V = length × width × height
V = lwh
Volume Ratio of Similar Cuboids:
V₁/V₂ = k³
Volume of a Cylinder:
V = πr²h
Similar Cylinders:
r/R = h/H = k
Volume Ratio of Similar Cylinders:
V₁/V₂ = k³
General Volume Ratio of Similar Solids:
V₁/V₂ = k³ = (l₁/l₂)³
Mass Ratio for Similar Solids:
If both solids have the same density:
m₁/m₂ = V₁/V₂ = k³
Surface Area Ratio of Similar Solids:
SA₁/SA₂ = k²
Volume and Surface Area Ratios:
Surface area ratio = k²
Volume ratio = k³

F. Interior and Exterior Angles

Interior Angle Sum of a Triangle:
180°
Interior Angle Sum of a Quadrilateral:
360°
Sum of Interior Angles of a Polygon:
S = (n − 2) × 180°
n = number of sides
Each Interior Angle of a Regular Polygon:
θ = [(n − 2) × 180°]/n
Interior and Adjacent Exterior Angles:
Interior angle + exterior angle = 180°
Sum of Exterior Angles of a Polygon:
360°
Each Exterior Angle of a Regular Polygon:
Exterior angle = 360°/n
Number of Sides from an Exterior Angle:
n = 360°/(each exterior angle)

G. Diagonals and Triangles of Polygons

Number of Diagonals:
D = n(n − 3)/2
Number of Triangles from One Vertex:
T = n − 2

H. Perimeter and Area of Regular Polygons

Perimeter of Any Polygon:
P = sum of all side lengths
Perimeter of a Regular Polygon:
P = n × s
Area of a Regular Polygon:
A = ½ × P × a
Area Using Number and Length of Sides:
A = ½ × n × s × a
Apothem and Regular Polygon:
The apothem is perpendicular to a side and extends from the centre of the regular polygon.

2. CONCEPTS

A. Reasoning and Proof

Inductive Reasoning:
Moves from specific observations to a general conclusion. The conclusion may not always be valid.
Deductive Reasoning:
Moves from a general rule or principle to a specific conclusion.
Mathematical Proof:
A proof uses logical reasoning, axioms, postulates and previously proved theorems to establish a result.
Proof of a Theorem:
A geometrical proof generally includes the statement, figure, given information, what is to be proved, and a logically ordered proof.

B. Similarity

Similar Figures:
Similar figures have the same shape, though their sizes may differ.
Properties of Similar Figures:
Corresponding angles are equal, and corresponding sides are proportional.
Triangle Similarity:
If two corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, their third angles are also equal because the angle sum of each triangle is 180°.
SAS Similarity:
Two triangles are similar if two pairs of corresponding sides are proportional and their included angles are equal.
Basic Proportionality:
A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally and creates a smaller triangle similar to the original triangle.

C. Areas and Solids

Area of Similar Figures:
The ratio of areas of similar figures equals the square of the ratio of corresponding lengths.
Common Height:
If two triangles have the same height, their area ratio equals the ratio of their bases.
Similar Solids:
Similar solids have the same shape and a constant ratio between corresponding lengths.
Length, Area and Volume:
For similar figures, length ratios are k, area ratios are k², and volume ratios are k³.
Mass of Similar Solids:
For solids with equal density, the mass ratio equals the volume ratio.

D. Polygons

Interior Angle Sum:
A polygon can be divided into n − 2 triangles by drawing diagonals from one vertex.
Regular Polygon:
All sides are equal and all interior angles are equal.
Irregular Polygon:
A polygon whose sides and angles are not all equal.
Exterior Angles:
The sum of one exterior angle at each vertex of any polygon, taken in the same direction, is 360°.
Perimeter:
The total length around a polygon, obtained by adding the lengths of all its sides.
Apothem:
The perpendicular distance from the centre of a regular polygon to one of its sides.

E. Locus

Locus:
A locus is the set of all points satisfying a specified condition.
Equidistant from a Fixed Point:
In a plane, the locus is a circle centred at the fixed point. In three-dimensional space, it is a sphere.
Fixed Distance from a Straight Line:
The locus consists of two lines parallel to the given line, one on each side, at the specified perpendicular distance.
Equidistant from Two Parallel Lines:
The locus is the line parallel to both lines and halfway between them.
Equidistant from Two Fixed Points:
The locus is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the two points.
Equidistant from Two Intersecting Lines:
The locus consists of the two angle bisectors of the angles formed by the lines.
Concentric Circles:
Circles with the same centre but different radii are called concentric circles.

3. DEFINITIONS

Demonstrative Geometry:
A branch of geometry in which geometrical statements are proved through logical reasoning using given information, fundamental assumptions and previously proved results.
Reasoning:
A logical process used to establish results and draw conclusions.
Mathematical Statement:
A meaningful sentence that can be classified as true or false.
Open Sentence:
A sentence containing variables whose truth or falsity cannot be determined until the variables are assigned values.
Closed Sentence:
A sentence whose truth or falsity can be determined after the variables have been assigned values.
Proposition:
A mathematical statement that is either true or false. Its parts include the premise, argument and conclusion.
Premise:
An assumption or statement from which reasoning begins.
Argument:
The logical chain of reasoning connecting the premise to the conclusion.
Conclusion:
The result obtained through an argument based on the premises.
Fundamental Assumptions:
Statements accepted as true without proof and used as a basis for further reasoning.
Axiom:
A mathematical statement accepted as true without proof. Axioms commonly express general mathematical properties.
Postulate:
A statement accepted without proof, particularly as a foundation of geometry.
Conjecture:
A statement believed to be true but not yet established by proof.
Proof:
A logical sequence of statements supported by axioms, postulates, definitions and previously proved results.
Theorem:
A mathematical statement established through logical proof.
Polygon:
A closed plane figure formed by a finite number of straight line segments.
Pentagon:
A polygon with 5 sides.
Hexagon:
A polygon with 6 sides.
Heptagon:
A polygon with 7 sides.
Octagon:
A polygon with 8 sides.
Nonagon:
A polygon with 9 sides.
Decagon:
A polygon with 10 sides.
Pentadecagon:
A polygon with 15 sides.
Interior Angle:
An angle formed inside a polygon by two adjacent sides.
Exterior Angle:
An angle formed between a side of a polygon and the extension of an adjacent side.
Scale Factor:
The constant ratio between corresponding lengths of similar figures or solids.
Apothem:
The perpendicular segment from the centre of a regular polygon to one of its sides. Its length is the radius of the inscribed circle.
Locus:
The set of points satisfying a given geometrical condition.
Perpendicular Bisector:
A line that passes through the midpoint of a segment and is perpendicular to it.
Angle Bisector:
A ray or line that divides an angle into two equal angles.
Concentric Circles:
Circles that share the same centre but have different radii.

4. KEY POINTS

1. Reasoning:
Inductive reasoning moves from specific observations to a general conclusion. Deductive reasoning applies general rules to reach a specific conclusion.
2. Proofs:
Every step in a mathematical proof must follow logically from given information, definitions, axioms, postulates or established results.
3. Axioms and Postulates:
Axioms and postulates are accepted without proof. Geometrical postulates form the basis for geometrical reasoning.
4. Fundamental Geometrical Postulates:
• Infinitely many lines can pass through a single point.
• Exactly one line passes through two distinct points.
• If two points of a line lie in a plane, the whole line lies in that plane.
5. Important Mathematical Properties:
• All right angles are equal.
• The whole is greater than its part.
• Equal sides of a triangle have equal opposite angles.
• The product of two negative real numbers is positive.
6. Similar Figures:
Corresponding angles are equal and corresponding side lengths are proportional.
7. Similar Triangles:
Two equal corresponding angles are sufficient to establish triangle similarity.
8. Basic Proportionality Theorem:
A line parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally.
9. Ratios of Similar Figures:
Length ratio = k; area ratio = k²; volume ratio = k³ for similar solids.
10. Regular Polygons:
All sides and all interior angles are equal.
11. Polygon Interior Angles:
The interior angle sum depends on the number of sides: (n − 2) × 180°.
12. Exterior Angles:
The sum of one exterior angle at every vertex is always 360°.
13. Diagonals:
The number of diagonals of an n-sided polygon is n(n − 3)/2.
14. Perimeter:
For a regular polygon, perimeter equals the number of sides multiplied by the length of one side.
15. Area of a Regular Polygon:
Area equals half the product of the perimeter and the apothem.
16. Locus of Equidistant Points:
• From a fixed point: circle in a plane.
• From two fixed points: perpendicular bisector.
• From two parallel lines: the parallel line halfway between them.
• From two intersecting lines: their angle bisectors.

5. APPLICATION

Similarity in Construction:
Similar triangles can be used to determine heights and distances that are difficult to measure directly.
Polygons in Design:
Polygons are used in buildings, structural designs, screens, frames and other everyday objects.
Area and Scale:
Area ratios help compare similar shapes when their corresponding lengths are known.
Volume and Scale:
Volume ratios help compare similar three-dimensional objects.
Locus in Geometry:
Locus rules help identify points satisfying equal-distance conditions and construct perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors and parallel lines.
