FORMULAS & RULES

1. Triangle Constructions

SSS: Three sides are given. Draw the base, draw arcs from its endpoints using the other two side lengths, and join the intersection to both endpoints.
SAS: Two sides and the included angle are given. Draw one side, construct the included angle, mark the second side, and join the remaining endpoints.
ASA: Two angles and the included side are given. Draw the side, construct the angles at its endpoints, and join the intersection of the rays.
HS: The hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle are given. Draw the given side, construct a perpendicular, and locate the third vertex using the given hypotenuse.

2. Square

Square with given side: Draw one side, construct perpendiculars at both endpoints, mark equal side lengths, and join the remaining vertices.
Square with given diagonal: Draw the diagonal, bisect it, construct its perpendicular bisector, and mark the other two vertices at the same distance from the midpoint as the diagonal's half-length.
Square properties: All sides are equal; all angles are 90°; diagonals are equal, perpendicular, and bisect each other.

3. Rectangle

Rectangle with two adjacent sides: Draw one side, erect perpendiculars at its endpoints, mark the other side length, and join the top endpoints.
Rectangle with diagonal and one side: Use the given diagonal as the diameter of a circle. Locate the opposite vertices using the given side length and the circle.
Rectangle properties: All angles are 90°; opposite sides are equal and parallel; diagonals are equal and bisect each other.

4. Parallelogram

Two sides and included angle: Draw the base and construct the given angle. Use compass arcs with the given side lengths to locate the fourth vertex.
Two diagonals and their included angle: Draw one diagonal and bisect it. Draw the second diagonal through the same midpoint at the given angle, marking equal half-lengths on both sides.
Parallelogram properties: Opposite sides are equal and parallel; opposite angles are equal; diagonals bisect each other.

5. Rhombus

Given side and diagonal: Draw the diagonal. With both endpoints as centres and the given side length as radius, draw arcs to locate the other two vertices. Join the vertices.
Given side and base angle: Draw one side, construct the given angle, mark the adjacent side of equal length, and complete the rhombus using compass arcs.
Rhombus properties: All sides are equal; opposite sides are parallel; diagonals bisect each other at right angles.

6. Trapezium

Given base, two sides and height: Draw the base. Construct a parallel line at the given perpendicular distance, mark the required endpoints on it using the side lengths, and join the vertices.
Trapezium property: A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.

7. Kite

Given two side lengths and a diagonal: Draw the diagonal. Use compass arcs with the given side lengths from the diagonal's endpoints to locate the remaining vertices, then join the vertices.
Kite properties: Two pairs of adjacent sides are equal; its diagonals are perpendicular.

8. Angle Bisector

Angle bisector: An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal angles.
If ray OP bisects ∠BOC, then ∠BOP = ∠POC.
Triangle incenter: The three internal angle bisectors of a triangle meet at one point called the incenter.
Square angle bisectors: The angle bisectors of adjacent angles meet at the centre. The centre is equally distant from all four vertices.

9. Diagonals

Number of diagonals in a quadrilateral: 2
Square: Diagonals are equal and perpendicular.
Rectangle: Diagonals are equal but generally not perpendicular.
Rhombus: Diagonals are perpendicular but generally not equal.
Parallelogram: Diagonals bisect each other but are generally neither equal nor perpendicular.

CONCEPTS

Triangle Construction

  • SSS uses three given sides.
  • SAS uses two sides and their included angle.
  • ASA uses two angles and their included side.
  • HS uses the hypotenuse and one side of a right-angled triangle.

Quadrilateral Construction

  • Square: Construct equal sides and right angles.
  • Rectangle: Construct right angles and opposite sides equal.
  • Parallelogram: Use equal opposite sides and compass arcs.
  • Rhombus: Use equal side lengths to locate the vertices.
  • Trapezium: Construct one pair of parallel sides.
  • Kite: Use two pairs of equal adjacent sides.

Angle Bisectors

  • An angle bisector divides an angle into two equal parts.
  • A triangle has three internal angle bisectors.
  • A quadrilateral has four angle bisectors, one at each vertex.
  • The three internal angle bisectors of a triangle meet at its incenter.

Compass and Straightedge

  • A compass transfers lengths and draws arcs.
  • A ruler draws straight lines and joins vertices.
  • Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.
  • A perpendicular bisector divides a line segment into two equal parts at 90°.

DEFINITIONS

Practical Geometry: The branch of geometry concerned with constructing geometrical figures using instruments such as a ruler and compass.

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

SSS: A triangle construction method in which all three sides are given.

SAS: A triangle construction method in which two sides and their included angle are given.

ASA: A triangle construction method in which two angles and their included side are given.

HS: A right-angled triangle construction method using the hypotenuse and one side.

Quadrilateral: A polygon with four sides, four vertices and four angles.

Diagonal: A line segment joining two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon.

Square: A quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles.

Rectangle: A quadrilateral with four right angles and equal opposite sides.

Parallelogram: A quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

Rhombus: A quadrilateral with four equal sides.

Trapezium: A quadrilateral with one pair of opposite sides parallel.

Kite: A quadrilateral with two pairs of equal adjacent sides.

Angle Bisector: A ray or line that divides an angle into two equal angles.

Incenter: The point where the three internal angle bisectors of a triangle meet.

Perpendicular Lines: Lines that intersect at an angle of 90°.

Perpendicular Bisector: A line that divides a segment into two equal parts at right angles.

KEY POINTS

  • Use a ruler and compass carefully to construct geometrical figures.
  • For SSS, SAS and ASA, follow the correct construction conditions.
  • For HS construction, the triangle must be right-angled.
  • A square has four equal sides and four right angles.
  • A rectangle has four right angles and equal diagonals.
  • A parallelogram has opposite sides equal and parallel.
  • A rhombus has four equal sides and perpendicular diagonals.
  • A trapezium has one pair of opposite sides parallel.
  • A kite has two pairs of equal adjacent sides.
  • The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.
  • The diagonals of a square are equal and perpendicular.
  • The diagonals of a rectangle are equal but not generally perpendicular.
  • The diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular but not generally equal.
  • A quadrilateral has two diagonals.
  • Every triangle has three internal angle bisectors.
  • The three internal angle bisectors meet at the incenter.
  • Adjacent angle bisectors of a square meet at its centre.
  • Keep construction lines light and final sides clear.
