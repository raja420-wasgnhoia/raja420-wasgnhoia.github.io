CONCEPTS

Chord Theorems

Theorem 9.1: One and only one circle can pass through three non-collinear points.
Theorem 9.2: A straight line drawn from the centre of a circle to bisect a chord is perpendicular to the chord.
Theorem 9.3: A perpendicular drawn from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord.
Theorem 9.4: Two congruent chords of a circle are equidistant from the centre.
Theorem 9.5: Two chords of a circle equidistant from the centre are congruent.

Arc Theorems

Theorem 9.6: If two arcs of a circle, or of congruent circles, are congruent, their corresponding chords are equal.
Theorem 9.7: If two chords of a circle, or of congruent circles, are equal, their corresponding arcs are congruent.
Theorem 9.8: Equal chords of a circle, or of congruent circles, subtend equal angles at the centre.
Theorem 9.9: If two chords subtend equal angles at the centre of a circle, or corresponding centres of congruent circles, the chords are equal.

Chord Distance Rules

Equal chords of the same circle are equidistant from the centre.
The longer chord is nearer to the centre, while the shorter chord is farther from the centre.
A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects that chord.
The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre of the circle.

Parallel Chords

For parallel chords, perpendiculars drawn from the centre to the chords lie on the same straight line.
If parallel chords lie on opposite sides of the centre, the distance between them is the sum of their distances from the centre.
If parallel chords lie on the same side of the centre, the distance between them is the absolute difference between their distances from the centre.

DEFINITIONS

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are at a fixed distance from a fixed point called the centre.
Circular Region: The union of a circle and its interior.
Chord: A line segment joining two points on a circle.
Arc of a Circle: A part of the circumference of a circle.
Semicircle: Each of the two equal arcs formed when a diameter divides a circle into two parts.
Minor Arc: An arc smaller than a semicircle, with measure less than 180°.
Major Arc: An arc greater than a semicircle, with measure greater than 180°.
Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is at the centre of a circle and whose arms pass through the endpoints of an arc.
Congruent Arcs: Arcs of the same circle, or of congruent circles, that have equal measures.
Congruent Chords: Chords that have equal lengths.
Sector: The region enclosed by two radii and the arc between them.
Segment of a Circle: The region enclosed by a chord and its corresponding arc.
Arc Length: The length measured along an arc of a circle.
Distance of a Chord from the Centre: The perpendicular distance from the centre to the chord.

KEY POINTS

  • One and only one circle passes through three non-collinear points.
  • A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.
  • A line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord.
  • Equal chords of the same circle are equidistant from the centre, and vice versa.
  • The longer chord is closer to the centre.
  • Equal arcs have equal corresponding chords.
  • Equal chords have equal corresponding arcs.
  • Equal chords subtend equal central angles.
  • Equal central angles subtend equal chords.
  • A minor arc is less than 180° and a major arc is greater than 180°.
  • A semicircle subtends a central angle of 180°.
  • The central angles around a point add up to 360°.
  • For a chord of length c at perpendicular distance d from the centre, c = 2√(r² − d²).
  • Arc length depends on the central angle and radius.
  • The perimeter of a segment includes both the arc length and chord length.
  • The perimeter of a semicircle includes the curved arc and the diameter.
