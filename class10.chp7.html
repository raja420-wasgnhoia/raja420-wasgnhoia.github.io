Important Formulas

1. Position Vector

If P(x,y), then r = x i + y j
AB = b − a

2. Vector in Component Form

u = [x,y]
−u = [−x,−y]

3. Magnitude of a Vector

|u| = √(x² + y²)
|u| = √[(x-component)² + (y-component)²]

4. Unit Vector

û = u / |u|
u = |u|û
If u = x i + y j, then û = (x i + y j) / √(x² + y²)

5. Scalar Multiplication

λu = [λx, λy]
|λu| = |λ||u|

6. Translation by a Vector

Translation vector = [a,b] → (x,y) → (x+a,y+b)
Right movement → positive x; Left movement → negative x
Upward movement → positive y; Downward movement → negative y

7. Equal Vectors

u = [x₁,y₁], v = [x₂,y₂] → u = v iff x₁ = x₂ and y₁ = y₂

8. Parallel Vectors

u = λv
x₁/x₂ = y₁/y₂ = λ

9. Addition of Vectors

u + v = (x₁+x₂)i + (y₁+y₂)j
[x₁,y₁] + [x₂,y₂] = [x₁+x₂,y₁+y₂]

10. Subtraction of Vectors

u − v = u + (−v)
[x₁,y₁] − [x₂,y₂] = [x₁−x₂,y₁−y₂]

11. Position Vector Between Two Points

PQ = (x₂−x₁)i + (y₂−y₁)j

12. Rectangular Components

F = Fₓ i + Fᵧ j
Fₓ = |F| cos θ
Fᵧ = |F| sin θ
|F| = √(Fₓ² + Fᵧ²)
θ = tan⁻¹(Fᵧ/Fₓ)

13. Ratio Formula

Internal division: r = (m b + n a) / (m+n)
External division: r = (n a − m b) / (n−m)

14. Midpoint Formula

r = (a+b)/2

Definitions

Scalar

A scalar is a physical quantity that has magnitude only and no direction. Examples include mass, time, speed, density, work, distance, length, area and volume.

Vector

A vector is a physical quantity that has both magnitude and direction. Examples include displacement, momentum, force, velocity and acceleration.

Geometrical Representation of a Vector

A vector is represented geometrically by a directed line segment. Its length represents magnitude and its arrowhead represents direction.

Position Vector

The position vector of a point P is the vector OP whose tail is at the origin and whose head is at P.

Negative Vector

The negative of a vector has the same magnitude but opposite direction.

Zero or Null Vector

A zero vector has zero magnitude and no definite direction. It is represented by 0 or [0,0].

Unit Vector

A unit vector is a vector whose magnitude is 1 and whose direction is the same as the given non-zero vector.

Translation by a Vector

Translation by a vector moves a point or figure by a given vector without changing its shape, size or orientation.

Parallel Vectors

Two non-zero vectors are parallel if one vector is a scalar multiple of the other. If the scalar is positive, they have the same direction. If it is negative, they have opposite directions.

Anti-Parallel Vectors

Parallel vectors having opposite directions are called anti-parallel vectors.

Equal Vectors

Two vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and direction. Their positions do not have to be the same.

Head-to-Tail Rule

To add two vectors, place the tail of the second vector at the head of the first vector. The vector joining the tail of the first vector to the head of the second vector is the resultant.

Triangle Law of Addition

The head-to-tail method of adding two vectors is also called the triangle law of vector addition.

Parallelogram Law of Addition

If two vectors are represented by two adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their resultant is represented by the diagonal drawn from their common tail.

Polygon Law of Addition

The addition of any number of vectors by placing them head-to-tail is called the polygon law of vector addition.

Rectangular Components

The two perpendicular components of a vector along the x-axis and y-axis are called its rectangular components.

Section Formula

The section formula gives the position vector of a point that divides a line segment internally or externally in a given ratio.

Midpoint

A midpoint is a point that divides a line segment into two equal parts.

Vector Laws and Properties

Commutative Law

u + v = v + u

Identity Property

a + 0 = 0 + a = a

Additive Inverse

a + (−a) = (−a) + a = 0

Scalar Multiplication

λ(a+b) = λa + λb
λ(a−b) = λa − λb

Key Points

  • A scalar has magnitude only, while a vector has magnitude and direction.
  • The length of a directed line segment represents the magnitude of a vector.
  • The arrowhead represents the direction of a vector.
  • The initial point of a vector is called its tail and the final point is called its head.
  • The position vector of P(x,y) is r = x i + y j.
  • The negative vector has the same magnitude but opposite direction.
  • The zero vector has magnitude zero and no definite direction.
  • A unit vector has magnitude 1.
  • For any non-zero vector, its unit vector is obtained by dividing the vector by its magnitude.
  • Equal vectors do not need to have the same position.
  • Equal vectors have equal corresponding components.
  • Equal vectors are also parallel.
  • If λ > 0 in u = λv, the vectors have the same direction.
  • If λ < 0 in u = λv, the vectors have opposite directions.
  • Vector addition is commutative.
  • Vector subtraction can be changed into addition by using the negative vector.
  • The x-component represents the horizontal part of a vector.
  • The y-component represents the vertical part of a vector.
  • When the tail of a vector is not at the origin, it is not a position vector.
  • Rectangular components are perpendicular to each other.
  • The horizontal component is found using cosine.
  • The vertical component is found using sine.
  • The internal ratio formula is used when a point lies between the two endpoints.
  • The midpoint formula is a special case of the internal ratio formula.

Applications to Geometry

Parallelogram

AB = DC
AD = BC
If both pairs of opposite sides are equal and parallel, the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

Midpoint of a Line Segment

M = (a+b)/2

Diagonals of a Parallelogram

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

Diagonals of a Rectangle

The diagonals of a rectangle bisect each other.

Diagonals of a Square

The diagonals of a square bisect each other.

Diagonals of a Rhombus

The diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other.

Trapezium

If one pair of opposite sides is parallel, the quadrilateral can be identified as a trapezium, subject to the given vector conditions.
