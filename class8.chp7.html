FORMULAS & RULES

1. Simultaneous Linear Equations

General form of two simultaneous linear equations:
a₁x + b₁y = c₁
a₂x + b₂y = c₂
A solution is an ordered pair (x, y) that satisfies both equations.
Methods of solving simultaneous equations:
1. Substitution method
2. Elimination method
3. Graphical method

2. Special Forms of Linear Equations

General form of a linear equation in two variables:
ax + by = c
The coefficients a and b cannot both be zero.
Equation of a horizontal line:
y = k
Its graph is parallel to the x-axis and passes through (0, k).
Equation of a vertical line:
x = k
Its graph is parallel to the y-axis and passes through (k, 0).

3. Equations in Slope-Intercept Form

General form:
y = mx + c
m = gradient (slope)
c = y-intercept
Equation of the form y = mx passes through the origin (0, 0).
The y-intercept is the point (0, c) on the y-axis.
If two lines have the same gradient but different y-intercepts, they are parallel.
If m > 0, the line rises from left to right.
If m < 0, the line falls from left to right.

4. Graphical Solution of Simultaneous Equations

To solve a system graphically:
1. Draw both equations on the same coordinate axes.
2. Plot points for each equation.
3. Draw the straight lines.
4. Find their point of intersection.
**Intersecting lines:** Exactly one solution.
**Parallel lines:** No solution.
**Coincident lines (the same line):** Infinitely many solutions.

5. Intercepts

x-intercept: The x-coordinate of the point where a line intersects the x-axis.
y-intercept: The y-coordinate of the point where a line intersects the y-axis.

6. Inequality Symbols

< means less than.
> means greater than.
≤ means less than or equal to.
≥ means greater than or equal to.

7. Rules for Solving Inequalities

Adding the same number to both sides does not change the inequality sign.
Subtracting the same number from both sides does not change the inequality sign.
Multiplying both sides by a positive number does not change the inequality sign.
Dividing both sides by a positive number does not change the inequality sign.
Multiplying both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.
Dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.
If a > b and c > 0, then:
ac > bc
a/c > b/c
If a > b and c < 0, then:
ac < bc
a/c < b/c
When solving a multi-step inequality, perform the same valid operation on both sides and reverse the sign whenever multiplying or dividing by a negative number.

CONCEPTS

1. Linear Equations in Two Variables

A linear equation in two variables has infinitely many ordered-pair solutions. Each solution is written as (x, y).

The graph of a linear equation is a straight line. Every solution corresponds to a point on the line, and every point on the line satisfies the equation.

Two distinct points are sufficient to draw a straight line.

2. Gradient and Slope

In the equation y = mx, the value of m represents the gradient or slope of the line.

A positive gradient makes the line rise from left to right, while a negative gradient makes it fall from left to right.

The graph of y = mx passes through the origin (0, 0).

3. The Equation y = mx + c

In y = mx + c, m represents the gradient and c represents the y-intercept.

The line cuts the y-axis at (0, c). If two lines have the same gradient but different y-intercepts, they are parallel.

4. Horizontal and Vertical Lines

A horizontal line has a constant y-coordinate and is parallel to the x-axis.

A vertical line has a constant x-coordinate and is parallel to the y-axis.

5. Solving Simultaneous Equations Graphically

Draw the graphs of both equations on the same coordinate axes. The point where the lines intersect is the common solution.

There are three possible cases:

  • One solution: The lines intersect at exactly one point.
  • No solution: The lines are parallel and never intersect.
  • Infinitely many solutions: Both equations represent the same line.

6. Equations and Inequalities

An equation states that two expressions are equal. An inequality compares two expressions using symbols such as <, >, ≤ or ≥.

An equation may have one, several or infinitely many solutions, depending on the equation. An inequality usually describes a range or set of values that satisfy it.

7. Number Line

Numbers increase from left to right on a number line. A number on the right is greater than a number on the left.

The solution of an inequality can be represented on a number line to show the values that satisfy it.

8. Solving Word Problems

  1. Understand the question.
  2. Identify the known and unknown quantities.
  3. Assign variables to the unknown quantities.
  4. Translate the information into equations or inequalities.
  5. Solve and interpret the result in the context of the question.

DEFINITIONS

1. Simultaneous Linear Equations

A pair of linear equations in two variables is called a system of simultaneous linear equations. A pair (x, y) that satisfies both equations is called a solution of the system.

2. Solution of a Linear Equation

A solution of a linear equation in two variables is an ordered pair (x, y) that makes the equation true.

3. Open Sentence

An open sentence is a mathematical statement containing a variable whose truth depends on the value assigned to that variable.

4. Closed Sentence

A closed sentence is a mathematical statement that has a definite truth value, either true or false.

5. Algebraic Inequality

An algebraic inequality is a mathematical statement that compares two expressions using one of the symbols <, >, ≤ or ≥.

6. Equation

An equation is a mathematical sentence formed by placing the equality symbol (=) between two expressions.

7. Inequality

An inequality is a mathematical sentence formed by placing an inequality symbol (<, >, ≤ or ≥) between two expressions.

8. Gradient (Slope)

The gradient or slope describes the steepness and direction of a straight line.

9. x-intercept

The x-intercept is the x-coordinate of the point where a line intersects the x-axis.

10. y-intercept

The y-intercept is the y-coordinate of the point where a line intersects the y-axis.

11. Horizontal Line

A horizontal line is a straight line parallel to the x-axis. Its equation has the form y = k, where k is a constant.

12. Vertical Line

A vertical line is a straight line parallel to the y-axis. Its equation has the form x = k, where k is a constant.

KEY POINTS

  • A linear equation in two variables has a straight-line graph.
  • Two distinct points are enough to draw a straight line.
  • The graph of y = mx passes through the origin (0, 0).
  • In y = mx + c, m is the gradient and c is the y-intercept.
  • The y-intercept point is (0, c).
  • Lines with the same gradient and different y-intercepts are parallel.
  • A horizontal line has the form y = k and is parallel to the x-axis.
  • A vertical line has the form x = k and is parallel to the y-axis.
  • The graphical solution of simultaneous equations is the point of intersection of their lines.
  • Intersecting lines have one solution, parallel lines have no solution, and coincident lines have infinitely many solutions.
  • The x-intercept is associated with the x-axis, while the y-intercept is associated with the y-axis.
  • Numbers increase from left to right on a number line.
  • An inequality represents values that satisfy a comparison between expressions.
  • Adding or subtracting the same number on both sides does not change the inequality sign.
  • Multiplying or dividing both sides by a positive number does not change the inequality sign.
  • Multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.
  • After solving an inequality, check that the result satisfies the original statement.
  • Word problems can be solved by identifying unknowns and translating the given information into equations or inequalities.
