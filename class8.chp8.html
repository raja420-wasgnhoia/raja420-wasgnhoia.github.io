FORMULAS & RULES

1. Translation

Translation rule:
(x, y) → (x + a, y + b)
Right/left movement changes x; up/down movement changes y.

2. Reflection

Reflection across the x-axis:
(x, y) → (x, −y)
The y-coordinate changes sign.
Reflection across the y-axis:
(x, y) → (−x, y)
The x-coordinate changes sign.

3. Rotation About the Origin

90° clockwise rotation:
(x, y) → (y, −x)
90° anticlockwise rotation:
(x, y) → (−y, x)
180° rotation, clockwise or anticlockwise:
(x, y) → (−x, −y)
Quarter turn = 90°
Half turn = 180°
Full turn = 360°

4. Dilation

Dilation about the origin:
(x, y) → (kx, ky)
k = scale factor, k ≠ 0.
k > 1 → Enlargement
0 < k < 1 → Reduction
Distance of an image point from the centre of dilation:
Image distance = k × original distance
If the centre of dilation is a vertex of the original figure, that centre remains fixed in the image.

5. Rigid and Non-Rigid Transformations

Rigid transformations:
Translation, reflection and rotation
Side lengths, angles, perimeter and area remain unchanged. The image is congruent to the pre-image.
Non-rigid transformation:
Dilation
The size changes, but the shape and angle measures remain the same. The image is similar to the pre-image.

6. Four Transformations: Quick Rules

Translation → Slide
Reflection → Flip
Rotation → Turn
Dilation → Enlarge or reduce

CONCEPTS

1. The Four Transformations

  • Translation: Moves a figure without turning or flipping it.
  • Reflection: Flips a figure across a mirror line.
  • Rotation: Turns a figure around a fixed point.
  • Dilation: Changes the size of a figure while preserving its shape.

2. Pre-image and Image

The pre-image is the original figure before a transformation. The image is the figure after the transformation.

3. Reflection

A reflection creates a mirror image across a line of reflection. The figure flips, but it does not slide or turn.

The line of reflection may be horizontal, vertical or diagonal.

4. Rotation

A rotation turns a figure around a fixed point called the centre of rotation.

A rotation is described by its centre, angle and direction: clockwise or anticlockwise.

5. Dilation

A dilation changes the size of a figure according to its scale factor.

The centre of dilation is the fixed reference point. The scale factor determines whether the image is enlarged or reduced.

The image and pre-image have the same shape and are similar.

6. Rigid Transformations

Translations, reflections and rotations are rigid transformations. They preserve lengths, angles, perimeter and area, so the image and pre-image are congruent.

7. Non-Rigid Transformations

Dilation is a non-rigid transformation. It changes lengths and area but preserves angle measures and shape, so the image and pre-image are similar.

DEFINITIONS

1. Transformation

A transformation changes an initial figure into a final image.

2. Translation

A translation is a transformation that slides a figure along a straight line.

3. Reflection

A reflection is a transformation that flips a figure across a line to produce its mirror image.

4. Line of Reflection

The line across which a figure is reflected is called the line of reflection or mirror line.

5. Rotation

A rotation is a transformation that turns a figure around a fixed point.

6. Centre of Rotation

The fixed point around which a figure rotates is called the centre of rotation.

7. Angle of Rotation

The amount a figure turns, measured in degrees, is called the angle of rotation.

8. Dilation

A dilation is a transformation that produces an image with the same shape but a different size.

9. Centre of Dilation

The fixed point from which the distances to image points are measured is called the centre of dilation.

10. Scale Factor

The number that determines the enlargement or reduction of a figure is called the scale factor.

11. Enlargement

An enlargement is a dilation that produces an image larger than the original figure.

12. Reduction

A reduction is a dilation that produces an image smaller than the original figure.

13. Congruent Figures

Figures that have the same shape and size are called congruent figures.

14. Similar Figures

Figures that have the same shape and equal corresponding angles, but may have different sizes, are called similar figures.

15. Rigid Transformation

A rigid transformation preserves lengths and angles, producing an image congruent to the pre-image.

16. Non-Rigid Transformation

A non-rigid transformation changes lengths or size while preserving the shape and angle measures in a dilation.

KEY POINTS

  • There are four basic transformations: translation, reflection, rotation and dilation.
  • Translation slides a figure; reflection flips it; rotation turns it; dilation changes its size.
  • Reflection across the x-axis changes the sign of y.
  • Reflection across the y-axis changes the sign of x.
  • A 90° clockwise rotation uses (x, y) → (y, −x).
  • A 90° anticlockwise rotation uses (x, y) → (−y, x).
  • A 180° rotation uses (x, y) → (−x, −y).
  • Rigid transformations are translation, reflection and rotation.
  • Rigid transformations preserve lengths, angles, perimeter and area.
  • Rigid transformations produce congruent figures.
  • Dilation is a non-rigid transformation and produces a similar figure.
  • A scale factor greater than 1 produces an enlargement.
  • A positive scale factor between 0 and 1 produces a reduction.
  • In dilation about the origin, multiply both coordinates by the scale factor.
  • The centre of dilation may be the origin or another fixed point.
  • Rotation requires a centre, an angle and a direction.
