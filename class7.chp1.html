FORMULAS & RULES

1. Place Value and Rounding

Place value: Value of a digit = Digit × Place value
Ones = 100, Tens = 101, Hundreds = 102, Thousands = 103
Tenths = 10−1, Hundredths = 10−2, Thousandths = 10−3
Rounding rule: Next digit ≥ 5 → round up; next digit < 5 → keep the target digit unchanged.
To round to n decimal places, inspect the decimal digit immediately after the nth place.
Estimation: Use rounded or convenient values to estimate and check whether an answer is reasonable.
The symbol ≈ means approximately equal to.

2. Integers and Absolute Value

Absolute value: |a| = distance of a from 0 on the number line.
|a| ≥ 0 for every integer or rational number a.
|−a| = |a|

3. Rational Numbers

A rational number is written as a/b, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0.
Natural numbers ⊂ Whole numbers ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational numbers
Rational numbers have decimal expansions that either terminate or repeat.
Equivalent fractions: a/b = (a × k)/(b × k), where b ≠ 0 and k ≠ 0.
Simplest form: A fraction is in simplest form when the HCF of the absolute values of its numerator and denominator is 1.

4. Comparing and Ordering Rational Numbers

For fractions with the same positive denominator: the fraction with the larger numerator is greater.
To compare fractions with different denominators, use a common positive denominator, such as the LCM of the denominators.
On a number line: a number to the right is greater; a number to the left is smaller.
Ascending order: Arrange numbers from smallest to largest.
Descending order: Arrange numbers from largest to smallest.

5. Addition and Subtraction of Rational Numbers

Same denominator: a/b + c/b = (a + c)/b
Same denominator: a/b − c/b = (a − c)/b
Different denominators: Find the LCM, convert to equivalent fractions, then add or subtract the numerators.
Mixed numbers: Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before performing operations when needed.
Commutative property of addition: a + b = b + a
Associative property of addition: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
Additive identity: a + 0 = 0 + a = a
Additive inverse: a + (−a) = (−a) + a = 0

6. Multiplication of Rational Numbers

Product: (a/b) × (c/d) = (a × c)/(b × d), where b ≠ 0 and d ≠ 0.
Commutative property: a × b = b × a
Associative property: (a × b) × c = a × (b × c)
Multiplicative identity: a × 1 = 1 × a = a
Multiplicative property of zero: a × 0 = 0
Multiplicative property of −1: a × (−1) = −a
Distributive property over addition: a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c)
Distributive property over subtraction: a × (b − c) = (a × b) − (a × c)
Multiplicative inverse: For a ≠ 0, a × (1/a) = 1.
For a/b ≠ 0, its reciprocal is b/a. The reciprocal of 0 does not exist.

7. Division of Rational Numbers

Division rule: (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b) × (d/c) = (a × d)/(b × c)
For division, b ≠ 0, c ≠ 0 and d ≠ 0.
Same signs: Quotient is positive.
Different signs: Quotient is negative.
Zero division rule: 0 ÷ a = 0, where a ≠ 0.
Division by zero is undefined.

8. Mean and Order of Operations

Arithmetic mean: Mean = Sum of all values ÷ Number of values
Order of operations: 1. Grouping symbols, 2. Multiplication and division from left to right, 3. Addition and subtraction from left to right.

CONCEPTS

1. Place Value

In the base-10 number system, each digit has a value based on its position. Whole-number places are powers of 10, while decimal places use negative powers of 10.

2. Rounding and Estimation

Rounding gives a number to a required degree of accuracy. Estimation provides an approximate answer and helps check whether a calculation is reasonable.

3. Integers and Absolute Value

Integers include negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers. Absolute value measures distance from zero, so it is never negative.

4. Rational Numbers

Rational numbers include integers, fractions and terminating or repeating decimals. They can be represented as a ratio of two integers with a non-zero denominator.

5. Comparing Rational Numbers

Rational numbers can be compared by converting them to decimals or equivalent fractions with a common positive denominator. Their positions on the number line also show their order.

6. Types of Fractions

Proper fraction: Numerator is smaller than denominator.

Improper fraction: Numerator is greater than or equal to the denominator.

Mixed number: A whole number together with a proper fraction.

7. Addition and Subtraction

Fractions need a common denominator before their numerators can be added or subtracted. The LCM is commonly used to find the least common denominator.

8. Properties of Addition

Changing the order of addends does not change the sum. Changing grouping does not change the sum. Adding zero leaves a number unchanged, and adding its opposite gives zero.

9. Properties of Multiplication

Changing the order or grouping of factors does not change the product. Multiplying by 1 leaves a number unchanged, while multiplying by zero gives zero.

10. Distributive Property

Multiplication distributes over addition and subtraction. A factor outside brackets can be multiplied by each term inside the brackets.

11. Multiplicative Inverse and Division

The multiplicative inverse of a non-zero rational number is its reciprocal. Dividing by a non-zero rational number is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal.

12. Mean

The arithmetic mean represents the average of a set of values. Find it by adding all values and dividing by the number of values.

13. Order of Operations

Follow a consistent order: simplify grouping symbols first, perform multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right.

DEFINITIONS

1. Base-10 Number System

A number system that uses ten digits, from 0 to 9, with place values based on powers of 10.

2. Rounding

The process of expressing a number to a required degree of accuracy by examining the next digit.

3. Estimation

Finding an approximate value of a quantity or calculation, usually to check whether an answer is reasonable.

4. Integers

The set of negative whole numbers, zero and positive whole numbers.

5. Absolute Value

The distance of a number from zero on the number line, without considering its direction.

6. Rational Number

A number that can be expressed as a/b, where a and b are integers and b is not zero.

7. Equivalent Fractions

Fractions that represent the same value, even though their numerators and denominators may differ.

8. Simplest Form

A fraction is in simplest form when its numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1.

9. Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The smallest positive number that is a multiple of each of the given numbers.

10. Commutative Property

A property stating that changing the order of two numbers in addition or multiplication does not change the result.

11. Associative Property

A property stating that changing the grouping of three or more numbers in addition or multiplication does not change the result.

12. Additive Identity

Zero is the additive identity because adding zero to any rational number leaves the number unchanged.

13. Additive Inverses

Two numbers are additive inverses if their sum is zero.

14. Multiplicative Identity

One is the multiplicative identity because multiplying any rational number by one leaves it unchanged.

15. Multiplicative Inverses

Two non-zero rational numbers are multiplicative inverses if their product is one.

16. Distributive Property

A property stating that multiplication over a sum or difference can be performed by multiplying each term separately and then adding or subtracting the products.

17. Numerical Expression

A combination of numbers and mathematical operations that can be evaluated according to the order of operations.

18. Arithmetic Mean

The sum of all values divided by the total number of values.

KEY POINTS

  • The denominator of a rational number can never be zero.
  • Every integer is rational, but not every rational number is an integer.
  • A rational number's decimal expansion either terminates or repeats.
  • Absolute value is always zero or positive.
  • On a number line, numbers increase from left to right.
  • Ascending order means smallest to largest; descending order means largest to smallest.
  • Use a common denominator to add or subtract fractions.
  • The LCM helps find the least common denominator.
  • Addition and multiplication are commutative and associative for rational numbers.
  • Subtraction and division are not commutative in general.
  • Zero is the additive identity; one is the multiplicative identity.
  • A number plus its opposite equals zero.
  • A non-zero rational number multiplied by its reciprocal equals one.
  • Zero has no multiplicative inverse, and division by zero is undefined.
  • When multiplying or dividing, same signs give a positive result and different signs give a negative result.
  • Multiplication distributes over both addition and subtraction.
  • To divide fractions, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
  • To find the mean, divide the sum of the values by the number of values.
  • For order of operations, simplify grouping symbols first, then multiplication/division left to right, and finally addition/subtraction left to right.
  • Round numbers by checking the digit immediately after the required place.
  • Estimation helps check whether a calculated answer is reasonable.
