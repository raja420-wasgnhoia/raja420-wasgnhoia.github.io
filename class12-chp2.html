Important Formulas

1. Limits
lim x→a f(x) = L
lim x→a⁻ f(x) = lim x→a⁺ f(x) = L
lim c = c
lim [c f(x)] = c lim f(x)
lim [f(x)+g(x)] = lim f(x) + lim g(x)
lim [f(x)/g(x)] = lim f(x) / lim g(x),  lim g(x) ≠ 0
lim [f(x)]ⁿ = [lim f(x)]ⁿ
lim x→0 (sin x)/x = 1
lim x→0 (1 − cos x)/x = 0
2. Continuity
f(a) is defined
lim x→a f(x) exists
lim x→a f(x) = f(a)
dy/dx = f′(x)
3. Tangent and Rate of Change
msec = Δy/Δx
mtan = lim Δx→0 Δy/Δx
f′(x) = lim Δx→0 [f(x+Δx) − f(x)]/Δx
v(t) = lim Δt→0 Δs/Δt
4. Basic Differentiation Rules
d/dx [xⁿ] = n xⁿ⁻¹
d/dx [c] = 0
d/dx [c f(x)] = c f′(x)
d/dx [f(x) ± g(x)] = f′(x) ± g′(x)
5. Product and Quotient Rules
d/dx [f(x)g(x)] = f(x)g′(x) + g(x)f′(x)
d/dx [f/g] = [g f′ − f g′]/g²
6. Trigonometric Derivatives
d/dx(sin x) = cos x
d/dx(cos x) = −sin x
d/dx(tan x) = sec²x
d/dx(cot x) = −csc²x
d/dx(sec x) = sec x tan x
d/dx(csc x) = −csc x cot x
7. Inverse Trigonometric Derivatives
d/dx(sin⁻¹x) = 1/√(1−x²)
d/dx(cos⁻¹x) = −1/√(1−x²)
d/dx(tan⁻¹x) = 1/(1+x²)
d/dx(cot⁻¹x) = −1/(1+x²)
d/dx(sec⁻¹x) = 1/[x√(x²−1)]
d/dx(csc⁻¹x) = −1/[x√(x²−1)]
8. Chain Rule
d/dx [g(x)]ⁿ = n[g(x)]ⁿ⁻¹g′(x)
dy/dx = (dy/du)(du/dx)
9. Implicit Differentiation
dy/dx = −y/x
5y² + sin y = x² → dy/dx = 2x/(10y + cos y)
10. Exponential and Logarithmic Derivatives
d/dx(eˣ) = eˣ
d/dx(eᵘ) = eᵘu′
d/dx(ln x) = 1/x
d/dx[ln g(x)] = g′(x)/g(x)
d/dx(aˣ) = aˣ ln a
11. Parametric Differentiation
dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt)
12. Differentials and Approximation
dx = Δx
dy = f′(x)dx
Δy ≈ dy
f(x+Δx) ≈ f(x) + f′(x)dx
13. Higher Derivatives
d²y/dx² = d/dx(dy/dx)
d³y/dx³ = d/dx(d²y/dx²)
dⁿy/dxⁿ = d/dx(dⁿ⁻¹y/dxⁿ⁻¹)
14. Extrema
Absolute Maximum: f(x) ≤ f(c)
Absolute Minimum: f(x) ≥ f(c)
Critical number: f′(c)=0 or f′(c) does not exist
15. Concavity
f″(x) > 0 → Concave upward
f″(x) < 0 → Concave downward
16. Second Derivative Test
f″(c) > 0 → Relative minimum
f″(c) < 0 → Relative maximum
17. Point of Inflection
Point of inflection: point where concavity changes.
18. Related Rates
A=x² → dA/dt = 2x(dx/dt)
V=4/3πr³ → dV/dt = 4πr²(dr/dt)
19. Price Growth Model
P(t)=P₀eʳᵗ
dP/dt = P₀reʳᵗ
20. Economics
Marginal Revenue = R′(x)
Marginal Cost = C′(x)
Break-even: R(x) = C(x)

Key Points

Limit: A limit describes the value that a function approaches as x approaches a particular value.
Left-hand limit: x approaches a from values less than a.
Right-hand limit: x approaches a from values greater than a.
A two-sided limit exists when the left-hand and right-hand limits are equal.
The existence of a limit does not require f(a) to be defined.
Continuity at x=a: f(a) must be defined, the limit must exist, and the limit must equal f(a).
A function can be continuous but not differentiable, such as |x| at x=0.
Differentiability at a point implies continuity at that point.
Derivative: The derivative represents the instantaneous rate of change and the slope of the tangent.
A tangent line may cross the graph and still be a tangent.
Tangent may fail to exist at a discontinuity, corner, cusp, or sharp point.
Critical number: A number in the domain where f′(c)=0 or f′(c) does not exist.
Critical numbers are candidates for relative extrema, but every critical number is not necessarily an extremum.
Absolute extrema are also called global extrema.
A function continuous on a closed interval [a,b] has an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum.
Concave upward: the graph bends upward and f″(x)>0.
Concave downward: the graph bends downward and f″(x)<0.
A point of inflection occurs when the concavity changes from upward to downward or vice versa.
For the second derivative test: f″(c)>0 gives a relative minimum, while f″(c)<0 gives a relative maximum.
Differentials: For a small change in x, Δy is approximately equal to dy.
Parametric differentiation: dy/dx is found by dividing dy/dt by dx/dt.
Higher derivatives are obtained by repeatedly differentiating the previous derivative.
Related rates: Quantities changing with time are related by differentiating their mathematical relationship with respect to time.
In economics, marginal revenue is the derivative of revenue and marginal cost is the derivative of cost.
