FORMULAS & RULES

1. Perimeter

Perimeter = Sum of the lengths of all sides of a closed figure
Perimeter of a square = 4 × side
Perimeter of a rectangle = 2 × (length + width)
Perimeter of a parallelogram = 2 × (a + b), where a and b are adjacent side lengths.
Perimeter of a scalene triangle = a + b + c
Perimeter of an isosceles triangle = 2a + b, where a is an equal side and b is the base.
Perimeter of an equilateral triangle = 3 × side
Perimeter of a trapezium = a + b + c + d
Distance travelled in n rounds = Perimeter × n

2. Area of 2D Shapes

Area of a square = side × side = side²
Area of a rectangle = length × width
Length of a rectangle = Area ÷ width
Width of a rectangle = Area ÷ length
Area of a parallelogram = base × altitude
Altitude of a parallelogram = Area ÷ base
Area of a triangle = ½ × base × altitude
Area of a trapezium = ½ × height × (sum of parallel sides)
Area = ½ × h × (a + b)

3. Paths, Lawns and Cost

Area of a path or lawn = Outer area − Inner area
Cost of covering a surface = Area × Rate per square unit
Cost of fencing or boundary strip = Perimeter × Rate per unit length
If a path of width x surrounds a rectangle of length l and width w, then:
Outer length = l + 2x
Outer width = w + 2x

4. Three-Dimensional Solids

A cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.
A cube has 6 square faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.
A sphere has 1 curved surface, no edges and no vertices.
A closed cylinder has 2 circular plane faces, 1 curved surface, 2 circular edges and no vertices.
A cone has 1 circular plane face, 1 curved surface, 1 circular edge and 1 vertex.
A hemisphere has 1 curved surface, 1 circular plane face and 1 circular edge.

5. Surface Area of Cuboid and Cube

Total surface area of a cuboid = 2(lw + wh + lh)
Surface area of a cube = 6 × side² = 6a²

6. Volume of Cuboid and Cube

Volume of a cuboid = length × width × height = lwh
Volume of a cuboid = Area of base × height
Volume of a cube = side × side × side = a³
Width of a cuboid = Volume ÷ (length × height)
Height of a cuboid = Volume ÷ (length × width)

7. Unit Conversions

1 m = 100 cm
1 m² = 10,000 cm²
1 m³ = 1,000,000 cm³
1 dm³ = 1 litre
1 litre = 1000 ml = 1000 cm³
1 m ≈ 3.28 feet

CONCEPTS

1. Two-Dimensional Figures (2D)
These figures have length and width. They have area but no volume. Examples include squares, rectangles and triangles.
2. Three-Dimensional Figures (3D)
These solids have length, width and height. They occupy space and have volume. Examples include cubes, cuboids, spheres, cylinders and cones. A 3D solid can be represented by a drawing on a flat surface, but the drawing is not the solid itself.
3. Perimeter
Perimeter is the total distance around a closed figure. It is measured in linear units such as mm, cm, m and km.
4. Area
Area measures the surface covered by a 2D shape. It is measured in square units such as cm² and m².
5. Paths Around Shapes
To find the area of a path around a lawn or building, subtract the inner area from the outer area. For a path outside a rectangle, add twice the path width to both the length and width.
6. Parallelogram Properties
Opposite sides are parallel and equal. Its diagonals bisect each other, and each diagonal divides it into two equal-area triangles. A square, rectangle and rhombus are special types of parallelograms.
7. Base and Altitude
The base is the side chosen for measuring area. The altitude is the perpendicular distance to the opposite side or its extension. The altitude is not necessarily the same as a sloping side.
8. Triangle Area
A triangle has half the area of a parallelogram with the same base and altitude. Therefore, its area is half the product of its base and perpendicular altitude.
9. Trapezium
A trapezium is a quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides. Its area is half the product of the height and the sum of the parallel sides.
10. Cuboid
A cuboid is a solid bounded by six rectangular faces. Examples include a brick, matchbox and book.
11. Cube
A cube is a cuboid whose length, width and height are equal. All six faces are squares. A dice is a common example.
12. Sphere and Hemisphere
A sphere is a round solid whose surface points are equally distant from its centre. Examples include a ball and a globe. A hemisphere is half of a sphere.
13. Cylinder
A closed cylinder has two circular plane faces and one curved surface. Examples include oil drums and tins.
14. Cone
A cone has one circular plane face, one curved surface and one vertex. An ice-cream cone is a familiar example.
15. Surface Area
Surface area is the sum of the areas of all the outer faces or surfaces of a solid. The net of a cuboid contains six rectangular faces.
16. Volume
Volume measures the amount of space occupied by a 3D solid. It is measured in cubic units such as cm³, dm³ and m³.
17. Perimeter After Dividing a Shape
When a rectangle is divided into two pieces, the new interior boundary is counted in the perimeter of both pieces. Therefore, the sum of the perimeters of the pieces can be greater than the original perimeter.

DEFINITIONS

Mensuration: The branch of mathematics concerned with measuring lengths, perimeters, areas, surface areas and volumes.
Perimeter: The sum of the lengths of all sides of a closed plane figure.
Area: The measure of the surface covered by a two-dimensional figure.
Altitude: The perpendicular distance between a base and the opposite side or vertex of a shape.
Parallelogram: A quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.
Diagonal: A line segment joining two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon.
Trapezium: A quadrilateral with one pair of parallel sides.
Solid: A three-dimensional figure that has length, width and height.
Cuboid: A solid bounded by six rectangular plane faces.
Cube: A cuboid whose length, width and height are equal.
Sphere: A solid whose surface points are all at the same distance from a fixed point called the centre.
Hemisphere: Half of a sphere, bounded by a curved surface and a circular plane face.
Cylinder: A solid with two parallel circular plane faces joined by a curved surface.
Cone: A solid with a circular base and a curved surface that meets at a single vertex.
Surface Area: The total area of the outer surfaces of a solid.
Volume: The amount of space occupied by a three-dimensional solid.
Dimensions: The length, width and height of a cuboid.
Vertex: A corner point of a solid or plane figure.
Edge: The line segment where two faces of a solid meet.
Face: A flat surface forming part of the boundary of a solid.

KEY POINTS

1. Perimeter uses linear units, while area uses square units and volume uses cubic units.
2. Always use the same units before adding, subtracting or comparing measurements.
3. The area of a path around a shape is found by subtracting the inner area from the outer area.
4. Cost of flooring, painting or covering a surface is usually calculated using area × rate per square unit.
5. Cost of fencing is calculated using perimeter × rate per unit length.
6. In a parallelogram, use the perpendicular altitude, not the sloping side, when calculating area.
7. For a trapezium, add the two parallel sides before multiplying by half the height.
8. A cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. A cube has the same counts.
9. Surface area is measured in square units, for example cm² or m².
10. Volume is measured in cubic units, for example cm³ or m³.
11. The total surface area of a cuboid is the sum of the areas of its six rectangular faces.
12. A cube has six equal square faces, so its surface area is six times the area of one face.
13. One cubic centimetre is the volume of a cube measuring 1 cm on each side.
14. One cubic decimetre equals one litre, and one litre equals 1000 cm³.
15. Write the correct unit with every final answer. For example, area = 25 cm² and volume = 25 cm³.
16. When a problem gives the volume, length and height of a cuboid, its width can be found by dividing the volume by length × height.
17. Remember: cube surface area = 6a², while cube volume = a³. Similar-looking formulas, completely different jobs.
